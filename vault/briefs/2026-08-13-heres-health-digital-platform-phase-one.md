# Here’s Health Digital Platform, Phase One

## Executive position

The correct commercial proposal is a whole-brand Here’s Health app, with the new café as the launch event and highest-frequency use case. That creates substantially more value than a single-location café utility and lets Shopify commerce, launch content and notifications create usefulness before the café opens.

The scope must still be sold and managed in phases. A full Home, Café, Shop, Rewards and Account platform with two commerce systems, payments, live order state, loyalty, unified identity, push, compliance, physical hardware and dual-store release is not automatically a safe six-week solo build. The six-week commitment becomes credible only if Week 1 proves the real Square and Shopify paths and the proposal has a hard cut line.

## Recommended commercial shape

Sell **Here’s Health Digital Platform, Phase One**, not “café app plus Shopify”. Phase One should include:

- Branded iOS and Android application.
- Here’s Health home and launch surface.
- Café click-and-collect through Square.
- Retail commerce through Shopify.
- Essential account, location and preference functions.
- Notifications and order status.
- Analytics, monitoring and operational foundations.
- Multi-location-ready backend.
- App Store and Play Store release preparation through Here’s Health-owned accounts.

Price and change-control separately for:

- Cross-system rewards unification.
- Advanced recommendations and personalisation.
- Rich editorial content tooling.
- Replenishment automation.
- Sauna booking integration if no suitable existing API or booking route exists.
- AI and business-operations automation.

## Product architecture

Primary navigation:

- Home
- Café
- Shop
- Rewards
- Account

Customer-facing experience should feel unified, but commerce remains split:

- Square owns café catalogue, basket, payment, order and fulfilment.
- Shopify owns retail catalogue, basket, checkout and fulfilment.
- No mixed café and retail transaction in version one.
- A shared application layer may own customer preferences, favourites, notifications and a unified presentation of history.

## Flagship customer loop

The quality benchmark is:

1. App opens to the customer’s preferred café.
2. Customer taps “Order your usual”.
3. Customer confirms collection time.
4. Customer pays through the native-feeling supported payment flow.
5. Café staff receive the order in their existing operating environment.
6. Customer receives truthful preparation and collection updates.

This path matters more than feature count. It must be exercised against the real hardware and staff workflow before the product is called production-ready.

## Launch sequencing

### Pre-opening value

- Full Shopify shopping.
- Café Coming Soon surface.
- Opening countdown, menu previews and launch content.
- Exclusive launch offers.
- Notification opt-in.
- Early incentive or reward where operationally supported.

### Café launch value

- Live Square menu and availability.
- Modifiers, dietary information and allergens.
- Collection scheduling and capacity rules.
- Payment, order submission and status.
- Favourites, reorder and history where stable.

### Post-launch expansion

- Coherent cross-channel rewards.
- More advanced personalisation and replenishment.
- Deeper sauna and content experiences.
- Management intelligence and operational AI.

## Six-week critical path

### Week 1, evidence gate

- Inspect Shopify, Square, hardware and staff operations.
- Prove catalogue, basket, checkout, representative café order and fulfilment.
- Confirm app-account readiness and legal ownership.
- Finalise scope, commercial boundaries and cut line.

### Week 2, product and design

- Research benchmark products.
- Map customer and staff journeys.
- Produce three genuinely distinct visual directions.
- Select design language, design system and prototype.
- Validate workflows with Conor and café staff.

### Weeks 3 and 4, core commerce

- Accounts, locations, Square menu/modifiers/basket/scheduling/order.
- Square payment and staff fulfilment.
- Shopify catalogue/search/basket/checkout.

### Week 5, retention and quality

- Notifications, favourites, reorder, history and preferences.
- Analytics, monitoring, accessibility, loading/error states and performance.

### Week 6, production candidate

- Real-device, payment, hardware, staff and checkout testing.
- Security review.
- Store assets, metadata and submission preparation.
- Production candidate deployment.

### Weeks 7 and 8, release buffer

- Store review responses, fixes, training, content, soft launch and readiness.

## Discovery gates

### Square

The following must be evidenced, not assumed:

- Merchant and location structure.
- Device inventory and purpose.
- Order Manager, kitchen printer or KDS route.
- Catalogue, modifiers, availability and loyalty.
- Refund and cancellation path.
- Order-status capability and source of truth.
- Staff receipt and recovery from failures.

### Shopify

- Plan and Storefront API access.
- Customer-account model and order-history capability.
- Product, collection, stock, discount and fulfilment model.
- Checkout Kit fit with current checkout configuration.
- Existing app conflicts or checkout customisation constraints.

### Operations and compliance

- Collection capacity and cut-offs.
- Time-based menus and sold-out handling.
- Allergen data owner and disclaimers.
- Privacy, consent, refund, support and accessibility responsibilities.
- Apple and Google organisation accounts.
- Named acceptance owners for product and operations.

## Commercial controls

- Expanded platform scope requires expanded platform pricing.
- Discounting may recognise the relationship, but the proposal must show full value before any strategic reduction.
- Third-party fees, developer accounts, photography, content production and ongoing infrastructure ownership must be explicit.
- Ongoing support should cover hosting, monitoring, security and API maintenance, OS compatibility, app releases, analytics, technical support and a defined improvement allowance.
- Change requests after the Week 1 scope lock need a written trade: cost, time or deferred feature.

## Principal risks

1. **Square fulfilment mismatch:** APIs may allow order creation while staff hardware or operating setup fails to surface orders correctly.
2. **Scope density:** two commerce systems plus loyalty, identity, notifications and dual-store release can exceed six weeks without a protected cut line.
3. **Client acceptance ambiguity:** the whole-brand platform is currently Sam’s thesis, not a recorded Conor decision.
4. **App-account delay:** Here’s Health organisation enrollment and agreements can block release regardless of code readiness.
5. **Order-status truth:** customer-facing “preparing” and “ready” states require dependable staff or system events.
6. **Identity fragmentation:** Square and Shopify customer identities may not unify cleanly in Phase One.
7. **Allergen liability:** the app must not infer or overstate allergen safety.
8. **Content dependency:** premium design depends on real brand assets, photography, copy and launch information.

## AI and data foundation

The launch app should capture consented, purpose-limited events that support future business intelligence without expanding launch scope. Candidate events include searches, product views, baskets, completed purchases, repeat café orders, modifiers, preferred locations and notification engagement.

Future AI work can evaluate owner briefings, demand and waste forecasting, purchasing, scheduling, support, marketing, recommendations, segmentation, replenishment, cross-selling, anomaly detection and internal knowledge. None belongs on the launch critical path.

## Current recommendation

Proceed immediately with Week 1 discovery and technical proof when access arrives. Pitch the larger platform confidently, but contract Phase One around the evidence-backed critical path. Protect the flagship café loop and Shopify utility. Defer anything that compromises staff operation, payment reliability, real-device quality or the six-week production candidate.
