# Solar installer workflow analysis (Ireland)

## What the biggest providers are actually doing
The largest visible Irish solar providers are no longer just "solar installers".
They are operating as **home energy sales + install + grant + aftercare businesses**.

Common layers across the market:
- lead capture / quote forms
- phone follow-up / video consults
- roof or site survey
- design and proposal
- grant paperwork / BER paperwork
- install scheduling
- roof crew + electrician coordination
- handover
- post-install support and monitoring
- upsell into batteries, EV chargers, heat pumps, retrofit bundles

## Evidence from market leaders
### Activ8 Energies
- 25,000+ installs claimed on island of Ireland
- strong customer-review funnel
- promotes solar + battery + heat pumps + one-stop-shop
- review language highlights sales, customer service, site survey, installation, and in-house team coordination

### PV Generation
- explicitly says it uses **in-house roofers and electricians**
- in-house grants team handles paperwork
- process includes order, survey call, install, BER visit, SEAI portal upload, technical support, feedback

### Solar Home

- offers video consultation + quote funnel
- emphasises grant paperwork and support for homeowners

### Go Solar
- site survey is a named step
- includes design, quotation, grants, installation, support
- pushes batteries / chargers as add-ons

### NRG Panel / Churchfield Home Services
- broad "one stop shop" retrofit positioning
- solar, heat pumps, insulation, grants
- support a home-energy bundle rather than just solar alone

## Where the time and money leak out
### 1. Lead qualification and quoting
- many leads need a human to triage
- customers often request quotes before providing enough data
- slow follow-up loses deals
- poor qualification wastes survey time

### 2. Site surveys / truck rolls
- still a major cost center
- often needed to confirm roof geometry, obstructions, cabling, access
- if the survey is poorly prepared, installers revisit the property
- a single wasted visit can cost roughly **€95-€455** in direct value depending on labor, travel and rework assumptions

### 3. Manual grant / BER paperwork
- SEAI grant handling and BER coordination are often centralised in office teams
- this is repetitive and status-driven work
- delays create customer frustration and cash-flow drag

### 4. Scheduling and coordination
- roof crew, electrician, scaffolding, grant timing, customer availability, and stock all have to line up
- any mismatch creates idle time
- this is especially painful for bigger operators doing high volume

### 5. Install handoff and support
- the install is not the finish line
- customers need monitoring, help, and fault triage
- most companies still treat this as a support cost rather than a productized workflow

### 6. Fragmented asset visibility
- solar, battery, EV charger, heat pump, and HVAC systems are often tracked separately
- no unified customer asset record
- makes service, upsell, and warranty work harder

## Software wedge
The best software opportunity is **not** broad solar design software.
The wedge is a **customer + asset + service workflow layer** that helps installers:
- pre-qualify leads remotely
- reduce avoidable site visits
- generate cleaner quotes
- automate grant / BER document handling
- schedule work more efficiently
- monitor installed assets
- triage service issues
- retain the customer across solar + heat pump + HVAC

## Rough value logic
Illustrative direct value per install:
- **€95 to €455 per install**
- midpoint around **€275**

At a 3,000-install/year operator:
- **€285k to €1.365M** annual value
- midpoint about **€825k**

This is enough to support a meaningful SaaS price if the workflow savings are real.

## Best first product direction
1. remote pre-qualification
2. survey capture and evidence pack
3. grant / BER document workflow
4. install scheduling dashboard
5. post-install monitoring + support layer

## Strategic conclusion
The biggest installers are already moving toward a home-energy platform.
The opportunity is to provide the **operating system** behind that platform,
not to compete as another roof-and-electrician business.
