# Evolv SSEA audit-to-solar handoff gate

## Bounded proposal

Test whether one **client-owned, independently completed SEAI energy audit can become the upstream evidence handoff for one Evolv commercial-solar decision**, without Evolv performing, steering or claiming the audit.

The sequence would be:

1. the business decides whether a broad energy audit is useful and applies in its own name;
2. an SEAI Registered Energy Auditor selected by the business completes the SSEA audit and retains responsibility for its findings;
3. only after the business receives and accepts the report, it may authorise Evolv to read the minimum solar-relevant fields; and
4. Evolv returns a short source-linked handoff that separates the auditor's findings from later installer design, grant, grid and commercial questions.

This is not an energy-audit service, lead-generation partnership, grant application, solar recommendation, installer contract, engineering design or new product lane. The commercial question is narrower: **does a client-controlled independent audit reduce qualification friction and make one real solar decision more credible than beginning with an installer quote alone?**

## Why this is genuinely new but subordinate

[[briefs/2026-09-23-evolv-commercial-solar-grant-grid-readiness-receipt]] begins with a proposed solar system and pre-commit grant/grid evidence. The SSEA path is upstream: the official audit first maps total energy use, significant users, lower-cost actions and renewable options, then assesses solar PV alongside other measures. It can therefore disconfirm a solar-first sale rather than merely document one.

A vault-wide duplicate and rejection check on 7 October found no existing SSEA, 2.8 GWh eligibility or Registered Energy Auditor handoff proposal. Existing solar and reporting notes remain separate. This proposal does not replace [[items/renew-reporting-source-baseline]], activate a new item, or assert that the July-recorded Renew survey opportunity is still live.

The timing signal is specific: SEAI's live SSEA page says the eligibility basis changes on **11 October 2026** from the current SME/non-obligated-entity tests to an annual-consumption threshold. The final applicant, auditor and terms documents are explicitly due to be updated that day, so no test should begin from the pre-change documents.

This note remains behind the paid Here’s Health delivery and the existing Renew requirement for one real owner-side baseline.

## Verified evidence

### Verified current SEAI position — 7 October 2026

- The live SSEA page offers a **€2,000 voucher** towards a professional energy audit and says the applicant must select a participating SEAI Registered Energy Auditor.
- The page says that from **11 October 2026**, public and private enterprises consuming **less than 2.8 GWh (10 TJ) per year from all energy sources, including heat, electricity and transport**, will be eligible, while enterprises above that threshold will not. It says the existing SME/non-SME and public-body floor-area/€35,000 tests will stop applying.
- The page also says all other existing criteria remain and that the Terms and Conditions, Applicant Guidance and Auditor Guidance will be updated on 11 October. The current June 2026 documents are therefore not authority for the changed eligibility details.
- Under the current documents, the applicant must be registered in Ireland, tax compliant, spend at least €10,000 per year on site energy excluding transport, remain within the de minimis ceiling, and use no more than one voucher per applicant organisation. Those conditions must be rechecked against the post-11-October documents rather than carried forward by assumption.
- The current applicant guidance says the recipient, not an auditor or consultant, applies for the voucher; it must contact and nominate a participating auditor first. A voucher is valid for eight months, an audit performed before issue is ineligible, and any cost above €2,000 belongs to the applicant.
- The current SSEA report calculator requires the Registered Energy Auditor to analyse bills and significant energy users, prioritise five to ten energy-efficiency and renewable actions, estimate cost, savings and payback, assess renewable-heat readiness, and **repeat the assessment for solar PV**. The template also states that no-cost and low-cost efficiency measures should be considered before renewable investment.
- SEAI's business energy-efficiency sequence places the professional audit before upgrades and renewables, says the audit can identify solar panels as one option, and directs businesses to registered professionals and current grants before commitment.
- The live NDMG page now publishes doubled capacity bands to an effective 45% rate, a displayed maximum of €325,200, and payment at the lower of the application-summary amount or 45% of invoiced cost. It says existing applications are not uplifted. Its timing text is internally inconsistent: the main note gives a **7 October 2026 to 6 July 2027** window and the banner says applications open at 8am on 7 October, while an adjacent bullet says enhanced grants are for new applications made from 6 October. Preserve that 6/7 October conflict and verify the governing application receipt or final scheme document before any reliance. The current page closes several announcement-stage gaps recorded in [[briefs/2026-10-02-evolv-ndmg-grant-uplift-transition-gate]], but does not make an audit, applicant or site eligible.

These sources establish a newly changing, subsidised, independent assessment path whose standard output includes solar feasibility. They do **not** establish that an Evolv prospect qualifies, that SSEA funding is available for a future application, that an audit will recommend solar, that a solar recommendation is technically sufficient, or that the handoff has commercial value.

## Inference to test

A buyer may trust a later Evolv solar proposal more when the demand baseline, competing efficiency measures and preliminary solar suitability come from a client-selected Registered Energy Auditor rather than from the seller. That could improve qualification and reduce avoidable proposal work.

This is an inference, not observed demand. The same audit may correctly put cheaper efficiency measures ahead of solar, show that solar is unsuitable, or add another step that slows a straightforward project.

## Assumptions to falsify

- After 11 October, the final SSEA documents preserve an eligibility route relevant to at least one existing warm or permissioned commercial rooftop opportunity.
- That business has not already used its one organisation-level voucher and values a broad audit independently of an Evolv sale.
- Annual energy consumption, annual qualifying spend, linked-enterprise aid and applicant status can be established by the business and auditor without Evolv deciding eligibility.
- A participating Registered Energy Auditor with no undisclosed seller conflict is available on acceptable timing and cost.
- The accepted report contains usable solar-PV findings while preserving the priority of no-cost, low-cost and non-solar measures.
- The client will permit Evolv to use only the necessary findings for one proposal decision.
- A short handoff reveals or resolves a material issue that an ordinary survey and quote would otherwise miss.
- The extra step does not displace the live owner-side baseline or current paid delivery priorities.

## Smallest validation test

### Gate 0 — wait for final authority

On or after 11 October, re-read the live SSEA page plus the updated Terms and Conditions, Applicant Guidance, Auditor Guidance and report template/calculator. Record title, visible revision/date, URL and retrieval date. Confirm the final treatment of:

- the less-than-2.8-GWh threshold and the measurement period;
- the €10,000 site-spend test and treatment of transport energy;
- one-voucher-per-organisation and site/MPRN limits;
- tax, de minimis and linked-enterprise declarations;
- voucher value, budget status and validity period;
- who applies, who selects the auditor, and who owns the report; and
- whether the report still includes the solar-PV assessment fields.

If the final documents are absent, contradictory or materially different, stop at a dated discrepancy note. Do not infer eligibility or use the old documents as if they implemented the new threshold.

### Gate 1 — one existing account, no contact from this note

Only if Sam already has a warm or permissioned commercial prospect, record from public or already-approved context why it might fit the broad range: Irish facility, plausible annual energy spend above €10,000, likely total consumption below 2.8 GWh, and no known prior SSEA voucher. Mark every field as unconfirmed. Do not create a prospecting lane or obtain bills, MPRNs, aid records or tax information.

If no existing account plausibly fits, stop and park the proposal.

### Gate 2 — one client-owned audit handoff

After Sam approves the exact account and the business independently chooses to explore an audit:

1. The business selects and contacts a participating Registered Energy Auditor, agrees any top-up cost, makes its own eligibility declarations and submits its own application. Evolv neither chooses on its behalf nor receives the voucher.
2. The auditor conducts the broad audit and supplies the accepted report to the business. Evolv does not draft, influence or sign the report and receives no referral payment in this test.
3. If the business gives explicit purpose-limited permission, read only the accepted report's annual electricity baseline, significant users, solar-PV readiness summary, estimated output or share, priority ranking, cost/payback range, stated supports, uncertainties and recommended first step. Keep bills, MPRN, tax records, personal data and the full report outside Ground Zero.
4. Produce a one-page handoff that preserves the auditor's wording and separates four layers: `AUDITOR FINDING`, `OWNER DECISION`, `QUALIFIED DESIGN/GRID CONFIRMATION REQUIRED`, and `COMMERCIAL ASSUMPTION`.
5. Compare the handoff with the existing survey or quote path. If the audit prioritises cheaper efficiency actions, shows no suitable solar route or lacks enough information, record that outcome without steering it towards a sale.
6. Return one outcome: **discard**, **retain as a manual client-owned handoff**, or **seek approval for one second-site test**. Do not automate, advertise or establish a paid auditor channel from one case.

### Pass gate

Retain the handoff only if:

- the post-11-October authority and the applicant's actual eligibility are confirmed by the applicant and Registered Energy Auditor, not Evolv;
- the audit remains broad, independent and client-owned;
- the handoff changes or clarifies one real decision, including a valid decision not to pursue solar yet;
- preparation after authorised report access takes no more than 30 minutes;
- the buyer says it improved confidence or reduced duplication relative to the ordinary survey/quote path; and
- no bill, MPRN, tax, aid, credential or unnecessary personal record enters Ground Zero.

A voucher, a favourable solar paragraph or a larger NDMG rate is not a pass by itself.

### Kill gate

Discard or park the wedge if the final rules exclude the account, no current prospect exists, the organisation has already used its voucher, audit timing misses the decision, the ordinary audit-to-owner process already provides the same handoff, Evolv's involvement compromises auditor independence, the report does not materially change the decision, a seller-first proposal is faster without reducing decision quality, or the test would delay paid Here’s Health work or the required Renew baseline.

## Downside and safeguards

- **Pre-effective-document risk:** the live page announces a future eligibility change while the June documents retain old rules. Wait for the revised 11 October set.
- **Voucher scarcity:** the current terms allow one voucher per applicant organisation, including connected facilities. Do not encourage a business to consume it for a narrow solar sales purpose when a broader or higher-value facility audit may be better.
- **Auditor independence:** only a participating Registered Energy Auditor can carry out and sign the supported audit. Evolv must not present itself as the auditor, pre-write conclusions or make payment contingent on a solar recommendation.
- **Seller conflict:** a solar provider benefits if solar ranks well. Preserve the full ordering of efficiency and renewable measures and disclose any later commercial role. Any referral fee, exclusivity or formal partnership is outside this test.
- **Eligibility error:** energy consumption, energy spend, de minimis aid, linked-enterprise status, tax compliance and prior-voucher use belong to the applicant and scheme authority. A public company profile cannot prove them.
- **Preliminary-output risk:** the SSEA solar assessment is not structural, electrical, planning, grant, export, connection, yield-guarantee or investment advice. The existing grant/grid and qualified-design gates still apply.
- **Cost and budget risk:** €2,000 may cover only part of the audit; current documents say vouchers are subject to annual budget and expire after eight months. Do not advertise a free or guaranteed audit.
- **Grant-urgency distortion:** the enhanced NDMG page is new-application-only and internally conflicts on a 6 versus 7 October start. That does not justify rushing an audit, application, contract or works; verify the governing dated receipt or scheme document. Existing applications do not receive the uplift.
- **Data minimisation:** audit materials can contain bills, meter identifiers, site plans, operational details and staff contacts. Use an approved client workspace, minimum fields, named access and deletion terms.
- **Priority drift:** this is a one-case no-build test, subordinate to [[items/renew-reporting-source-baseline]], the owner-side baseline in [[project_state/renew]], and the paid Here’s Health delivery priority.

- applying for, reserving, accepting, assigning or redeeming a voucher;
- accessing bills, MPRNs, tax or aid records, site plans, credentials, portals, quotes or audit files;
- conducting, influencing, signing or representing an SEAI energy audit;
- accepting a referral fee, exclusivity, partnership or lead-sharing arrangement;
- recommending, quoting, contracting, applying for, purchasing, installing, connecting or energising solar PV;
- outreach, publication, spending, software development, automation or production mutation.

Sam must approve the exact existing account and evidence scope after conflict, confidentiality and IP review. The business must independently choose the audit, auditor, application and disclosure. The Registered Energy Auditor retains responsibility for the audit. Any contact, cost, report access, solar proposal, referral arrangement, paid pilot or second-site test requires separate approval.
