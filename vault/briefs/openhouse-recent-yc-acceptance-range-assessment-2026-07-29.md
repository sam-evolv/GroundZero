# OpenHouse recent YC acceptance-range assessment

## Verdict

OpenHouse is clearly within the realm of companies YC could accept. It is not an outlandish application and it is not too early.

OpenHouse compares favourably with several W26 and S26 companies on product completeness, real-world deployment and founder-domain depth. It compares unfavourably with the strongest recent physical-world and vertical-AI companies on independent customer evidence, commercial outcomes and team composition.

The right classification is:

- Borderline but plausible interview candidate today
- Below the likely acceptance line on current commercial evidence
- One independent paid pilot away from a materially stronger position
- Two independent paying deployments with measured ROI would put it comfortably inside the recent interview range

## Important evidence caveat

YC directory profiles describe companies now, after acceptance and often after several months of YC progress. They do not disclose the exact evidence present at application time. Public launch posts may also have been written after acceptance.

The profiles therefore establish the range of founders, stages, team sizes and ideas YC accepts. They do not prove that a specific current metric caused acceptance.

YC's own current promotional copy says 40 percent of companies joined with just an idea. Its official guidance also states that zero revenue and solo-founder applications can be accepted. Stage alone does not exclude OpenHouse.

## OpenHouse against the recent range

### Founder-market fit

Assessment: strong and within the accepted range.

Sam's direct role in taking a planned 750-plus-home development from greenfield to more than 200 completed homes is more relevant than the generic founder background shown by many recent profiles.

### Product

Assessment: strong and within the accepted range.

A live multi-tenant product across four developments is beyond idea stage and beyond a demo. Several recent accepted companies were founded in 2026 and publicly describe much narrower initial products.

### Technical-founder signal

Assessment: plausible but weaker than many recent teams.

Sam has demonstrated that he can build and ship with AI coding agents. YC may still worry about technical depth, enterprise reliability and capacity as a solo founder. The shipped product is the answer, not claiming AI replaces a technical cofounder.

### Team

Assessment: accepted but riskier.

Aurorin proves recent solo-founder acceptance. Operon currently shows one active founder. Most close physical-world examples have two to four founders with complementary domain and technical backgrounds.

Solo status does not disqualify OpenHouse. It increases the burden of proof on velocity, judgment and commitment.

### Traction

Assessment: below the stronger recent range.

OpenHouse has real operational deployment but no independent customer, revenue or clean active-usage metric. Strong recent comparables name savings, major design partners, active projects or concrete customer outcomes.

This is OpenHouse's largest disadvantage.

### Market and ambition

Assessment: within the accepted range if framed correctly.

Handover and aftercare is a narrow B2B workflow. The persistent home record creates a plausible expansion into warranty, maintenance and home performance. This is venture-legible, but the economics need external validation.

### Geography

Assessment: not a meaningful blocker.

Recent accepted companies include Alloovium from Brisbane and Zymbly from London. A credible commitment to attend YC and build in the US matters more than being Irish.

## Final judgment

OpenHouse is not hoping for an exception to YC's normal range. It fits a recognisable recent archetype:

> Domain founder builds vertical AI for a neglected physical-world workflow, begins with a narrow B2B wedge, turns fragmented documents and events into structured operational context, and expands from that record.

Recent accepted companies including Operon, FlowManual, SubVysion, Alloovium, Zymbly and Edviro show that YC is actively funding versions of this pattern.

The rejection case is not “this kind of company does not get into YC.” The rejection case is:

> The founder built a large internal product before proving that an independent customer will pay or repeatedly use it.

That is a solvable evidence gap.

## Highest-value move

A signed independent paid pilot is the cleanest way to move OpenHouse from plausible to competitive.

A strong target would contain:

- An unrelated residential developer
- A named live development
- At least 50 committed homes
- At least €10,000 in contracted annual revenue
- First invoice paid
- A defined launch date
- The exact workflow being replaced
- One measurable outcome, such as reduced handover administration, fewer repetitive homeowner queries or faster aftercare resolution

This is not a formal YC threshold. It is the smallest proof package likely to neutralise the central objection.

Two such independent deployments, combined with measured ROI, would put OpenHouse comfortably inside the recent interview comparison range.

The second-best move is a clean, defined cohort showing active use and one measurable developer outcome inside Longview.

More product breadth will not materially improve the comparison with recent accepted companies.
