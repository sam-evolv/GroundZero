>

>
> I also put together a simple live page for anyone who wants to follow his week: **[insert David Howard site link]**. It has the live scoring, key updates and a bit more of David’s story in one place, especially useful for anyone following from work.
>
> Looking forward to getting over there on Thursday. No grand predictions, just a very good week ahead.

### Why this works

- Leads with the genuine surrealness, not a status claim.
- Makes David the protagonist.
- Makes the website useful rather than an advert for Sam’s work.
- Signals competence and proximity without saying “look what I’ve done.”

### Publishing notes

- Use one candid, credible photo: travelling, a wide tournament scene, or David in action where rights/access permit. Avoid a staged “manager” photo.
- Put the website link in the final paragraph, not the opening line.
- Do not mention sponsors or make a direct fundraising ask in this post.
- Post Thursday afternoon/evening once Sam is on site or has a real first image/context.

## Quiet distribution ideas for the week

1. Add a discreet **“Follow David this week”** card on the site containing tee time, timezone, live score, today’s update and share link.
2. Add a short **“Why follow David?”** story module: local roots, route to The Open, current ambition. Keep it factual and human.
3. Add a single **“Partner with David”** link in the footer or About section, leading to a concise sponsorship/contact page. Do not make it a banner or pop-up.
4. Publish one short daily update after each round: score, position, one observed fact, next tee time. This is more useful than lots of social posts.
5. Ask David’s existing supporters to share the live page rather than ask them to share a generic announcement.
6. Prepare a one-page sponsor leave-behind for any real conversation: David story, audience/visibility, current season, sponsor package principles, and one contact method.
