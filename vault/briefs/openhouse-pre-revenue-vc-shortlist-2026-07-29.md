# OpenHouse pre-revenue VC shortlist

## Bottom line

The LinkedIn post is directionally right but commercially loose. All 20 firms invest early, but they do not all have a verified policy of investing before traction, they do not all invest internationally, and several have specialist founder or sector filters that make OpenHouse a poor fit.

OpenHouse should be described as:

> Pre-revenue but not pre-usage. Operationally live across four developments, covering 281 homes and 143 handover agreements; now converting validated internal usage into the first independent paid homebuilder deployment.

The best current targets from the post are:

1. BoxGroup
2. Pear VC
3. Founder Collective
4. ENIAC Ventures
5. Asylum Ventures
6. Weekend Fund
7. Boldstart Ventures

Basecase Capital is an excellent thesis match but difficult to access because it does not take normal pitch meetings.

## What the post actually says

Jourdan Wright lists 20 firms she says are worth researching before revenue or customer traction:

- Boldstart Ventures
- Precursor Ventures
- Basecase Capital
- Asylum Ventures
- 1517 Fund
- Kindred Ventures
- Amplify Partners
- Weekend Fund
- Haystack
- BoxGroup
- Conviction
- ENIAC Ventures
- Bee Partners
- Bloomberg Beta
- Founder Collective
- Long Journey Ventures
- Day One Ventures
- Fifty Years
- Homebrew
- Pear VC

Her own caveats are sensible: most fund startups rather than service businesses, expect venture-scale potential, and require thesis-specific research. The problem is that the headline collapses “early-stage” and “verified no-traction investor” into one category.

## Ranked OpenHouse shortlist

### 1. BoxGroup

Fit: very high

Official facts:

- Invests from pre-seed through Series A
- Says it is comfortable with more vision than traction and raw product
- Invests up to $1 million initially
- Explicitly says it funds companies anywhere
- Explicitly lists solo founders, software, enterprise, ideas and experiments
- Portfolio includes Adaptive in construction financial management and Matterport in spatial property technology
- Public contact: hello@boxgroup.com

Why OpenHouse fits:

- Removes the main stage, geography and solo-founder objections
- Has construction and real-estate precedent
- OpenHouse has more evidence than the raw-product stage BoxGroup describes

Primary objection:

- Longview is captive validation, not an independent customer

Sources:

- https://www.boxgroup.com/about
- https://www.boxgroup.com/portfolio

### 2. Pear VC

Fit: very high

Official facts:

- Explicit pre-seed and seed specialist
- Says no idea is too early
- Published pre-seed range: $250,000 to $2 million
- Invests in vertical SaaS, AI, enterprise and climate technology including the built environment
- Relevant portfolio includes Adaptive, Darwin Homes, Flock Homes and Honey Homes
- PearX application route is public

Why OpenHouse fits:

- Best combined match across stage, vertical SaaS, AI and the built environment
- Pear's founder-led sales support directly addresses OpenHouse's current weakness

Primary objection:

- Pear's operating centre is strongly Bay Area-based, despite no blanket published country restriction

Sources:

- https://pear.vc/how-we-invest/
- https://pear.vc/pearx/
- https://pear.vc/companies/

### 3. Founder Collective

Fit: high

Official facts:

- Explicitly pre-seed and seed
- Invests from idea to IPO
- Sector agnostic and anti-thematic
- Has no rigid ownership target
- Portfolio filter includes international and EU or Canada
- Relevant portfolio includes Higharc, IPX, CompStak and Crexi
- Public contact: contact@foundercollective.com

Why OpenHouse fits:

- Strong built-environment precedent
- Ireland appears viable
- Domain-founder and system-of-record story is legible

Primary objection:

- Must distinguish OpenHouse from home design and building-data companies already in the portfolio

Sources:

- https://foundercollective.com/
- https://foundercollective.com/portfolio/

### 4. ENIAC Ventures

Fit: high thesis, medium accessibility

Official facts:

- Positions itself as partnering with founders from zero to one
- Broad AI and SaaS mandate
- Relevant portfolio includes Briq, Struction and Areal.ai
- No current public founder application or generic fundraising inbox found
- No clear international eligibility policy published

Why OpenHouse fits:

- Strongest direct construction-software precedent in the list
- Clearly understands construction workflow software and AI document processing

Primary objections:

- Geography is unverified
- A warm introduction is likely required

Sources:

- https://www.eniac.vc/
- https://www.eniac.vc/companies

### 5. Asylum Ventures

Fit: high but low probability

Official facts:

- Leads and co-leads pre-seed rounds
- Published cheque range: $1 million to $2 million
- Looks for unusually creative and obsessive founders
- Portfolio includes Homefig and Noso, showing interest in homes and physical-world field work
- No formal country restriction published

Why OpenHouse fits:

- Founder-created product from firsthand homebuilding pain
- Home and field-work precedent
- Strong narrative fit if OpenHouse is presented as a new operating layer, not merely handover SaaS

Primary objection:

- Very small, subjective portfolio selection
- Must distinguish immediately from Homefig

Source:

- https://www.asylum.vc/

### 6. Weekend Fund

Fit: high

Official facts:

- Invests at pre-seed and seed and says earlier is better
- Current homepage shows $200,000 to $500,000 checks
- Explicitly invests around the world
- Invests across B2B and consumer
- Public contact: team@weekend.fund

Why OpenHouse fits:

- Good stage, geography and cheque-size match
- Product-led investor network should understand the live product quickly

Primary objection:

- No strong construction or property precedent found

Sources:

- https://www.weekend.fund/
- https://www.weekend.fund/about

### 7. Boldstart Ventures

Fit: high if technical differentiation is credible

Official facts:

- Explicitly works before incorporation and before the first line of code
- Published initial range: $500,000 to $5 million
- Enterprise-only thesis spanning AI-native applications, infrastructure, security and models
- Describes itself as the first check at inception
- No formal geographic restriction found

Why OpenHouse fits:

- Enterprise AI application at inception stage
- Strong founder-experienced pain and real deployment

Primary objection:

- Boldstart repeatedly emphasises technical founders and unique technical insight
- OpenHouse cannot look like conventional workflow SaaS with an LLM interface

Sources:

- https://boldstart.vc/
- https://boldstart.vc/faq/

## Strong thesis match but difficult route

### Basecase Capital

Fit: very high thesis, low accessibility

Official facts:

- Explicitly invests pre-product, pre-traction and often pre-idea
- Explicitly welcomes solo founders told they need a cofounder
- Leads with $1 million to $5 million
- Portfolio is heavily technical and B2B
- Officially says it does not take pitch meetings

Decision:

- Do not cold-pitch
- Search only for a credible warm path through a portfolio founder, technical operator or mutual investor

Source:

- https://basecase.vc/

## Low-priority or ineligible

### Precursor Ventures

- Explicitly has no traction or metric requirement
- Usually invests up to $500,000
- Has Juniper Square and other vertical-software precedent
- Officially concentrates on North American-headquartered companies

Decision: approach only with a real North American company-building plan or strong warm introduction.

Source: https://precursorvc.com/philosophy

### Bloomberg Beta

- Explicitly invests before incorporation
- Typical first checks: $500,000 to $1 million
- Covers AI, real estate, construction and work software
- Published geography is North America
- Discourages generic cold outreach

Decision: not a current target without a substantive North American plan.

Source: https://github.com/Bloomberg-Beta/Manual

### Conviction

- AI-native specialist and often first investor
- Published check range begins at $1 million
- Portfolio contains frontier AI companies including Mistral, Cognition, Harvey and Sierra

Decision: a reach target only if OpenHouse can prove its AI system is the defensible core rather than a feature.

Source: https://www.conviction.com/

### Amplify Partners

- First investor for technical founders
- Strongly oriented toward engineers, researchers, models, infrastructure and technical products

Decision: low priority unless OpenHouse develops a genuinely deep technical moat.

Source: https://www.amplifypartners.com/

### 1517 Fund

- Explicit idea-stage investor
- Founder filter centres on dropouts, students, uncredentialed builders and renegade scientists

Decision: poor founder-profile fit unless Sam meets one of the stated eligibility categories.

Source: https://www.1517fund.com/

### Fifty Years

- Pre-seed and seed
- Focuses on scientists, engineers and deep technology solving civilization-scale problems
- Its no-deck 5050 programme is free and does not itself guarantee investment

Decision: not a fit for current OpenHouse positioning.

Source: https://fiftyyears.com/

### Homebrew

- 90 percent pre-seed or seed
- Published early checks: $100,000 to $500,000
- Current mandate requires the company to be located in North or South America and initially target those markets

Decision: currently ineligible. Revisit only after a real Americas market move, not merely a Delaware incorporation.

Source: https://www.homebrew.co/

## What to say to investors

Do not lead with:

> We have €0 revenue and 85 homeowner accounts.

Lead with:

> I helped take a planned 750-plus-home development from greenfield to more than 200 completed homes. I then built OpenHouse, the developer-to-homeowner system I wished existed. It is operationally live across four developments, covering 281 homes and 143 handover agreements. We are now converting that validated internal usage into the first independent paid homebuilder deployment.

The company line:

> OpenHouse gives homebuilders one system to hand over each home and manage aftercare from a living, source-backed record of that home.

The non-obvious insight:

> Handover is the only moment when the developer still has the authority, documents and installed-system context required to create an accurate digital record of the home cheaply.

## Recommended action

Do not mass-email 20 firms.

Run six tailored routes:

1. BoxGroup: direct email plus warm-introduction search
2. Pear: PearX application plus warm introduction
3. Founder Collective: targeted email plus warm introduction
4. ENIAC: warm route through construction portfolio or operators
5. Asylum: highly tailored founder narrative
6. Weekend Fund: concise product-led email

Prepare Boldstart only if the technical differentiation section survives hostile review.

In parallel, pursue the independent paid developer deployment. That proof will improve every investor conversation more than another internal usage statistic.
