# OpenHouse post-Cairn strategy reset

## Trigger

Cairn Homes declined as a usable first large-developer route after indicating preference for an in-house / Microsoft-led path, despite acknowledging OpenHouse is more advanced than their current thinking around home and energy monitoring.

This is a material blow because Cairn represented the largest Irish developer target, but it is not a terminal signal for OpenHouse. It is a signal that the first route cannot depend on one dominant developer choosing an external product before the category is legitimised.

## Immediate emotional read

Sam is drained and disappointed after the meeting. The strategic response should reduce pressure, create concrete next actions, and avoid pretending this was not a hit.

Update 2026-06-30: Sam reported that the Cairn meeting felt predetermined. The head of customer care appeared to have decided not to use Walkin House before the meeting, despite acknowledging capability gaps in Cairn's current route. Sam estimates this effectively removes roughly 7–8% of the Irish new-build market from the immediate target list. The psychological hit is real, but Sam also noted he handled it better than previous setbacks, went for a walk, and is becoming more resilient to disappointment.

Key signal from the meeting:
- Cairn's current system lacks even a rudimentary chatbot, so customer questions still route back to customer care.
- Cairn acknowledged they do not have the energy/home intelligence layer.
- The rejection was therefore not a clean product-quality rejection. It was an adoption/incentive/politics rejection.
- This mirrors prior experience with SE Systems: obvious efficiency improvements can still be ignored when the buyer lacks urgency, ownership, or incentive to change.

## Strategic interpretation

The Cairn outcome weakens the direct top-tier Irish developer wedge, but it strengthens the investor narrative:

> The largest developers know the category matters, but may try to internalise it. OpenHouse therefore needs capital, legitimacy, and channel leverage rather than founder-only enterprise sales.

## Ranked path forward

### 1. Funding and legitimacy route: primary

Use NDRC / Dogpatch, Enterprise Ireland, angels, and strategic proptech / climate / energy backers to add credibility and access.

Why:
- founder-led cold access to developers is too slow and emotionally expensive
- investors and accelerators can create legitimacy, intros, and forcing function
- OpenHouse already has a live product, real schemes, and a sharp energy-intelligence thesis
- NDRC has rolling applications and advertises a €100k uncapped SAFE plus mentoring, investor intros, Dogpatch membership, and demo day exposure
- Enterprise Ireland PSSF appears to be the best public funding route now: €50k or €100k convertible loan note, with at least €5k new cash co-investment required per €50k tranche

Immediate targets:
- Enterprise Ireland Pre-Seed Start Fund
- NDRC rolling investment application
- NDRC Pre-Accelerator Autumn backup before 20 July 2026
- HBAN / Irish angel syndicates for co-investment and credibility
- Furthr for PSSF readiness / Foundry / VC navigation
- Resolve Partners / Accelerate Green and ESB Innovation for energy strategic routes
- proptech, climate, energy, and built-environment angels
- strategic advisors who can open UK/Ireland developer or housing association rooms

### 2. UK scale route: parallel

Use Ireland as proof market but start UK mapping immediately.

The first UK wedge should be **new-build warranty / compliance aftercare**, not broad energy yet. Position OpenHouse as an AI homeowner evidence + snagging + NHQB / Ombudsman-ready aftercare layer.

UK likely wedges:
- SME / mid-market housebuilders
- housing associations
- build-to-rent operators
- warranty / aftercare / resident-experience platforms
- NHQB / New Homes Ombudsman evidence workflows
- EPC / retrofit / heat pump support ecosystem

Competitor framing from research:
- Spaciable = portal / home user guide / compliance
- Clixifix = defect workflow
- OpenHouse = AI home model + evidence + homeowner engagement + energy lifecycle

Thesis line:

> Ireland proves the home model. The UK gives OpenHouse the scale market.

### 3. US route: strategic research, not immediate pivot

US is larger and may be more open to ambitious startup products, but product localisation is non-trivial.

Likely US wedges:
- new-home builders
- homeowner warranty / maintenance record
- utility and demand-response partnerships
- IRA / rebate / electrification support
- home energy management around solar, batteries, EVs, heat pumps and tariffs

Do not fully rebuild for the US until a funder, channel partner, or clear pilot path appears.

### 4. Direct-to-consumer route: core venture-scale route, not just backup

DTC should no longer be framed as a panic fallback. It is a core company thesis because it opens the market from developer-controlled new builds to every existing home. Developer channels are still useful for clean source data and proof, but the largest market is retroactively giving homes a brain after handover.

DTC is possible if OpenHouse is honest about the data problem. It should not be sold as an address-only magic digital twin. It should be sold as a **Home Context Builder + Energy / Retrofit / Maintenance Copilot**: public data bootstraps the file, then homeowner uploads and repeated usage turn it into a persistent home memory.

Best starting markets:
- England/Wales first for DTC tests: EPC API/bulk data, Land Registry price paid data, planning.data.gov.uk, and Octopus Energy API make the bootstrap strongest.
- Ireland second: Property Price Register and planning data help, but BER is restricted/personal and consent/trusted-partner access is needed.
- US later: commercially attractive but fragmented and likely dependent on paid data aggregators or specific utilities/cities.

Possible context sources:
- homeowner-uploaded documents, manuals, warranties, BER/EPC, bills and photos
- OCR of handover packs, appliance documentation, surveys, invoices and inspection reports
- consented smart meter / bill data
- UK EPC, Land Registry, planning.data.gov.uk, Octopus Energy where applicable
- Ireland Property Price Register, consented BER, planning datasets, upload-first utility data
- US Green Button / UtilityAPI / paid property datasets where justified
- device manuals and app exports for heat pumps, EV chargers, solar inverters and batteries

DTC product must beat generic ChatGPT by being persistent, structured, localised, cited, reminder-driven, and exportable.

Best DTC MVP:
- Home Health Snapshot
- 90-day action plan
- Retrofit / renovation brief
- bill explainer
- heat pump confidence layer
- photo issue diagnosis and warranty history
- home logbook / digital building passport
- maintenance reminders

Pricing hypothesis:
- free bootstrap tier
- €59–99/year core subscription
- €29–79 one-off project pass
- €149–249/year premium for active retrofit/renovation households

DTC risk:
- without developer data, context quality drops
- context acquisition is still work for consumers
- acquisition cost may be high
- generic ChatGPT can answer many one-off questions
- homeowner subscriptions may be episodic rather than always-on
- liability around retrofit/planning/structural/electrical advice must be carefully bounded

## Revised company thesis

OpenHouse should be pitched as:

> The living home model for modern low-carbon homes. OpenHouse starts at handover, helps homeowners understand and maintain the property, then expands into energy intelligence, diagnostics, warranty evidence, and eventually agentic optimisation.

Cairn saying no does not mean the problem is fake. It means the largest incumbent buyer may be politically and organisationally hard to sell before OpenHouse has outside legitimacy. The new job is not to beg the market to understand the product. The new job is to get enough backing, proof, and channel leverage that the product becomes safer to adopt than to ignore.
