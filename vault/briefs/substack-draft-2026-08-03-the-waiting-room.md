# The waiting room

On Friday night I submitted an application to Y Combinator for OpenHouse.

Monday arrived and, naturally, nothing in my actual life had changed.

It is a bank holiday here. I went to the gym this morning. I did less work than I intended over the weekend, went for a few drinks and woke up feeling a bit rough. Not a crisis. Not a heroic founder story either. Just the reality of trying to build something around a full-time job and a normal life.

There is a strange period after you submit an application like that. For weeks the deadline gives everything a shape. There is always another answer to improve, another claim to check, another piece of the product to show more clearly. Then you press submit and the work becomes waiting.

I don't want to build my life around waiting for somebody to choose me.

Getting into YC would change things very quickly. It is a real goal for me, and I will keep trying until I either get there or build something that makes the question irrelevant. But an accelerator is an accelerant. It cannot be the operating plan.

The harder truth is that I am still in the same position I have been in for a while. I have a good job, and leaving it before my bonus is paid would make very little financial sense. At the same time, I know I want to work for myself. I have built a serious product in the hours around work, but I have not yet turned that effort into the commercial proof I want.

That gap can feel like stagnation. It probably isn't, but it feels like it.

OpenHouse is live across four developments. Homeowners have used it to ask hundreds of property-specific questions. The product is more real than it has ever been. What it does not yet have is an external developer saying yes and paying for it. There is only so much another week of product work can solve when the missing evidence is commercial.

So this week is deliberately small.

I am following up on two warm conversations. One is a potential introduction to a developer. The other is with someone who knows property and technology and had offered to connect with me before OpenHouse existed. I am not sending fifty generic messages or building another feature to manufacture the feeling of progress. I need a real conversation with the right person.

I am also starting this journal privately.

I don't know whether I will publish it. That is almost beside the point for now. I want a record of what building this actually feels like, not a cleaned-up founder story written after the outcome is known. The business, the job, training, weekends that get away from me, the optimism and the days when I feel trapped between patience and urgency.

If this works, I want to remember the waiting room accurately.

And if it takes longer than I hope, I want the record to show that the quiet periods were still part of the work.
