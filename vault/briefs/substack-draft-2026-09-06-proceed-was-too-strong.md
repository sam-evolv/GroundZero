# Proceed was too strong

Last Sunday I wrote that a client had said proceed.

This Sunday, the first job is to correct that sentence.

The record I supplied on 29 August said that Here’s Health and Donworth Studio were proceeding with the app. This week I clarified that the owner is not fully committed. The work happening now is pre-sales proof intended to earn that commitment. It is not an agreed production build, and it is not permission to complete the whole platform for free.

That is not a small wording change.

Last week’s draft tried to be careful. It said that proceeding was not the same as cash in the bank, and it listed the missing scope, budget, payment and acceptance decisions. Even with those caveats, the title gave the week a cleaner commercial result than the current record supports.

The useful thing about keeping a journal backed by evidence is that it can correct me. The less comfortable thing is that it has to.

## The screenshot beat the test report

A real piece of work did ship this week.

A browser version of the Here’s Health app was built from reviewed source, passed hundreds of automated browser checks and received an independent acceptance at that bounded scope. I approved publication of those exact files to a separate preview. It was not the live production app, and it did not connect or prove the real commerce systems.

Then I opened it on my phone.

The Shop screen had uneven product cards and blank image tiles. Whatever the test report said, the result did not meet the visual standard I had asked for.

That screenshot changed the accepted state. The published preview still functions, but it is no longer the quality bar. A replacement is being worked on locally. Its source checks pass and a partial browser slice passes, but the overall gate fails because dozens of product and content image slots remain unresolved. There is no replacement publication and no final review.

This is the kind of distinction that gets lost when technical work is written as momentum. A reviewed deployment existed. A physical screen still looked wrong.

Both facts matter.

The remaining decision is not merely where to find more pictures. Some sample catalogue records are inaccurate or incomplete. I have to decide whether the proof may use verified current merchant products and clearly labelled illustrative café photography, or whether only exact client-supplied assets are acceptable.

That decision is still open. The client has not been contacted about it in the recorded evidence.

## A delivery system became more serious than the delivery

The rest of the week was dominated by Donworth Studio’s machinery.

I accepted a new operating standard for substantial work. I remain the single approval point. One agent builds in an isolated workspace, another independently verifies the exact artifact, and the system is supposed to distinguish implementation, build, installation and real user acceptance instead of compressing them into the word done.

That standard was not just written down. Parts of it were exercised across the Donworth Desktop work.

A corrected unsigned Mac package reached independent acceptance with conditions. A Windows installer reached artifact-custody acceptance. An activation pack was corrected after rejection and became decision-ready. A Finder wrapper was rebuilt to keep the test isolated and fail closed when its contents drifted.

None of that produced an accepted tester journey.

The Windows build has not run on a genuine Windows 10 or 11 machine. The Mac Finder journey still has not completed the account, chat and notification path. One real attempt stopped before launch because Finder had created an ordinary `.DS_Store` file and the wrapper treated it as unapproved drift. Later unattended retries did not start the app at all. They were eventually contained and the task was returned to triage.

The evidence chain became more rigorous. The product still did not reach a person through the intended path.

The same week, the Donworth Studio marketing site went live on its real domains and was independently checked on desktop and mobile. That is a genuine public result. A 71-company prospect list was also verified as a research reserve, then corrected because its original ranking was not strong enough to drive outreach.

No outreach was sent in the recorded evidence.

A live website is not a second client. A reviewed installer is not a tester using the product. A research reserve is not a sales conversation.

## OpenHouse still has the same gate

OpenHouse was quieter.

Its production source advanced at the start of the week with private-document and quote-decision work, and the public portal continued to return successfully. The record still does not bind the latest production deployment to an immutable source commit. It also does not contain an authenticated homeowner exercise or the direct database evidence required to close the tenant-isolation and migration gates.

More importantly, there is no new paid developer validation in Ground Zero for this week.

That remains the result Enterprise Ireland said would change the funding position, according to my report of that meeting. Technical movement can make the product safer and more complete. It does not move that commercial gate by itself.

Aire repeated a familiar pattern. The underlying Hermes project moved rapidly, ending the recorded week 203 commits ahead of the installed version. The accepted Aire candidates remained where they were. Nothing new was integrated, and no authenticated, rendered or physical-device journey was accepted.

The engine moved again. The product did not.

## What I actually reported feeling

I did not leave a fresh mental or physical check-in in Ground Zero this week.

The latest direct emotional report is still from 3 August, when I said I felt stagnant rather than defeated. The latest physical correction is from 4 August, when I said the bank-holiday weekend had been genuine time off and that I was returning to the gym. A separate health log records a completed lower-body session on 6 August, but it does not describe this week.

I cannot honestly say whether correcting the Here’s Health position felt disappointing, clarifying or both. I cannot say whether seeing the preview on my phone made me frustrated or simply decisive. I cannot infer whether the Donworth delivery work felt energising, draining or absurdly detailed.

The record does not tell me how I slept, whether I trained, how I ate, whether I felt physically strong or whether doing this around a full-time job was sustainable.

I have now written several weekly entries around the same absence.

That repetition does not let me fill the blank. It makes the blank more significant.

The system can tell me that an app failed to start because Finder created one file. It can count unresolved image slots and upstream commits. It can separate a published preview from a production app and an accepted package from a real user journey.

It still cannot tell me what the week cost me physically or mentally because I did not record it.

There is no honest hero story to put in that space.

## The honest result

This week contained a correction, a public site, a preview that failed the phone test, a more rigorous delivery method and a large amount of exact technical evidence.

The correction matters most.

Here’s Health is a pre-sales opportunity, not committed production work. The preview is real, but the replacement is incomplete. Donworth Studio now has a live public home, but no recorded outreach or second client. Its Desktop artifact is closer to being trustworthy, but no tester has completed the intended journey. OpenHouse remains technically active and commercially unchanged at its decisive gate. Aire’s engine advanced without advancing the accepted product.

It would be easy to make the lesson that I am becoming more rigorous.

That is partly true. It is also too flattering on its own.

The harder truth is that the distance between convincing work and a commercial result is still open. Better evidence prevents me from lying to myself about that distance. It does not close it.

Last week I wrote that a client said proceed.

This week’s record says I still have to earn the yes.
