# OpenHouse Investor Readiness Operating Plan

Created 2026-06-28 10:52 IST.

## Executive decision

OpenHouse should not be pitched as handover software with AI features. The investable company is:

> **OpenHouse is the intelligence layer installed into homes at construction. It starts as the everyday property assistant, creates a living home model, then expands into diagnostics, home energy intelligence, subscriptions, and eventually agentic energy optimisation.**

The first commercial wedge remains developer-provisioned new builds because developers hold the source-of-truth build data. B2C and retrofit are expansion markets once the model is proven.

## The distilled company thesis

Modern homes are becoming energy systems, but homeowners are not given an operating layer. Developers have the best data about each home, but that context is lost after handover. OpenHouse captures it at construction, turns it into a living home model, and uses it to help homeowners understand, maintain, diagnose, and eventually optimise their home.

### One-line pitch

**OpenHouse makes every new home intelligent from the moment it is built.**

### Investor-grade pitch

**OpenHouse is the construction-stage intelligence layer for modern homes. Developers pay a low per-home fee to create a living home model at handover. Homeowners use it as a practical property assistant from day one, then subscribe for energy intelligence and, later, agentic optimisation of heat pumps, EV charging, solar, batteries and tariffs. Ireland proves the model; the UK scales it.**

## Strategic hierarchy

### 1. Day-one adoption layer: property assistant

Homeowners ask useful questions immediately:
- What size is my living room?
- Where is the stopcock?
- What paint colour is in the kitchen?
- How do I reset the heat pump?
- Can I drill into this wall?
- Is this crack normal?
- Who installed this appliance?
- What warranty covers this?

They can also upload images of cracks, leaks, appliance faults, snagging issues, alarms, defects or finishes. OpenHouse classifies the issue, checks home context, asks follow-up questions, assesses warranty relevance, and routes the right next step.

### 2. Core asset: living home model

The home model is the asset. The assistant is the interface.

The model should know:
- scheme, unit, room and dimension data
- build fabric and specification
- installed systems and devices
- warranties and manuals
- installers and contractors
- maintenance and issue history
- homeowner questions and preferences
- energy usage and diagnostics over time
- developer-relevant portfolio patterns

### 3. Strategic expansion: energy intelligence

OpenHouse translates home performance into Money, Comfort and Risk.

It helps with:
- bills
- heat pump behaviour
- EV charging timing
- solar generation and self-consumption
- batteries
- smart meters
- tariff optimisation
- ventilation and comfort issues
- suspected defects or installation issues

### 4. Long-term upside: agentic optimisation

OpenHouse moves through a trust ladder:
1. Advisory intelligence: explain and recommend.
2. Assisted control: ask approval before action.
3. Agentic manager: optimise heat pump, EV, hot water, solar, battery and ventilation within homeowner-defined rules.

Control only happens with consent, rules and override.

## Business model

### Phase 1: developer provisioning

Starting price: **€99/home**.

Purpose:
- low-friction adoption
- create the home model
- embed OpenHouse at handover
- establish homeowner relationship
- collect proof points

Do not optimise for maximum developer ARPU at the beginning. Optimise for installed base, data capture and proof.

### Phase 2: homeowner subscription

Starting price: **€9.99/month**.

Includes:
- property assistant continuation
- energy insights
- bill interpretation
- seasonal guidance
- maintenance reminders
- diagnostic nudges
- issue history and warranty support

### Phase 3: agentic tier

Indicative price: **€19.99–€29.99/month**.

Requires connected systems and trust maturity. Includes approved or autonomous optimisation of EV charging, heat pump schedules, hot water, solar/battery usage and tariff timing.

### Phase 4: network revenue

Later opportunities:
- developer analytics
- housing association reporting
- utility/grid partnerships
- estate-level optimisation
- anonymised benchmarking
- performance evidence products

## Market sequencing

### Ireland: proof market

Use Ireland to prove:
- developer willingness to pay €99/home
- homeowner activation
- homeowner subscription willingness
- assistant usage frequency
- issue routing value
- energy insight usefulness
- reduction in repeated questions / callbacks

### UK: scale market

Use UK to prove venture-scale potential:
- larger developer base
- housing associations
- retrofit programmes
- smart meter scale
- heat pump and EV growth
- energy price sensitivity
- larger investor story

Key line: **Ireland proves the model. The UK creates the venture-scale market.**

## What must be true for this to become a multi-million euro company

1. Developers accept OpenHouse as a low-friction handover/intelligence layer.
2. The home model can be created cheaply and consistently from developer data.
3. Homeowners use the assistant after handover, not just once.
4. Image upload and issue diagnosis feel genuinely useful.
5. The assistant can answer simple property questions with high confidence.
6. Energy insights can produce believable Money, Comfort and Risk value.
7. A meaningful percentage of homeowners convert to €9.99/month.
8. Agentic control can later justify higher ARPU.
9. The data ownership model feels trustworthy.
10. Ireland pilots create proof strong enough for UK/investor conversations.

## Critical proof metrics

### Developer proof

- Number of homes contracted.
- Developer willingness to pay €99/home.
- Time required to ingest one scheme.
- % required data successfully captured before handover.
- Reduction in repeated homeowner questions.
- Number of issues routed correctly.
- Developer NPS / testimonial.

### Homeowner proof

- Activation rate after handover.
- % homeowners asking at least one useful question.
- Questions per active home per month.
- Image uploads per active home.
- % answers rated useful.
- % issues resolved without manual developer intervention.
- Subscription conversion at €9.99/month.

### Energy proof

- % homeowners connecting bill, tariff, smart meter or device data.
- Number of energy insights per active home per month.
- Estimated savings identified.
- Actual savings validated where possible.
- Heat pump / EV / solar issue categories detected.
- Comfort issues explained through Money, Comfort and Risk.

### Investor proof

- Developer LOIs or paid pilots.
- Live homes onboarded.
- Homeowner engagement data.
- Early subscription signal.
- Evidence of repeatable scheme onboarding.
- Clear data ownership model.
- Product demo that feels premium and inevitable.

## Likely investor objections and answers

### Is this just a chatbot?

No. The assistant is the interface. The asset is the structured home model created from construction-stage data, documents, devices, warranties, issue history and energy signals.

### Why would developers pay?

It improves handover, reduces repeated questions, gives better aftercare, creates a premium buyer experience and gives scheme-level visibility. At €99/home it is a low-friction cost.

### Why would homeowners pay?

Because the assistant remains useful after handover: property questions, issue diagnosis, warranties, maintenance, bills, comfort problems, energy guidance and eventually agentic optimisation.

### Who owns the data?

Homeowners control personal and energy data. Developers supply build/spec/warranty data. OpenHouse owns the software, structured model format, diagnostics and anonymised aggregate benchmarks. Developers get portfolio insight, not household surveillance.

### Why now?

Homes are becoming more complex due to heat pumps, solar, EV charging, batteries, smart meters, tariffs and higher-performance construction. People need an operating layer.

### Why does OpenHouse win?

The developer wedge gives OpenHouse high-context data at the source. Generic energy tools see usage; OpenHouse sees the home that created the usage.

## Immediate founder priorities

### Priority 1: do not let the story sprawl

The pitch is not smart homes, not retrofit consultancy, not a grid platform, not a generic AI assistant. The pitch is:

> construction-stage home intelligence that starts as a property assistant and expands into energy intelligence.

### Priority 2: prove day-one utility

The product must feel useful before the energy layer is complete. If a homeowner cannot ask normal questions about the home and get great answers, the rest is too abstract.

### Priority 3: get paid deployment proof

A paid €99/home developer pilot is more valuable than theoretical TAM. It proves the wedge.

### Priority 4: gather investor-grade metrics

Track everything that shows repeatability, engagement and willingness to pay.

### Priority 5: keep the trust bar high

Data ownership, consent, and no-surveillance positioning must be clear from the start.

## Final strategic view

OpenHouse can become a multi-million euro company if it avoids being trapped as a handover tool and proves a larger category: construction-stage home intelligence.

The near-term job is not to build the full agentic energy system. The near-term job is to prove that developers will pay to create a living home model and that homeowners will use it repeatedly as a practical assistant. Once that is true, energy intelligence and agentic optimisation become credible expansion layers with much higher revenue potential.
