# Here’s Health food-waste margin baseline

## Bounded proposal

Offer Here’s Health an optional, paid **Food-Waste Margin Baseline** only after the launch-critical Square and Shopify work is stable. Start with one café, existing or new, and measure waste against aggregate Square sales and simple kitchen waste records before proposing forecasting, purchasing automation or an AI product.

This is a post-launch analytics and advisory wedge inside the possible wider Here’s Health operational-AI relationship. It is not part of the six-week app critical path, not a new company, and not evidence that Here’s Health currently has a material waste problem.

## Why this clears the research bar

Official Irish data released by the EPA on 8 July 2026 estimates that Ireland generated 708,000 tonnes of food waste in 2024. Restaurants and food services generated about 181,000 tonnes, 26% of the national total, while retail and distribution generated about 85,000 tonnes, 12%.

The EPA's August 2025 Food Service Pathway says that:

- more than 60% of food leaving food-service businesses may be avoidable;
- the purchasing cost of avoidable food waste is approximately €3 per kg before broader handling and disposal costs;
- full-service restaurants in the underlying 2016 to 2018 research averaged 263 grams of food waste per cover;
- a business can begin with information it already has, including waste bills, contractor weight reports and a standard EPA workbook.

The benchmark is directional only. A health-food café, takeaway-heavy location or new-site ramp may behave very differently from a full-service restaurant. Here’s Health must be measured directly before any savings or product claim is made.

The European Commission records that the 2025 Waste Framework Directive amendment introduced national 2030 reduction targets of 10% for processing and manufacturing and 30% per capita jointly across retail and consumption, including restaurants, food services and households, compared with a 2021 to 2023 reference period. These are national targets, not evidence of a direct Here’s Health compliance obligation or a reason to sell fear.

## Why it fits Here’s Health

- The supplied project brief already identifies future demand forecasting, purchasing and waste reduction as possible operational-AI work after Conor returns at the end of August.
- Square should provide item, modifier, timestamp, order and location-level sales signals needed for a demand baseline, subject to access and export verification.
- The EPA provides an Irish measurement method and workbook, so the first proof can be a service rather than custom software.
- One measured café can test whether the opportunity is material before any multi-location or cross-system build.

Square sales alone cannot measure preparation waste, unsold food, spoilage or plate waste. A short manual or weighed waste record remains necessary.

## Smallest validation test

Run a **14-day baseline plus one 14-day intervention check** at one café only after Conor explicitly approves the test and normal operations are sufficiently stable. If an existing café has cleaner data than the new location, use the existing café first.

1. Obtain location-specific, weight-based food-waste data from the waste contractor where available. If bins are shared or only billed by lift, weigh or estimate one dedicated food-waste stream using the EPA pathway and record the method.
2. Use a five-reason kitchen log: preparation, overproduction or unsold, spoilage, customer return or plate, and other. Keep the logging burden below two minutes per service period.
3. Export aggregate Square data for the same period: item quantities, modifiers, timestamps, order count or covers, daypart and net sales. Exclude customer identity and individual behavioural profiles.
4. Use the EPA Food Service Measurement Workbook to calculate kg per order or cover, avoidable kg, waste by reason and an indicative cost. Prefer Here’s Health's actual recipe or purchase cost; use the EPA €3 per kg figure only as a labelled benchmark.
5. Identify one low-risk operational change, such as a smaller preparation batch, changed reorder point, narrower late-day production window or portion adjustment. The café operator must choose it.
6. Repeat measurement for 14 comparable days. Report the measured difference, data limitations, staff burden and whether the annualised savings case survives conservative assumptions.

### Pass criteria

- usable waste and Square records exist for at least 12 of 14 baseline days;
- the data can be joined at location and day or daypart level without personal customer data;
- at least one repeated, operator-recognised waste cause is visible and has a safe intervention;
- the follow-up period shows a measured reduction or a decision-changing negative result;
- the demonstrated or conservatively annualised margin opportunity is at least three times the proposed annual service cost;
- Conor or the named operator confirms that the insight is worth paying for before any dashboard, forecast or automation is built.

If the records are weak, the café is still ramping, no repeated cause appears or staff logging is burdensome, stop and park the wedge.

## Assumptions to falsify

- Here’s Health can obtain location-specific waste weights or create a reliable short measurement window.
- Square exports expose enough item, timestamp, modifier and location detail for aggregate daypart analysis.
- Waste causes can be logged consistently without disrupting staff.
- A meaningful share of measured waste is preventable rather than unavoidable preparation residue.
- At least one low-risk operational change produces a margin effect large enough to justify a paid recurring service.
- The result is reusable across another café or store rather than being a one-off spreadsheet exercise.

## Downside and constraints

- National sector totals do not prove Here’s Health has a large problem.
- The 263 g per-cover benchmark comes from 2016 to 2018 research and may not match a health-food café, takeaway orders or a new opening.
- New-location ramp, promotions, weather and opening-week novelty can make a short before-and-after comparison misleading.
- Waste contractor records may be shared, estimated or too coarse for daypart analysis.
- Square records demand and sales, not preparation, spoilage or plate waste.
- Staff logging may cost more attention than the insight is worth.
- A reduction could come from lower sales or menu changes rather than better operations, so sales-normalised measures and operator review are required.
- This work could distract from the accepted Here’s Health Phase One and Sam's higher-priority ventures if activated too early.
