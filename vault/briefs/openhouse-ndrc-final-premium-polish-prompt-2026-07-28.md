# OpenHouse NDRC final premium polish prompt

Paste this complete prompt into the same Claude Design project.

```text
The OpenHouse NDRC interview deck now has the correct three-slide story and approved copy. Perform one final premium craft pass across the complete artifact.

This is not another strategy pass. Do not add slides, rewrite the narrative, introduce new claims or broaden the product story. Preserve the current factual content and make the deck more visually impressive, cohesive and presentation-ready through better composition, materiality, product framing, typography and architectural detail.

The target is a premium founder presentation that feels deliberately art-directed for OpenHouse, not a generic startup template and not an investor deck compressed into three pages.

## Overall creative direction

Preserve and elevate the established OpenHouse visual identity:

- near-black charcoal;
- warm evidence gold;
- controlled off-white and warm ivory;
- precise architectural line work;
- restrained editorial typography;
- premium residential materiality;
- one dominant visual idea per slide;
- strong negative space;
- calm confidence rather than startup theatre.

Make the slides feel connected as three chapters of one story:

1. The problem was observed from inside residential development.
2. The insight became a real homeowner product.
3. The product becomes a persistent model for the life of the home.

Use one consistent visual grammar throughout:

- Gold represents sourced context, connection and remembered knowledge.
- Charcoal represents the spatial and technical environment.
- Warm ivory represents clear homeowner understanding.
- Fine architectural lines and evidence threads connect construction knowledge to the living home model.

Add visual depth through subtle texture, controlled lighting, line weight, hierarchy and layered architectural detail. Do not add decorative clutter, fake technical diagrams or gratuitous effects.

## Slide 1: founder and origin

Keep all approved copy exactly:

NDRC | PRE-ACCELERATOR

Built from inside the problem.

Sam Donworth
FOUNDER, OPENHOUSE
Residential development, sales and operations

200+
homes completed across Longview

750+
homes planned across the development

The people building a home know everything about it. The homeowner inherits fragments.

Required polish:

- Remove the empty outlined portrait circle beside Sam’s name. Do not replace it with a generated or fake founder image.
- Make Sam’s identity block feel deliberate without needing a photograph. Use typography, a restrained gold rule, architectural coordinates or a subtle site-plan reference.
- Preserve the strong left-right composition.
- Refine the right-side transformation from complete home to fragmented documents so it feels more cinematic and precise.
- Keep the house architectural and physically credible, not holographic.
- Improve the sense that construction knowledge is flowing downward and becoming fragmented at handover.
- Retain the 200+ and 750+ metrics, but ensure they support the story rather than looking like generic startup KPI cards.
- Keep the founder quote prominent and readable.
- Preserve excellent contrast and generous negative space.

The slide should feel grounded, personal and authoritative within five seconds.

## Slide 2: real product proof inside an iPhone

Keep all approved narrative copy exactly:

THE PRODUCT

A home that can explain itself.

STARTED
Digital handover and homeowner support

BUILT
A live product with real scheme data

BECOMING
A living Home Record and operating assistant

The home model is the asset. The assistant is the interface.

The current product visual still feels like a cropped webpage placed beside the slide. Replace it with a premium, complete iPhone product composition.

### Required iPhone treatment

- Show one complete modern iPhone body in a straight-on view.
- The entire phone must be visible, including its outer frame, rounded corners, status bar or Dynamic Island area, safe areas and bottom home indicator.
- Do not use an angled 3D phone, floating perspective mockup, hand holding the device or multiple phones.
- The iPhone should occupy approximately 75 to 85 percent of the slide height and remain entirely inside the safe slide bounds above the footer.
- Use realistic but restrained device material. Dark graphite or black titanium is appropriate.
- Avoid exaggerated reflections, giant shadows, neon edges or glossy advertising effects.

### Required full-screen app content

Show a complete mobile OpenHouse conversation screen inside the iPhone, using the verified interaction already present in the deck:

- Longview homeowner context or header;
- the homeowner’s uploaded crack photograph;
- the complete question: “There is a small crack appearing next to my window sill, is this normal?”;
- a concise, readable OpenHouse answer based on the homeowner’s photo;
- one cautious next-step or monitoring action if it already exists in the verified product content;
- OpenHouse branding and visual language consistent with the real product.

The phone screen must look like a coherent mobile product screen from top to bottom, not a desktop webpage squeezed into an iPhone.

Do not invent a new diagnosis, warranty conclusion, customer fact, metric or unsupported capability. If the existing response is too long, shorten only its visible presentation copy while preserving its meaning and caution. A suitable visible response shape is:

“This looks like a hairline crack in the plaster, which is common as new homes settle. Monitor it for widening or movement near the opening. I can help you record it for review.”

Use that wording only as a presentation-safe condensation of the verified interaction. Do not present it as structural diagnosis.

### Product-proof composition

- Make the phone the dominant visual on the right.
- Preserve the left-side STARTED, BUILT and BECOMING progression.
- Keep enough separation that the phone never touches or overlaps the left-side copy.
- Remove any oversized OpenHouse watermark or decorative logo behind the narrative.
- Use one concise caption beneath or beside the phone, above the footer:

REAL PRODUCT, ANSWERED AGAINST THE HOMEOWNER’S PHOTO

- The caption must not overlap the phone, screen content or footer.
- Ensure the question and answer remain readable when the slide is screen-shared.
- Do not add a second device, floating UI cards or magnified callout bubbles unless absolutely necessary. Prefer making the single phone larger and clearer.

The slide should communicate in five seconds: OpenHouse is a real homeowner product grounded in the actual home, not a generic chatbot.

## Slide 3: long-term vision

Keep all approved copy exactly:

WHERE THIS GOES

Useful at handover. Valuable for the life of the home.

ONE PERSISTENT MODEL

HANDOVER
Understand the home

OWNERSHIP
Maintain, troubleshoot, remember

UPGRADES
Know what to change first

ENERGY
Explain Money, Comfort and Risk

OpenHouse knows your home and helps you run it right.

NDRC can help turn founder-built proof into a focused, repeatable and investable company.

Required polish:

- Preserve the near-black charcoal background. Do not return to a full-slide gold background.
- Keep every lifecycle label clearly visible in gold and every description readable in warm ivory.
- Strengthen the house as the visual protagonist through more precise architectural detail and subtle concentric memory layers.
- Show the lifecycle as one continuous system around the same home, not four disconnected feature bullets.
- Use restrained evidence threads or orbit lines to connect Handover, Ownership, Upgrades and Energy to the persistent model.
- Keep the house and line work visible but subordinate to the headline and lifecycle.
- Slightly increase the contrast of the NDRC sentence so it remains readable when screen-shared, while keeping it subordinate to the main OpenHouse closing line.
- Preserve strong negative space and avoid filling the slide with additional explanation.

This slide should feel ambitious and inevitable, but credible rather than futuristic.

## Typography and composition polish

Across all three slides:

- Preserve the existing OpenHouse type system.
- Refine line breaks so headlines feel intentional and balanced.
- Maintain a clear hierarchy between eyebrow, headline, evidence, supporting copy and footer.
- Keep body text at presentation-readable sizes.
- Use consistent margins, baselines and footer placement.
- Keep all footer elements restrained and aligned.
- Avoid arbitrary uppercase microcopy beyond the established labels.
- Do not use more than one italic statement per slide.
- Avoid crowding the bottom third of any slide.

## Do not introduce

- Extra slides, title pages or appendix pages.
- New claims, customers, users, revenue, savings, commitments or traction.
- Fake founder photography or generated likenesses.
- Generic AI imagery, holographic orbs or glowing neural networks.
- AI-purple gradients, neon glows or glassmorphism.
- Generic three-card layouts.
- Multiple device mockups.
- Angled floating phones.
- Fake dashboards or invented product metrics.
- Clip-art houses.
- Decorative motion that could fail during a live presentation.
- Em dash characters.

## Final artifact checks

Before finishing, inspect the actual presentation rather than only the source canvas:

1. Exactly three slides appear in the presentation controls and export.
2. Slide 1 contains no empty portrait circle.
3. Slide 2 contains one complete, straight-on iPhone entirely inside the slide bounds.
4. The iPhone displays a coherent full mobile screen, not a cropped desktop webpage.
5. The homeowner question and concise answer are readable at screen-share size.
6. The phone, caption and footer do not overlap.
7. Slide 3 retains its charcoal background and all lifecycle labels remain visible.
8. No text, illustration or device crosses the 16:9 safe bounds.
9. OpenHouse casing is correct everywhere.
10. No em dash characters appear.
11. No unsupported claims or invented evidence appear.
12. Export a fresh three-slide PowerPoint and, if available, a clean three-page landscape PDF.
13. Open both exports and inspect every slide for clipping, font substitution, missing vectors, low contrast, blank pages and browser chrome.

Do the simplest thing that makes the existing deck feel significantly more premium. Improve hierarchy, materiality and product framing before adding anything. Build and finish the polished artifact now.
```
