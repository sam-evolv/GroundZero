# OpenHouse A1 show-house evidence capture

## Purpose

Use the A1-rated Longview show house as a permissioned internal case study to test how much trustworthy, home-specific assistance OpenHouse can create from a guided walkthrough, device photographs and available handover evidence.

This is a product-capability and evidence-acquisition test. It is not a measured energy-performance, savings or resident-behaviour study because the show house is not normally occupied.

## Permission and privacy boundary

- Confirm the capture is permitted for internal OpenHouse product testing.
- Do not publish the media or case study without separate organisational approval.
- Do not record people, conversations, visitor information, sales records or personal details.
- Avoid filming alarm codes, Wi-Fi credentials, pairing QR codes, access tokens, MPRNs, account numbers, keys or security arrangements.
- Serial and model numbers may be useful internally, but redact them from anything public unless publication is approved.
- Do not open electrical enclosures, remove covers, climb onto the roof or alter equipment settings.

## Capture method

Record one continuous orientation walkthrough, then take separate focused photographs. Move slowly. Narrate the room, device and what you can see. Hold each label or control display steady for three to five seconds.

Use three views for every material device:

1. **Context:** the full device in its location.
2. **Controls:** the screen, buttons, current mode and visible settings.
3. **Identity:** manufacturer, model and rating plate in focus.

Do not rely on the walkthrough video for small labels. Photograph them separately.

## Walkthrough sequence

### 1. Exterior orientation

- Front, rear and relevant side elevations.
- Approximate solar-panel location, orientation and visible panel count from ground level only.
- Outdoor heat-pump unit in context.
- EV charger in context.
- Any external vents, meters or plant visible safely.
- Narrate which direction is approximately north if known.

### 2. Entrance and home identity

- BER certificate or rating summary if displayed.
- House type, approximate floor area and number of floors if known.
- Handover folder, Home User Guide, floor plan or specification schedule.
- Heating, ventilation, solar, EV and electrical documents available.

### 3. Room-by-room walkthrough

For every room:

- State the room name.
- Slow full-room sweep from the doorway.
- Windows and external doors.
- Heat emitters, underfloor-heating controls or radiators.
- Thermostats or room sensors.
- Supply and extract vents.
- Any unusual controls, switches or equipment.
- Room dimensions only if already known or safely measurable. Do not imply survey accuracy from video.

### 4. Heating and hot water

Capture:

- Outdoor heat-pump unit: context, model plate and visible connections.
- Indoor hydrobox or heat-pump module.
- Hot-water cylinder and rating plate.
- EPH thermostats and controllers in every zone.
- Current room temperature, setpoint and operating mode shown on each display.
- Heating zones and labels.
- Manifold, pumps and visible valves if safely accessible.
- Immersion or hot-water boost controls.
- Any weather-compensation or flow-temperature display.
- Pressure gauge if externally visible.
- Installer or commissioning labels.

Narrate without changing anything:

- What is currently on or off.
- Which thermostat appears to control which area.
- Whether the system uses radiators or underfloor heating.
- Any schedules visible through ordinary display navigation only. Stop if navigation risks changing a setting.

### 5. Ventilation

Capture:

- MVHR, MEV or other ventilation unit if present.
- Manufacturer and model label.
- Main controller and current mode.
- Boost switches.
- Supply and extract vents by room.
- Filter access area without opening it unless normal owner access and permission are clear.
- Commissioning or airflow record if available.

Do not assume trickle vents, extract fans or ceiling valves identify the full ventilation design without supporting documents.

### 6. Solar PV

Capture:

- Panels from ground level.
- Inverter in context.
- Inverter manufacturer and model.
- Current display status and generation reading if visible.
- Isolation switches and labels without touching them.
- Battery and gateway if installed.
- Generation meter if present.
- Handover documentation, array size, panel count and commissioning certificate if available.

Do not record app credentials, pairing codes or Wi-Fi details.

### 7. EV charging

Capture:

- Charger in context.
- Manufacturer and model.
- Indicator-light state.
- Cable and connector type.
- Visible controls and labels.
- Load-management equipment if documented or safely visible.
- Handover guide or commissioning information.

Do not scan pairing QR codes, connect an account or initiate a charging session.

### 8. Electricity and metering

Capture only from the outside:

- Consumer unit and circuit labels.
- Smart meter display where permitted.
- Import and export indicators if visible.
- Solar generation meter where present.
- Relevant isolator labels.

Do not remove covers. Avoid filming the MPRN, meter serial number or account information in footage intended for sharing.

### 9. Documents

Photograph or scan every available page relevant to:

- BER certificate and Advisory Report.
- A1 or NZEB specification.
- Heat-pump design and commissioning.
- Heat-loss indicator or heat-loss calculation.
- Heating zones and design temperatures.
- Cylinder and hot-water configuration.
- Ventilation design and commissioning.
- Solar array size, inverter and expected output.
- EV charger and load management.
- Electrical single-line or system diagram if supplied.
- Product manuals and warranties.
- Service intervals and installer contacts.
- Air-tightness or pressure-test result.
- Home User Guide and handover checklist.

Capture the full page square-on before taking close-ups. Include page numbers and document covers so evidence can be reconstructed correctly.

## Spoken narration prompts

Use plain statements such as:

- “I am entering the kitchen from the hallway.”
- “This EPH thermostat appears to control the ground floor, but that is not confirmed.”
- “The display currently shows 21 degrees and the system has not been adjusted.”
- “This label belongs to the outdoor heat-pump unit.”
- “I do not know whether this switch controls hot-water boost.”
- “This document was supplied in the show-house handover folder.”

Explicit uncertainty is useful evidence. Do not guess to make the capture sound complete.

## What OpenHouse should attempt after capture

### Level 1: Verified inventory

- Identify rooms, devices, controls and documents.
- Extract manufacturer and model candidates.
- Link every material fact to a photograph, video timestamp or document page.
- Mark each fact as confirmed, candidate, unknown or conflicting.

### Level 2: Home systems map

- Explain how heating, hot water, ventilation, solar, EV charging and metering appear to connect.
- Identify missing documents or commissioning evidence.
- Distinguish observed connections from inferred relationships.

### Level 3: Home-specific operating guide

- Explain each control in plain English using the exact model manual where found.
- Produce room and zone guidance.
- Explain normal heat-pump and ventilation behaviour.
- Create maintenance, warranty and service reminders.
- Produce a first-week, first-month and first-winter guide.

### Level 4: Question answering

Test at least these questions:

1. How should I run the heat pump in this house?
2. Which thermostat controls each area?
3. What does the current EPH display mean?
4. How is hot water produced and boosted?
5. What should I avoid changing without an installer?
6. How does ventilation work and when should boost be used?
7. What solar equipment is installed?
8. How would I know whether the panels are generating?
9. How should the EV charger interact with the home's electricity demand?
10. What maintenance is due and when?
11. What evidence is missing from the handover pack?
12. What data would OpenHouse need to explain a high first bill?

### Level 5: Handover completeness audit

- Documents supplied and missing.
- Device identity confirmed and unconfirmed.
- Controls mapped and unmapped.
- Commissioning evidence present and absent.
- Safety-critical questions requiring a professional.
- The next-best evidence request for each unresolved issue.

## Claims boundary

The capture may support claims that OpenHouse can:

- build a sourced home and systems record;
- retrieve model-specific guidance;
- explain controls and documents;
- identify handover gaps;
- prepare useful homeowner questions and operating guidance.

It cannot yet support claims that:

- the show house is operating efficiently;
- any observed setting is right or wrong;
- OpenHouse has identified energy savings;
- the heat pump is commissioned correctly;
- the solar or EV system is optimised;
- a resident would achieve a particular bill;
- video alone creates survey-grade dimensions or hidden-system knowledge.

## Success criteria

The test succeeds if OpenHouse can produce:

1. A provenance-linked home inventory.
2. A clear system map with explicit unknowns.
3. A genuinely home-specific operating guide.
4. At least ten grounded answers that outperform generic ChatGPT advice.
5. A useful handover-completeness audit.
6. One ranked next evidence request.
7. A list of what still requires measured data, commissioning evidence or a professional.
8. Measured capture time, processing time and time to first useful answer.

## Processing handoff

When the capture is complete:

- upload the walkthrough video;
- upload focused photographs as original files rather than screenshots where possible;
- upload available documents as PDFs or full-page images;
- state what permission exists for internal use and whether any public case-study use is permitted;
- provide any known house type, floor area, BER number with identifying fields redacted, and system specification;
- do not send passwords, account credentials, pairing codes, alarm information or access tokens.

The first processing pass will create an evidence manifest before producing guidance. No inference should be promoted into the Home Record without source, confidence and confirmation.
