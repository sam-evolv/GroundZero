# Investor Pitch Deck Research for OpenHouse

Created 2026-06-28.

## Sources checked

Accessible primary / high-quality sources:

1. Y Combinator Startup Library, Kevin Hale, "How to design a better pitch deck".
   - Core points: investors remember only a few ideas; slides should be legible, simple, obvious; communicate 5 to 7 important ideas; one idea per slide; avoid illegible, complicated or subtle slides; use large type, bold text, simple font and good contrast.
2. Sequoia Capital, "Writing a Business Plan".
   - Core sections: company purpose, problem, solution, why now, market potential, competition/alternatives, business model, team, financials, vision.
3. Guy Kawasaki 10/20/30 rule, common fundraising heuristic.
   - Direct page was blocked by Cloudflare during research, but the rule is widely cited: 10 slides, 20 minutes, 30-point font. Treat as a heuristic, not a law.
4. DocSend pitch-deck analytics, common fundraising benchmark.
   - Direct pages were blocked by Cloudflare during research. Use carefully as directional: investors skim decks quickly, so the deck must be understandable on a fast pass.

## Core conclusion

There is no perfect deck. There is a perfect job for the deck:

> Make a high-quality investor understand the business, remember the core insight, believe the upside, and want a meeting.

For OpenHouse, the deck should not try to explain every feature. It should make investors remember 5 to 7 ideas:

1. Homes are becoming complex energy systems.
2. Homeowners do not know how to operate them.
3. Developers hold the source-of-truth data at construction.
4. OpenHouse captures that context and creates a living home model.
5. The everyday property assistant is the adoption layer.
6. Energy intelligence and agentic optimisation are the expansion layers.
7. Ireland proves it; the UK scales it.

## Recommended length

### Send-ahead deck

Ideal: **12 to 15 slides**.
Maximum: **18 slides** if the visual density is low and the appendix is separated.

Why:
- Sequoia's recommended structure implies roughly 9 to 10 core content sections.
- YC emphasizes that investors only remember a few ideas and that slides should not become visual essays.
- Send-ahead decks must work without narration, but still need to be skimmable.

### Live meeting deck

Ideal: **10 to 12 slides**.

Use live narration for nuance. Do not cram the deck with everything you want to say.

### Appendix

5 to 10 extra slides are acceptable, but separate them clearly. Appendix slides can hold:
- detailed financial scenarios
- extra product screenshots
- data model examples
- competitor notes
- regulatory/market detail
- pilot metrics

Do not let the appendix pollute the main deck.

## Recommended content density

### Main rule

**One slide = one idea.**

From YC: simple ideas are not intertwined. A simple slide expresses one idea.

### Content per slide

For OpenHouse main slides:
- 1 headline sentence.
- 1 strong visual, diagram, screenshot or metric.
- 2 to 4 short bullets maximum.
- 25 to 50 words maximum on most slides.
- Avoid full paragraphs except maybe one thesis slide.

### Type and layout

Use:
- large type
- strong contrast
- short headlines
- plain labels
- text near the top when presenting live
- product visuals instead of descriptive text where possible

Avoid:
- tiny screenshots with unreadable UI
- dense blocks of copy
- multiple ideas per slide
- subtle punchlines
- large tables in the main deck
- complex diagrams that require verbal explanation to decode

## Should pricing be discussed?

Yes, for OpenHouse, but carefully.

### Why include pricing

OpenHouse has a believable wedge only if the buyer and price make sense. Investors will ask:
- Who pays?
- How much?
- Why will they pay now?
- Does this become recurring?
- Is the starting price too small to support venture scale?

Pricing is part of the business model. Sequoia explicitly includes "Business model: How do you intend to thrive?" as a deck section.

### How much pricing detail to include

Include a simple pricing ladder, not a detailed tariff sheet.

Recommended slide content:

1. **Developer provisioning:** €99/home.
   - Low-friction wedge to create the home model at construction/handover.
2. **Homeowner subscription:** €9.99/month.
   - Ongoing property assistant and energy intelligence.
3. **Agentic tier:** €19.99–€29.99/month later.
   - Connected optimisation of heat pump, EV, solar, battery, hot water, tariffs.
4. **Network revenue later:** developer analytics, housing associations, utility/grid partners.

Key wording:

> €99/home gets OpenHouse installed. Recurring energy intelligence captures the long-term value.

### What not to do with pricing

Do not show a complicated pricing table with many tiers.
Do not overclaim savings before proof.
Do not make the deck look like a sales brochure.
Do not imply agentic pricing is current revenue if it is a roadmap.
Do not make €99/home look like the whole business.

## Ideal OpenHouse deck structure

### 1. Title / one-line company purpose

**OpenHouse makes every new home intelligent from the moment it is built.**

Subtitle:
**The intelligence layer installed at construction and useful for the life of the home.**

### 2. Problem

Modern homes are becoming complex energy systems, but homeowners are not taught how to operate them.

### 3. Why now

Heat pumps, EVs, solar, batteries, smart meters, energy prices and time-of-use tariffs are moving complexity into the home.

### 4. Source-of-truth wedge

Developers hold the context during construction, but it disappears after handover.

### 5. Solution

OpenHouse captures construction-stage context and creates a living home model.

### 6. Product: everyday property assistant

Homeowners can ask mundane questions, talk to the assistant, upload images and diagnose issues.

### 7. Product: energy intelligence

OpenHouse explains home performance through Money, Comfort and Risk.

### 8. Future: agentic optimisation

Advisory first, assisted control next, agentic energy manager later with consent, rules and override.

### 9. Business model

€99/home developer provisioning, €9.99/month homeowner subscription, higher agentic tier later.

### 10. Data ownership / trust

Homeowners control personal and energy data. Developers supply build data. OpenHouse owns the software, model format and anonymised aggregate intelligence.

### 11. Market / expansion

Ireland proves the model. The UK creates the venture-scale market.

### 12. Competition / differentiation

Smart meters show what happened. OpenHouse explains why and what to do next.

### 13. Traction / proof / pilots

Use real proof where available: live schemes, developer relationships, homes onboarded, product usage, paid pilot proposal, testimonials, engagement.

### 14. Team / founder-market fit

Sam is inside the problem: property development, AI-native execution, premium product, developer trust.

### 15. Ask / use of funds

Raise to prove the construction-stage wedge, ship home model/property assistant/energy MVP, run paid developer pilots, and prepare UK expansion.

## Specific OpenHouse recommendations

### Main deck length

Target **15 slides**.

This is the right compromise because OpenHouse has to explain:
- developer wedge
- property assistant
- living home model
- energy intelligence
- agentic roadmap
- pricing
- data ownership
- Ireland-to-UK scale

Trying to do this in 8 slides would probably make it too abstract. Going beyond 18 slides risks losing focus.

### Content style

Each slide should have:
- a strong headline that can stand alone
- one visual
- 2 to 4 bullets
- no more than one major concept

### Pricing slide

Include pricing in the main deck. Keep it simple.

### Data slide

Include data ownership in the main deck. Do not push it to appendix.

### Financial model

Include one simple scenario slide in the main deck or appendix depending on deck length.

Use language:

> These are scenario models, not forecasts.

### Product screenshots

Use screenshots/mockups heavily. Investors need to see that this is not just a thesis.

Key visuals:
- homeowner asks: "What size is my living room?"
- image upload issue diagnosis
- living home model diagram
- Money / Comfort / Risk answer
- developer portfolio insight
