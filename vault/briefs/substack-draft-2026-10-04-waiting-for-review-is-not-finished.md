# Waiting for review is not finished

At 21:01 on 1 October, a signed iOS build of the Here’s Health app was submitted to Apple.

The last verified Apple state is **Waiting for Review**.

That is a real threshold. The build was uploaded, processed, selected and submitted. The app is no longer only a browser preview, a local branch or a set of release plans.

It is also not finished.

The current project record still says **NO-GO**. Apple has not approved the app. Manual release is selected. The submitted build predates later rota work. The payment, merchant, notification, physical-device and client-acceptance gates are still open.

This week produced the closest thing to a launch moment in the journal so far, and the honest account is still that the product has not launched.

## Apple can review a build I still cannot call complete

Here’s Health remains paid client work. The deposit did not arrive this week, but it changed the meaning of everything that did.

Build 8 was signed and submitted with its privacy disclosures, review notes and release settings recorded. A simulator copy of the same source reached the café basket review screen and showed the supplied allergen declaration and acknowledgement control. The recorded mobile suite passed 595 tests with one opt-in skip.

Those are substantial facts.

The same receipt also says there was no verified production payment or till arrival, no verified Shopify checkout inside the app, no verified push delivery and no physical-device run of build 8. One café location remained outside the enabled production-ordering set. The published allergen key had a recorded omission rather than an invented correction.

By Sunday, the backend had moved again. Six rota tables existed in the hosted database and contained one tenant, one employee identity, five entities, one audit row, two completed requests and two conflict scopes. A new draft pull request contained matching rota migrations and product source, but no deployment receipt bound the hosted state to that exact head. Build 8 predates the rota tranche.

Commerce also sat in an awkward middle state. A production Shopify checkout session existed, but it was still awaiting payment and had no native or confirmed order. All 27 recorded Square orders were sandbox-only. Twenty-three were paid inside the sandbox. None established a real production sale.

Apple is reviewing one bounded artifact. It is not reviewing the whole delivery chain on my behalf.

It cannot tell me whether a real customer payment reaches the merchant correctly, whether the till receives and fulfils the order, whether a refund works, whether the client accepts the rota experience, or whether the current hosted state matches the source I think it does.

Waiting for Review is a milestone. It is not a synonym for done.

## OpenHouse moved where it needed to move, but not far enough

OpenHouse’s production source advanced at the start of the week.

Seven commits across 60 files reached the production line. The exact head was tied to a successful production deployment, and the public portal and login surface rendered. The source included authentication, tenant-ownership and tenant-scoped analytics work.

That is the kind of movement the active risk required.

It did not close the risk.

The range contained no migration path. No controlled second-user or second-tenant actor journey was exercised. No persisted-row comparison, storage route or authenticated homeowner journey was accepted. The Supabase advisor findings remained open, including tables with row-level security enabled but no policies, security-definer findings, mutable search paths and disabled leaked-password protection.

Those findings are not evidence of a breach. They do not prove that one homeowner can access another homeowner’s data. They are also not something I can wave away because the relevant source changed and the portal stayed online.

The current dashboard still puts the tenant-data gap first for OpenHouse. That is right.

A separate research note this week proposed a first-interaction AI transparency receipt. The public marketing surfaces already say “AI Online” and “Powered by AI”, but the authenticated text, voice and photo journeys were not inspected. The note is a useful test design, not a compliance result, a product release or a buyer.

OpenHouse became more defensible in source. It did not become commercially validated, and its main safety gate did not become accepted.

## The delivery machinery kept outrunning the user result

Donworth Studio produced another version of the same contrast.

A new draft Desktop pull request appeared with source for runtime health checks, supplier-price review, recovered-job visibility, offline channel admission and daily exception workflows. It is mergeable and clean in GitHub, but it has no review, no independently rerun test suite, no accepted package, no installation and no rendered user journey.

The signed Mac app already installed on the machine remains an older v0.18.9 build and was not launched in the recorded checks. The live update feeds still disagree by platform, including a failing Mac Intel route.

A separate Porter Privé member-experience concept was browser-tested and published to a public preview after explicit authorisation. Its booking, guest, concierge, cancellation and reload journeys passed across seven responsive sizes. No outreach was sent. Sam’s visual approval, physical-device Safari test, current-platform discovery and asset-rights confirmation remain open.

That concept is more real than a mockup sitting in a folder. It is less real than a client conversation.

The underlying Hermes installation also advanced to stable v0.21.5 during the week. By Sunday, immutable upstream was already 1,706 commits ahead of that installed version. Aire and Cara still refused connection, and no accepted authenticated or physical-device journey advanced.

The numbers are dramatic. The user result is not.

## Research stayed responsible, but it still did not become revenue

Five new Evolv research proposals were added between 29 September and 4 October.

They covered:

- exact-product Triple E and Accelerated Capital Allowance evidence before a commercial solar purchase;
- the emerging EPBD solar trigger for new and renovated non-residential buildings;
- the announced but not yet fully documented uplift to the Non-Domestic Microgen Scheme;
- the difference between self-consumption and the SRESS export-only route; and
- future cross-premises energy-sharing readiness while Ireland’s operational framework remains unfinished.

Each note is careful about what it cannot claim. Each separates a dated public rule from a customer-specific decision. Each keeps client records, applications, contact and production action outside the research scope.

That discipline matters because the policy details are genuinely consequential. A buyer can make an expensive mistake by assuming a grant rate, a solar obligation, a tax treatment, an export route or an energy-sharing service exists before the governing facts are settled.

But the business position did not change this week.

The first commercial rooftop remains live. Reporting remains manual. No permissioned customer test, paid owner-side baseline, buyer response or willingness-to-pay result is recorded for any of the five new proposals.

Research can reduce the chance of saying something false. It cannot create demand by itself.
