### What is your company going to make? Please describe your product and what it does or will do.

OpenHouse gives every new home an assistant grounded in the exact property. At handover, the developer turns specifications, manuals, warranties, finishes, room details and local information into a living home record. The homeowner can ask how the heating works, what a warranty covers, which paint was used, how large a room is, how to set up broadband, how to reduce energy use or who provides a local service. They can save useful answers, upload photos and create a properly documented aftercare issue.

Routine questions are answered without staff involvement. OpenHouse anonymously groups the questions across a development so the developer can see recurring confusion, repeated problems and missing information long after handover. That reduces avoidable aftercare and helps the developer improve the next homes it delivers.

## Idea

### Why did you pick this idea to work on? Do you have domain expertise in this area? How do you know people need what you're making?

I picked this because I deal with the problem every day. I am one of three people helping deliver Longview Estates and work across sales, handover and aftercare. We spend months assembling specifications, drawings, manuals, warranties and contacts, then hand the buyer a folder and lose the thread across PDFs, email and staff memory. Homeowners ask questions whose answers already exist somewhere, while the developer learns little from what people struggle with after moving in.

I built OpenHouse inside that workflow. The questions coming through the product show that people want practical help with their exact home, while the developer needs a way to answer once, spot patterns and improve what it builds next. It proves the product is useful in a real operating environment. It does not yet prove that an independent developer will pay, which is the next test.

### Who are your competitors? What do you understand about your business that they don't?

Foundation and Spaciable are the closest competitors. Clixifix and Verisk Punchlist Manager focus more deeply on defects and warranty, while many developers still rely on PDFs, email, spreadsheets and staff memory.

Most products treat the homeowner portal as a place to distribute information or close tickets. I see it as a feedback system. Each anonymous question shows where people are confused, what information is missing and which problems keep recurring. OpenHouse uses the developer's record to answer the homeowner, then returns the aggregate learning to the developer. That can reduce today's aftercare while improving the next phase of homes. The defensibility is not the portal itself. It has to come from developer distribution, integrations and a home record that becomes more useful with every handover and aftercare interaction.

### How do or will you make money? How much could you make? Give your best estimate.

Developers will pay a scheme setup fee and a fee for every home provisioned. My first pricing hypothesis is €99 per home, so a developer delivering 500 homes a year would be worth €49,500 before setup fees. After handover, the assistant, warranty, maintenance and developer-insight layer can become a recurring annual contract.

The provisioning fee is the entry point, not the whole business. If 1,000 large developers eventually paid an average of €100,000 a year, OpenHouse would reach €100 million in annual revenue. Neither price has been commercially validated. The first paid external scheme will test the initial price, the operational value and which recurring workflows developers will pay to keep.
