# Core positioning

OpenHouse is the construction-stage intelligence layer for modern homes.

Developers already have the truth about every new home during construction: drawings, dimensions, specifications, materials, devices, manuals, warranties, installers, BER information and commissioning records.

OpenHouse captures that context before it disappears and turns it into a living home model.

Homeowners use OpenHouse first as an everyday property assistant for practical questions, issue diagnosis, image uploads, warranties, manuals and aftercare.

That living model then expands into home energy intelligence, Money / Comfort / Risk guidance, developer portfolio insight, homeowner subscriptions, and later agentic optimisation of heat pumps, EV charging, solar, batteries, hot water and tariffs.

The story should be:

**construction-stage context → everyday property assistant → living home model → energy intelligence → trusted agentic optimisation → Ireland proof → UK scale**

# Product examples that must be visible

The deck must make the day-one utility obvious.

Homeowner questions:

- What size is my living room?
- Where is the stopcock?
- What paint colour is in the kitchen?
- How do I reset the heat pump?
- Can I drill into this wall?
- Is this crack normal?
- What warranty covers this appliance?
- Who do I contact about this leak?
- Why is my bill higher this month?
- When should I charge my EV?

Example product answer:

Homeowner: What size is my living room?

OpenHouse: Your living room is 4.8m x 3.9m, approximately 18.7m².

Image upload flow:

1. Homeowner uploads a photo of a crack, leak, alarm, finish defect or appliance fault.
2. OpenHouse classifies the issue.
3. It checks home context and history.
4. It asks follow-up questions.
5. It assesses warranty relevance.
6. It recommends the next step or routes it.

Example response:

This looks like a finish crack rather than structural movement. It may be warranty-relevant if it widens or appears near openings. I can log it for review.

Use cautious language. Do not imply perfect diagnosis.
