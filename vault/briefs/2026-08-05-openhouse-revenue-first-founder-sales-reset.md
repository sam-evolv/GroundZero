# OpenHouse revenue-first founder-sales reset

## Current context

Sam is mentally exhausted by trying to make OpenHouse commercially acceptable without producing independent revenue. The core frustration is not product capability. It is the lack of one external developer willing to pay or formally commit.

Verified current position:

- OpenHouse has €0 external revenue.
- Carol Tallon introduced Sam to David at Bridgewater on 4 August 2026 and sent him the OpenHouse interview.
- Sam replied proposing a call or meeting. No meeting date or response has been captured yet.
- Cairn did not become a usable first-developer route. Sam previously estimated that Cairn represented roughly 7 to 8 per cent of Irish new-build supply. The combined 15 per cent Cairn-plus-Bridgewater estimate in this voice note is an assumption, not a verified market-share figure.
- OpenHouse has conservative internal operating proof: 259 property-specific questions across 79 home contexts in four Longview developments.
- YC and the NDRC pre-accelerator remain pending as last verified.
- Sam recalls being told by a High Potential Start-Up contact that revenue or investment would materially strengthen the funding case. That recollection should not be represented as a formal funding commitment or guaranteed acceptance.

## Durable decision

The next phase is founder-led revenue creation, not another broad product-build cycle.

The target is one external developer commitment. The order is:

1. Give the Bridgewater warm introduction a clean chance to convert into a meeting.
2. If a meeting is secured, prepare aggressively but keep it discovery-led. The desired outcome is a second operational session, a one-scheme workflow map or a paid pilot proposal.
3. If Bridgewater remains silent after a reasonable follow-up window, progress the existing warm Irish routes and then the ranked cold queue.
4. Add a bounded UK lane once the Irish warm routes are in motion. Do not abandon Ireland based on Cairn and Bridgewater alone.
5. Do not open a broad US cold-outbound lane until there is a specific buyer segment, channel, warm route or credible local wedge. The US market is larger, but generic remote outreach from Cork is not automatically easier.
6. Freeze speculative features. Product changes must be tied to a named buyer, pilot requirement, release-safety gate or measured usage problem.

## Candid interpretation

Sam is right that building is not the current bottleneck. Founder sales is.

He is also right that optics matter less than evidence. One paid external deployment would change investor, accelerator and hiring conversations more than another deck or product surface.

The correction is to avoid turning that insight into undisciplined activity. “Spraying emails and LinkedIn posts” can consume every evening while producing little learning. The commercial system must be narrow enough that each response improves the offer.

## Thirty-day commercial proof loop

### Target outcome

- two developer discovery conversations;
- one written one-scheme proposal;
- one paid pilot, signed commitment or explicit rejection that materially changes the offer.

### Buyer and offer

- Buyer: residential developer handover, customer-care or aftercare owner.
- Initial offer: one measured scheme-launch and aftercare workflow.
- Commercial model: implementation fee plus a per-home fee, with roughly €99 per home still treated as an unvalidated hypothesis.
- First ask: a 30-minute workflow conversation, not a deck review or full platform pitch.

### Sequence

1. Bridgewater.
2. Sean Gallagher reconnection for market guidance and a relevant operator introduction.
3. One targeted message to the strongest relevant Evara contact.
4. One targeted message to the strongest relevant Glenveagh customer-experience or customer-care contact.
5. Then Richmond Homes, McGarrell Reilly and Lioncor one at a time if the warm routes do not produce conversations.
6. Build the UK lane from developers with a visible handover or aftercare owner and a scheme that can support a bounded design-partner pilot.

### Weekly evidence

Track only:

- targeted messages sent;
- replies;
- conversations booked;
- qualified workflow problems;
- proposals issued;
- paid or signed commitments;
- reasons for rejection.

Do not count impressions, general encouragement, deck refinements or new features as commercial progress.

## Bridgewater rule

Do not “throw the kitchen sink” at the first meeting. That would turn urgency into a feature dump.

Prepare the full proof, but use only what matches Bridgewater's stated process. The meeting should establish:

- how handover information is assembled;
- where homeowner questions, snags and warranty requests go;
- who owns the workflow;
- which upcoming scheme provides the cleanest test;
- what a pilot must prove;
- and who must approve it.

The close is a dated next step, not “I will send more information.”

## NDRC and funding rule

Revenue improves the case, but no accelerator or funder should be treated as guaranteed. If a Bridgewater meeting or pilot is secured, use it as fresh evidence and ask NDRC or the relevant programme contact whether they want an update. Do not claim the company has revenue until a contract is signed and payment is due or received under clearly defined terms.

## January job decision

The default remains to keep the day job until the January bonus is paid unless an existing exit gate is independently met.

One pilot alone is not automatically enough to resign. Before leaving, verify:

- signed contract value and payment timing;
- cash actually received;
- personal and company runway after tax and delivery costs;
- delivery capacity while supporting the customer;
- employer, confidentiality, IP and conflict boundaries;
- and whether the commercial pipeline can plausibly repeat.

See [[decisions/2026-08-03-day-job-bonus-and-openhouse-gates]].

## Founder role insight

Sam's strongest founder skills are product vision, systems thinking, innovation and one-to-one persuasion. The weakness is not an inability to sell. It is avoiding the repeated, uncomfortable prospecting work required to create enough one-to-one sales opportunities.

A future commercial cofounder or senior hire may be valuable, but only after Sam has personally learned the sale, buyer language, objections and implementation boundary. Hiring someone to discover the market from scratch would outsource the learning OpenHouse most needs.
