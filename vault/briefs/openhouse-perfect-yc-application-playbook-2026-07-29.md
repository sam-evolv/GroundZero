# OpenHouse perfect YC application playbook

## Bottom line

The perfect YC application is not the most polished application. It is the shortest truthful chain of evidence that makes a partner believe five things:

1. This founder is unusually capable.
2. He knows something important that others have missed.
3. He has already moved surprisingly fast.
4. A specific group of users wants what he is building.
5. The narrow starting product can become a very large company.

OpenHouse already has strong evidence for the first three. It has plausible but incomplete evidence for the fifth. It is weak on the fourth because the only deployment is founder-connected, revenue is €0, and clean engagement evidence is missing.

The application prose is already strong. The acceptance bottleneck is external proof, a safe demo and legal hygiene.

## The distinction that matters

### Application quality

The current copy is clear, candid and materially better than most startup applications. It explains the buyer, workflow, founder insight, internal deployment and expansion path without pretending that internal use is external traction.

### Acceptance evidence

The current company evidence is less complete:

- Strong founder-market fit
- Strong build velocity
- Real production deployment
- Weak independent customer validation
- No revenue
- No clean retention or repeat-usage metric
- No measured developer ROI
- IP assignment and Longview permission still need formal resolution

Improving the writing further has sharply diminishing returns. One paid external commitment improves several YC questions simultaneously: demand, sales ability, pricing, distribution and independence from Longview.

## What a YC partner must understand in the first minute

### 1. What OpenHouse does

> OpenHouse gives homebuilders one system to hand over each home and manage aftercare from a living record of that home.

This identifies the buyer, product, workflow and durable object.

### 2. Why Sam

> I helped take a planned 750-plus-home development from greenfield to more than 200 completed homes, saw this problem repeatedly, and built OpenHouse largely alone during evenings and weekends.

This is specific founder evidence. Do not substitute “deep industry experience.”

### 3. What exists

> OpenHouse is live internally across four Longview Estates developments. Production contains 281 home records, 143 distinct homes with timestamped purchaser handover agreements and 85 homes linked to homeowner accounts.

Immediately qualify the scope:

> Longview is my employer, not an independent paying customer. OpenHouse has €0 revenue.

### 4. The insight

> Handover is the only moment when the developer still has the authority, documents and installed-system context required to create an accurate digital record of the home at low cost.

The consequence:

> The developer is both the first buyer and the distribution channel into every completed home.

### 5. Why it can become large

> Developers first pay for handover and aftercare. Each deployment creates a persistent home record that can support warranty, maintenance and home-performance workflows after completion.

Do not list ten future markets. The expansion should look causal, not imaginative.

## The perfect evidence hierarchy

YC accepts companies at many stages, including zero revenue. Revenue remains powerful because it compresses several forms of proof into one fact.

For OpenHouse, evidence should be ranked as follows:

1. Paid external pilot with money received
2. Signed paid pilot with a fixed invoice date
3. Independent developer actively using the product
4. Signed proposal pending procurement
5. Clean repeat-usage and outcome evidence from Longview
6. Non-binding letter of intent
7. Named introduction or sales conversation
8. Product records and provisioned accounts
9. Roadmap and market claims

The current application relies heavily on levels five through eight. The highest-value move is reaching level one or two.

## Field-by-field standard

### 50-character description

Keep:

> Handover and aftercare for homebuilders

Why it works:

- Concrete workflow
- Clear customer
- No buzzwords
- Leaves room for the larger company to be discovered

Do not replace it with “operating system,” “digital twin,” “home intelligence” or “AI platform.”

### What is your company going to make?

A perfect answer follows this order:

1. Buyer and primary job
2. User path
3. Current operational consequence
4. Expansion from the same record

Recommended structure:

> OpenHouse gives residential developers one system to hand over homes and manage aftercare. The developer provisions the approved information for each home, including its house type, installed systems, documents and warranties. The homeowner receives that record at handover, can ask property-specific questions and can report an issue with photos and context. The developer sees questions, missing information and aftercare issues tied to the correct home instead of scattered across PDFs, email and WhatsApp. Each deployment creates a home record that can later support warranties, maintenance and home performance.

The answer should not explain all product modules. YC needs the causal loop, not a feature inventory.

### How far along are you?

Lead with actions, not database size.

Strong order:

1. Live internally across four developments
2. 143 homes with completed timestamped handover agreements
3. 85 homes linked to homeowner accounts
4. 281 total home records
5. One observed behaviour and resulting product change
6. Internal, founder-connected status
7. €0 revenue

Why: completed handover actions are stronger than provisioned records. Product records prove scope, not demand.

### Are people using the product?

This is the weakest major answer because OpenHouse cannot yet report a clean active-user or retention metric.

The honest answer should remain explicit:

> Yes, inside Longview Estates. We do not yet have a clean retention metric and do not treat provisioned homes as active users.

Before an interview, produce a defined cohort with:

- Homeowners invited
- Accounts activated
- Homes completing a handover action
- Homes asking at least one useful question
- Repeat active homes over 30 days
- Issues submitted with evidence
- Answers delivered from approved sources
- Developer interventions required

No denominator, no metric.

### Revenue

Keep it brutally simple:

> €0 revenue. The Longview deployment is internal operating proof, not an arm's-length paying customer.

Do not soften or contextualise the zero. The rest of the application explains what has been built.

### Why this idea?

The perfect answer is a compressed causal story:

1. Sam operated the workflow.
2. He repeatedly observed a specific failure.
3. He identified why the failure occurs.
4. He built and deployed a remedy.
5. He states what still needs proving.

The strongest failure is not merely fragmented documents. It is the loss of property-specific context when responsibility passes from the developer to the homeowner.

End with:

> The internal deployment proves the product can be built and operated. The next proof is whether an independent developer will pay for it.

This candour is persuasive because it shows Sam knows the exact open question.

### Competitors and insight

The application must acknowledge Foundation first because it is the most credible current overlap.

Recommended structure:

- Foundation: homebuilder customer experience, buyer portals, ownership and warranty workflows
- Spaciable: homeowner portals and handover content
- Clixifix and Verisk Punchlist Manager: defects and warranty
- HomeBinder and Centriq: homeowner records
- CRM, PDFs, shared drives, email and WhatsApp: actual everyday substitutes

The distinction should be structural:

> Most tools optimise one workflow or one side of the relationship. OpenHouse starts with the developer's approved record for the individual home and keeps that context attached to homeowner questions, issue evidence and aftercare.

Do not claim that competitors cannot add AI. They can. The harder difference is organising the system around a persistent home identity created at handover.

### Business model and market size

The current pricing answer is directionally reasonable but remains hypothetical.

Risks in the current answer:

- The printed-folder comparison may make OpenHouse look like a digitised cost replacement rather than important operational software.
- €99 per home alone may not create enough revenue per developer.
- The recurring portfolio and aftercare components are unpriced and unvalidated.
- The route to €100 million depends on a large number of high-volume developers.

The perfect answer needs one real commercial test:

- Scheme setup fee
- Per-home provisioning fee
- Recurring fee during active aftercare
- Exact pilot cohort
- Exact price presented to a developer
- What outcome the customer is paying to improve

Until that exists, say clearly that pricing is a hypothesis.

The venture-scale case should rely on expansion in contract value, not only multiplying a small per-home fee across the entire world.

### How will you get users?

The answer is strong when it stays operational:

1. Sell one paid scheme to a mid-sized developer.
2. Import approved house-type and handover information.
3. Provision a bounded homeowner cohort.
4. Measure handover completion and aftercare outcomes.
5. Expand to other schemes inside that developer.
6. Repeat with similar homebuilders.

The Bridgewater introduction is not traction. It is the next sales action.

### Founder and technical work

Do not apologise for being solo or using AI coding agents.

The evidence is:

- Sam owns product and technical decisions.
- Sam reviews, tests and deploys the work.
- No human non-founder wrote product code.
- The product is live and technically substantial.

The application should not imply that AI is a cofounder. YC is assessing Sam's judgment and execution.

### Most impressive achievement

The Longview development achievement is credible because it demonstrates:

- Responsibility
- Physical-world execution
- Persistence
- Planning complexity
- Coordination across a large project

Keep the exact scope. Avoid wording that could imply sole responsibility for Longview.

### Commitment

The answer must be unambiguous:

> If accepted, I will leave my current role, work exclusively on OpenHouse and attend the batch in San Francisco.

No hedging, provided this is still true.

### Legal and IP

This is a company-readiness issue, not a writing issue.

Before investment or submission if possible:

1. Match the legal name and capitalisation exactly to the Irish company record.
2. Execute assignment of personally created OpenHouse IP to the company.
3. Document Longview's knowledge and permission for the internal deployment.
4. Clarify the lawful basis and responsibility for property and homeowner data.
5. Preserve the honest disclosure that some work occurred during Longview hours.

The Dropbox public application is directly relevant. Dropbox disclosed employer-adjacent work and had written confirmation that the employer had no ownership claim.

Do not hide the issue. Resolve it.

## Founder video standard

The perfect video is not cinematic. It should feel like Sam explaining the company to an intelligent person in one take.

Required beats:

1. Name and founder status
2. Specific domain role
3. The observed failure
4. What OpenHouse does
5. What is live
6. Unusual speed or commitment

Avoid:

- Decks
- Cuts and effects
- Memorised delivery
- Broad market language
- Long feature lists
- Trying to sound American or “startup-like”

A good opening:

> Hi, I'm Sam Donworth, the sole founder of OpenHouse. I help run a residential developer where we have taken a planned 750-plus-home development from greenfield to more than 200 completed homes.

The strongest close is build evidence, not motivational commitment:

> OpenHouse is now live internally across four developments. I built it during evenings and weekends, and if accepted I will work on it full-time.

## Product demo standard

The demo must prove the unique insight rather than show the entire product.

Best 90-second path:

1. Open one safe example home.
2. Show its approved documents and installed systems.
3. Ask one question the record can answer.
4. Show the property-specific source.
5. Ask one question the record cannot support.
6. Show a clarification or refusal rather than a guess.
7. Submit one issue with photo evidence.
8. Show the issue reaching the developer with the correct home context.

The demonstration should make this conclusion unavoidable:

> OpenHouse knows this home and carries that knowledge across the developer-to-homeowner handoff.

A dashboard tour will not do that.

## What makes an application memorable

Memorability is not clever wording. It is one unusual fact plus one sharp insight.

For OpenHouse:

- Unusual fact: a solo founder built a substantial production system during evenings and weekends while helping operate a large residential development.
- Sharp insight: the home record must be created at handover because the source context becomes harder and more expensive to reconstruct afterwards.

Everything else should reinforce those two points.

## The fatal application mistakes

1. Implying Longview is an independent customer
2. Calling 281 provisioned home records active users
3. Hiding €0 revenue
4. Claiming external demand from an introduction
5. Leading with a broad consumer AI assistant
6. Treating Foundation as irrelevant
7. Claiming a data moat that does not yet exist
8. Using roadmap capabilities in the present tense
9. Submitting a broken or privacy-unsafe demo
10. Leaving employer IP and data permission ambiguous
11. Overexplaining product architecture
12. Trying to sound impressive instead of being clear

## What would make the application materially better

### Highest-impact improvement

A signed independent paid pilot.

Why it matters:

- Proves willingness to pay
- Proves Sam can sell outside Longview
- Tests pricing
- Validates the developer distribution strategy
- Creates an external user cohort
- Gives YC a concrete post-application update

### Second improvement

Clean cohort usage and outcome evidence from the existing deployment.

Why it matters:

- Distinguishes a live product from a provisioned database
- Shows user understanding
- Supports the aftercare pain hypothesis
- Gives Sam the metric fluency expected in an interview

### Third improvement

Execute IP assignment and document Longview permission.

Why it matters:

- Removes a potentially fatal diligence issue
- Makes the commitment to form a standalone company credible
- Prevents an avoidable distraction from the founder and product story

### Fourth improvement

A reviewer-safe trust-loop demo.

Why it matters:

- Proves the product exists
- Demonstrates the product's real differentiation
- Shows safety and property-specific grounding
- Makes the company understandable faster than more prose

## The correct weekly focus

This week is not an application-writing week. It is a proof week.

1. Resolve IP and Longview permission.
2. Submit the factual late application with safe videos.
3. Put a priced pilot in front of Bridgewater or another independent developer.
4. Build the Longview cohort evidence sheet.
5. Send YC one concise progress update when a fact materially improves.

Ignore new product breadth unless it is required to complete the safe demo or close the paid pilot.

## Final standard

A perfect OpenHouse application should make a YC partner think:

> Sam understands a real housing workflow that most software founders have never seen. He built far more than expected while part-time and put it into real homes. His insight is that the permanent home record must be created while the developer still holds the source context. The commercial proof is still early, but he knows exactly what remains unproven and is moving quickly to answer it.

That is stronger than pretending OpenHouse already has product-market fit.
