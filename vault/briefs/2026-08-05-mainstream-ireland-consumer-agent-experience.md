# Mainstream Ireland consumer assistant experience and three-minute magic

## Decisive conclusion

The mass-market product should not introduce people to agents. It should make the idea of an agent irrelevant.

The front door is a familiar promise:

> **Send it to Cara. She'll sort the next step.**

The first three minutes must not be a chat tutorial, a list of features, a personality quiz, a connector wall or a model demonstration. The person should give Cara one real piece of their life, see her understand what matters, approve one useful action and receive a finished result.

The first magic loop is:

`real thing -> understood correctly -> useful next step prepared -> one-tap approval -> done and receipted`

The initial product is not "Facebook for AI agents" in the literal sense of a directory or social network. The defensible interpretation is that Cara becomes the default identity, context and trust layer through which ordinary people first use digital workers. Facebook did not win by explaining social-network protocols. It gave people one identity, familiar people and an immediately legible feed. Cara should give people one assistant, one memory and one place where things get finished.

## Verified Irish market evidence

### Ireland is digitally active but agent-unfamiliar

Observed from the Central Statistics Office, 2025:

- 95% of people aged 16 and over had used the internet in the previous three months.
- 95% of internet users used it daily.
- Email was used by 95% of internet users.
- Internet or mobile banking was used by 94%.
- Online shopping was used by 85%.
- Social networking was used by 75%.
- More than seven in ten internet users aged 75 and over used internet banking.

Observed from the CSO's first dedicated 2025 generative AI chapter:

- 42% of internet users had used generative AI in some form during the preceding three months.
- Among non-users, 54% said they had no need for it.
- 20% said they did not know how to use it.
- 19% cited security, privacy or safety concerns.
- Security concern was higher among female non-users, at 24%, than male non-users, at 13%.

Observed from NALA, citing DESI 2023:

- More than one in four adults in Ireland, 27%, had below-basic digital skills.
- NALA explains that this can mean difficulty using everyday technology such as smartphones and computers.
- People with unmet literacy needs may be able to read but struggle with tasks such as completing forms or finding information online.

Observed from the European Commission's 2025 Ireland Digital Decade report:

- 77% of Irish respondents felt that digitalisation of public and private services was making life easier.
- 92% considered it important that public authorities address fake news and disinformation online.

### Strategic interpretation

Ireland is not an offline market. It is a confidence, comprehension and trust market.

The target user may use WhatsApp, Revolut, Facebook, email, online shopping and internet banking every day while still believing "AI is not for me." The product should therefore borrow the interaction grammar of messaging, banking approvals, parcel tracking and phone contacts. It should not borrow the vocabulary or aesthetics of AI software.

The adoption problem is not teaching people what AI stands for. It is proving that this assistant is useful, safe and intended for them.

## The target user

Do not define the mainstream user as old, unintelligent or digitally incapable. That framing will produce patronising design.

Design for three behavioural conditions:

### 1. Digitally active, conceptually uninterested

They use online banking, WhatsApp, Facebook, email and shopping, but have no interest in prompts, models or agents. They will try something if the result is obvious.

### 2. Capable but anxious about getting it wrong

They can complete digital tasks, but dislike unfamiliar permissions, forms, technical choices and irreversible actions. They need progressive confidence rather than education.

### 3. Busy and mentally overloaded

They understand the task but postpone it because it involves finding a message, checking a date, making a call, writing a reply or remembering a deadline.

The product succeeds when all three say:

> "I don't know how it did that. I just sent it to Cara."

## Category and positioning

### What the product is called

Use a human name as the working product identity. Cara is strategically strong because it is:

- easy to say and spell
- naturally used in a sentence
- a plausible phone contact
- warm without sounding childish
- Irish-adjacent without shamrocks or forced cultural theatre
- capable of becoming a verb-like referral phrase: "send it to Cara"

Cara must remain a working name until Irish and EU trademark, app-store, domain and product-collision checks pass.

### Public category

Use:

- your digital assistant
- help with life admin
- a helping hand on your phone
- send it to Cara
- Cara will sort the next step

Do not use in normal acquisition or product navigation:

- AI
- artificial intelligence
- agent
- chatbot
- automation
- model
- workflow
- integration
- prompt
- token
- computer use

Transparency must still be honest. Legal, privacy and trust surfaces can state plainly that Cara is an automated digital assistant and explain how it works. Simplicity cannot become deception.

### Primary promise

> **That thing you've been meaning to sort? Send it to Cara.**

Supporting line:

> Take a photo, share a message or tell her what happened. Cara finds what matters, prepares the next step and asks before she does anything important.

Primary CTA:

> **Sort something now**

Secondary CTA:

> **Watch Cara sort one**

## The three-minute experience

### The governing rule

The first session should begin with a real object, not an empty text box.

A blank prompt asks a newcomer to understand the product, invent a use case and phrase the request correctly. That is three cognitive jobs before the product has earned any trust.

The first screen should ask:

> **What do you need sorted?**

Then offer three large actions:

- **Take a photo**
- **Share something**
- **Tell Cara**

Underneath, one line:

> A letter, appointment, booking, bill, email or anything you've been putting off.

No signup wall should precede the first value moment. The trial can use a short-lived anonymous session with a clear retention statement. Ask for an account only when saving the result, connecting a service or continuing across devices requires one.

### Exact sequence

#### 0:00 to 0:15

The person lands from an advert, QR code, referral or shared result.

They see:

> **Send it to Cara. She'll sort the next step.**

The page is almost empty. One large button says **Sort something now**. No navigation menu, pricing grid, logos, feature cards or AI explanation appears above it.

#### 0:15 to 0:35

They choose **Take a photo**, **Share something** or **Tell Cara**.

The best first inputs are familiar pieces of life admin:

- an appointment letter
- a school notice
- a travel confirmation
- a bill or renewal letter
- an email that needs a reply
- a form they do not understand
- a screenshot containing a date or request

If they have nothing to hand, **Try an example** opens a realistic but clearly labelled sample. The sample is a fallback, not the default, because a personal object creates the strongest magic.

#### 0:35 to 1:05

Cara shows genuine progress in plain English:

- **Reading it**
- **Found the important bits**
- **Preparing the next step**

Do not display fake reasoning, technical logs, tokens or a long thinking animation. Progress statements must correspond to real task stages.

#### 1:05 to 1:35

Cara returns an action card, not a paragraph.

Example from an appointment letter:

> **I've got it.**
>
> Dental appointment  
> Tuesday 18 August at 10:30  
> Douglas Medical Centre
>
> I've prepared:
> - a calendar event
> - a reminder to leave at 9:50
> - a note to bring your medical card
>
> **Add these**

The source remains visible behind **See what I used**. If Cara is uncertain, it says exactly what is unclear instead of guessing.

#### 1:35 to 1:55

When the user taps **Add these**, request calendar permission in context:

> **Allow Cara to add this appointment?**
>
> She can add this event. She cannot read the rest of your calendar unless you choose that later.

Then the operating-system permission prompt appears. Permission follows understood value, not the other way around.

#### 1:55 to 2:15

The result lands with a restrained haptic and a simple receipt:

> **Done.**
>
> Appointment added  
> Reminder set  
> Nothing was sent to anyone

Actions:

- **View it**
- **Undo**

#### 2:15 to 2:40

Cara asks for the minimum needed to preserve continuity:

> **Want me to remember this and help with the next one?**

Use phone-number verification, Sign in with Apple or a passkey. Do not introduce a password.

#### 2:40 to 3:00

Finish with the larger possibility, not a feature menu:

> **Next time, send anything here.**
>
> I can read it, find what matters and prepare the next step. I'll always ask before I send, buy, book or change anything important.

Then show one optional expansion:

> **Want Cara to find things on your computer when you're away?**

This begins paired-device setup only after the first win.

## The holy-fuck test

A first-run experience passes only when it satisfies all six conditions:

1. **Mine:** it used the person's real object, message or situation.
2. **Right:** it extracted the important facts correctly.
3. **Finished:** it produced or completed something useful, not just advice.
4. **Fast:** the result arrived within 180 seconds.
5. **Safe:** the person understood and approved consequential access or action.
6. **Showable:** the person can explain or demonstrate the moment to someone else in one sentence.

A response that merely summarises a letter, writes generic prose or answers a question does not pass, even if the model output is impressive.

### Proposed alpha gate

At least 70% of target users should complete a personal first task without help. Of those, at least 40% should independently use language equivalent to "I didn't expect it to do that" or immediately name another task they want Cara to handle.

The stronger signal is behavioural:

- they give Cara a second real item within 24 hours
- they connect one service after the first win
- they show or recommend it to another person

## First input templates

The alpha should support four object-to-action contracts exceptionally well.

### Appointment or event

Input:
- letter
- screenshot
- booking email

Output:
- confirmed date, time and place
- calendar event
- travel or departure reminder
- list of required items
- source receipt

### School or household notice

Input:
- school message
- club notice
- local event notice

Output:
- plain summary
- calendar item
- required payment or item reminder
- drafted message to another household member
- no send without approval

### Bill or renewal

Input:
- utility bill
- insurance renewal
- subscription notice

Output:
- amount and deadline
- what changed
- reminder
- cancellation or comparison checklist
- prepared contact message
- no payment, cancellation or switch without approval

### Travel confirmation

Input:
- booking email
- screenshot
- PDF

Output:
- itinerary card
- calendar items
- check-in and departure reminders
- document checklist
- shareable household version

These are legible to mainstream users and can be deterministically evaluated. Avoid tax, legal, medical diagnosis, benefits eligibility and financial advice in the first alpha.

## Product structure

The product should have four simple destinations:

### Ask

The default entry point. Camera, share and voice are more prominent than typing. It is a launcher for real objects and intentions, not a chatbot history.

### Doing

A parcel-tracking-like view of active work:

- Reading your renewal
- Waiting for your approval
- Checking two options
- Computer offline, will continue when it reconnects

Every task has a clear state. Nothing stalls silently.

### Done

A calm feed of completed work and receipts:

- what Cara did
- what she used
- what was sent or changed
- what can be undone

This becomes the evidence that the product works.

### You

The inspectable personal Ground Zero:

- what Cara remembers
- connected devices and services
- permissions and last use
- personal, work and household boundaries
- delete, disconnect and export

Do not call this Memory, Context, Data or Settings in the primary navigation. **You** is more understandable.

## Look and feel

### Overall character

Cara should feel like a calm, exceptionally competent Irish service, not an AI lab and not a children's app.

Emotional target:

- familiar enough to trust immediately
- premium enough to trust with important things
- warm enough to ask without embarrassment
- quiet enough not to feel manipulative
- fast enough to feel almost physical

### Visual system

Reuse the strongest existing Cara direction:

- warm cream or paper surfaces
- deep ink or turf-charcoal text
- one restrained Atlantic green accent
- amber for attention and approval pending
- red only for destructive or failed actions
- humanist display type with the system font for body copy
- large text, generous line height and strong contrast
- minimum 44-point touch targets
- cards that resemble useful documents or receipts, not chat bubbles

Irishness should appear through language, photography, service scenarios and quiet warmth. Do not use shamrocks, Celtic motifs, emerald gradients, pub clichés or exaggerated slang.

### Motion and sound

Motion has only three jobs:

- show where an item came from
- reveal that Cara understood it
- confirm that an action completed

Use short interruptible springs, restrained haptics and one subtle completion sound. No glowing orb, particles, pulsing brain, robot animation, confetti or endless thinking shimmer.

### Progress as theatre of competence

The product may dramatise real work, but never fake it.

Good:

- Reading the letter
- Found the appointment details
- Adding the reminder

Bad:

- Analysing with advanced intelligence
- Agent is executing workflow
- Thinking deeply
- 17 tools used

### Accessibility and literacy

NALA's guidance should become product rules:

- organise information around the reader's needs
- use white space, bullets and subheadings
- keep paragraphs short
- use clean fonts and strong contrast
- put simple visuals beside instructions
- avoid background images behind text

GOV.UK's service guidance adds:

- users scan rather than read
- start with less
- ask simple questions
- use one thing per page
- if the interface requires explanation, redesign it

Every screen should be tested at large text sizes, with VoiceOver or TalkBack, on a smaller phone, in sunlight and on a weak connection.

## Voice and language

Cara's tone is:

- brief
- direct
- warm
- calm
- confident without certainty theatre
- lightly Irish in rhythm, not in costume

Examples:

Instead of **Processing your document**:

> Reading it now.

Instead of **The agent needs additional context**:

> I can't see the date clearly. Is it Tuesday the 18th?

Instead of **Workflow complete**:

> Done. It's in your calendar.

Instead of **Connect integration**:

> Let Cara add this one.

Instead of **Access denied**:

> I couldn't open that folder. Nothing changed. Choose another folder or try again.

Do not overuse "grand", "sound", "no bother" or Irish idioms. One badly judged localism will feel more artificial than neutral English.

## Trust design

Trust must be visible in the main experience, not hidden in a privacy policy.

### Progressive permissions

- no connector wall at signup
- ask only when a specific useful action needs access
- explain the exact scope in one or two sentences
- default to one task or read-only access
- offer broader remembered permission only after repeated use

### Approval states

Every consequential action follows:

`prepare -> show -> approve -> execute -> verify -> receipt`

Always require explicit approval for:

- sending a message or email
- booking or cancelling
- purchasing or paying
- publishing
- deleting
- changing an account
- sharing personal information

### Failure behaviour

A trustworthy assistant does not hide failure.

It says:

- what happened
- what did not happen
- what information was preserved
- whether it will retry
- what the user can do next

### Memory

Cara should remember less at first and explain it better.

After a useful moment:

> **Remember that Sarah handles school messages?**

Options:

- Yes
- Not now
- Never remember this kind of thing

The **You** area shows the source and lets the user correct or remove it.

## The attraction strategy

### Do not advertise an AI assistant

Advertise an avoided annoyance and show the resolution.

Campaign platform:

> **Send it to Cara.**

Supporting executions:

- School letter on the counter. **Send it to Cara.**
- Insurance renewal you keep moving around the kitchen. **Send it to Cara.**
- Appointment buried in a screenshot. **Send it to Cara.**
- Email you have opened six times. **Send it to Cara.**

The ad should show the entire input-to-result loop in ten to fifteen seconds. Never lead with a talking avatar, abstract intelligence, a feature grid or a claim that Cara can do everything.

### Product-led word of mouth

Every user should always have a safe pub demo:

1. take a photo of a real notice
2. watch Cara extract it
3. approve one action
4. show the receipt

Completed results may include a privacy-safe referral footer:

> Sorted with Cara

Sharing must never reveal the underlying document or personal details by default.

### Acquisition sequence

#### Closed Irish alpha

Recruit 30 people deliberately mixed across:

- age
- confidence
- iPhone and Android
- household type
- office and non-office work
- previous and no previous GenAI use

Do not fill the cohort only with founders and technology enthusiasts. Include at least ten people who have never used a generative AI product and at least five who regularly ask a family member for digital help.

#### Demonstration-led launch

Use short real-life demonstrations through:

- trusted Irish creators whose audience is not technology-first
- radio and podcast demonstrations where the host gives Cara a real item
- family and household organisation communities
- QR cards at relevant high-intent moments
- referrals from existing users

The creative test is simple: can a viewer understand what went in, what Cara did and what came out with the sound off?

#### Institutional distribution later

Only after consumer retention is proven, explore:

- banks and insurers
- utility providers
- mobile carriers
- credit unions
- public-service access partners
- employers and unions

These may provide trust and reach, but they can also slow the product and compromise user alignment. Cara must remain accountable to the person, not become an institutional sales wrapper.

## The Facebook-scale interpretation

The platform path should be:

### Stage 1: one assistant

Cara handles a few bounded jobs exceptionally well through camera, sharing, voice and messaging.

### Stage 2: one personal Ground Zero

Cara remembers the person's preferences, people, documents, commitments and prior outcomes with provenance and correction.

### Stage 3: one action network

Third-party services expose safe actions through Cara. The person still speaks to Cara. They do not choose or manage agents.

The public unit is a **job**, not an agent:

- compare this renewal
- book this service
- organise this trip
- prepare this application

### Stage 4: trusted circles

People create deliberate household, caring, project or work circles with explicit boundaries. Cara can coordinate a school notice, shared trip or household task without leaking one person's private context to another.

### Stage 5: ecosystem

Developers and service providers publish capabilities behind standard permission, approval, receipt and quality contracts. Cara owns identity, trust, context, routing and the user relationship.

This is where a platform moat can emerge:

- personal context accumulated over time
- trusted cross-device identity
- verified action history
- workflow evaluation data
- service-provider reliability scores
- household and project collaboration
- distribution through completed work

Do not launch an agent marketplace. A marketplace asks a newcomer to understand the very concept the product is supposed to hide.

## Benchmark translation

### Apple onboarding and privacy

Observed principle:
- get to useful action quickly
- request permissions when the feature needs them
- make privacy a visible product property

Transfer:
- first result before account creation
- calendar access requested only after a calendar event is prepared
- permission ledger in **You**

### WhatsApp linked devices

Observed mechanism:
- device linking is framed as linking a familiar device, commonly through a scan, rather than network configuration

Transfer:
- after the first win, the desktop app shows a QR code
- phone says **Connect this computer**
- user chooses the folders Cara can use
- test request proves the link immediately

### Dropbox and TeamViewer

Observed positioning:
- both lead with familiar outcomes such as finding, sharing, syncing or accessing files, not underlying infrastructure

Transfer:
- say **Find things on this computer when you're away**
- do not say remote execution, local agent or device relay

### Grammarly and Loom

Observed positioning:
- each owns one instantly understood job before expanding into a broader platform

Transfer:
- Cara must own **sort the next step** before promising an operating system for life

### Notion AI, Raycast and Manus

Observed risk:
- current products lead with AI, agents, models, action engines or broad capability language that assumes category familiarity

Break:
- remove category education from the front door
- show a domestic object becoming a completed action

### Lindy

Observed useful pattern:
- a named assistant and concrete claims about removing recurring work are easier to understand than agent infrastructure

Adaptation:
- keep the named relationship but avoid executive-assistant language for mass-market users

## Research and validation plan

### First 30 sessions

Observe in person where possible. Do not teach the interface.

Record:

- time to first input
- whether the person understands what can be submitted
- where they hesitate
- whether they read permission copy
- extraction correctness
- time to action card
- approval comprehension
- task completion
- second-task intention
- actual second task within 24 hours
- spontaneous description of Cara
- spontaneous sharing or referral

Ask after the task:

- What did you think Cara was going to do?
- What surprised you?
- What worried you?
- What would you send her tomorrow?
- What would you never let her touch?
- If Cara disappeared tonight, what would you miss?

Do not ask whether they like AI or whether they would use an agent.

### Fail conditions

Redesign or stop if:

- users need prompt-writing guidance
- more than 30% choose the sample because they cannot identify a personal use
- people interpret a prepared action as already executed
- permission scope is misunderstood
- task completion exceeds three minutes for the core classes
- users praise the demo but do not return with a second item
- referrals come only from technology enthusiasts
- support requires explaining agents, models or integrations

## Product principles

1. **Show the job, never explain the category.**
2. **Real object before blank prompt.**
3. **Value before account.**
4. **Permission after purpose.**
5. **Prepare before acting.**
6. **One task, one state, one clear next step.**
7. **Receipts are part of the product.**
8. **Memory is earned and inspectable.**
9. **Irish in judgement, not decoration.**
10. **The assistant is the interface.**
11. **No silent stalls.**
12. **If it needs a tutorial, it is not simple enough.**

## Recommended immediate decision

Build and test the first-magic mobile web flow before building the broader managed-agent platform.

The first version should do one thing publicly:

> **Give Cara a real piece of life admin. She understands it, prepares the next step and gets it done with your approval in under three minutes.**

If non-technical Irish users repeat that loop voluntarily, the larger platform thesis deserves investment. If they only admire the demonstration, the product is theatre rather than a new mass-market gateway.
