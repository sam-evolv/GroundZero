# Evolv commercial-solar Triple E and ACA decision receipt

## Bounded proposal

Test whether Evolv can add a one-page, source-linked **Triple E and Accelerated Capital Allowance decision receipt** to one current commercial rooftop quotation before equipment is purchased.

For the exact proposed photovoltaic modules and inverters, the receipt would record:

- manufacturer, model and product code from the quotation and datasheets;
- whether the exact code appears on the live SEAI Triple E register, with the result and retrieval date preserved;
- the purchaser, ownership or finance structure, intended trade use and first-use accounting period;
- the applicable `Heating and Electricity Provision` expenditure threshold;
- the NDMG grant amount, reclaimable-VAT treatment and provisional net qualifying-cost basis;
- every unresolved field requiring the customer's accountant, installer, SEAI, Revenue or another qualified party; and
- a clear outcome: `POTENTIALLY ELIGIBLE — PROFESSIONAL CONFIRMATION REQUIRED`, `NOT ESTABLISHED`, or `INELIGIBLE ON RECORDED FACTS`.

This is not tax advice, a tax calculation, a grant or ACA application, an equipment recommendation, or a promise of tax savings. The commercial question is narrower: **does an exact-product and timing receipt expose a material assumption before purchase and improve one buyer decision beyond the ordinary quote?**

If useful, this should become a small appendix to [[briefs/2026-09-23-evolv-commercial-solar-grant-grid-readiness-receipt]], not a separate software product or consultancy lane.

## Why this is genuinely new but bounded

Ground Zero contained no markdown match for `Accelerated Capital Allowance`, `Triple E`, `capital allowance` or `support stacking` when checked on 29 September 2026.

The existing grant-and-grid receipt checks grant timing, connection route and handover evidence. It does not bind the **exact quoted product codes**, purchase or finance structure, first-use accounting period, grant-net cost basis and tax-professional confirmation needed before an owner relies on ACA in an investment case.

The trigger is current official evidence rather than a generic tax idea: Revenue's February 2026 eBrief confirms that Finance Act 2025 extended the section 285A energy-efficient-equipment ACA to 31 December 2030, while SEAI's live Triple E materials explicitly list photovoltaic systems and inverters under the qualifying technology class. This creates a stable enough research window for one manual test, but it does not establish that any current Evolv quote, customer or product qualifies.

## Verified evidence

- Revenue's January 2026 Tax and Duty Manual says section 285A allows 100% of qualifying capital expenditure on eligible energy-efficient equipment to be claimed for the year in which the equipment is first used for the trade. The scheme runs to 31 December 2030.
- The same manual says the claimant can be a company or unincorporated business; the equipment must be new, owned by the business, used for its trade and included on SEAI's published list. Equipment that is leased, let or hired does not qualify.
- Revenue says there is no pre-approval process for ACA: normal self-assessment applies, and the claim is made through the CT1 or Form 11. This makes accountant confirmation and evidence custody more important, not less.
- SEAI's live ACA page says the exact model should be checked on the Triple E register before purchase. It says the equipment must be included on the published list at some stage during the accounting period in which it is first **provided**, while Revenue's January 2026 manual describes the claim period by when the equipment is first **used** for the trade. The receipt must preserve that wording difference and require accountant or Revenue confirmation rather than silently equating the dates.
- SEAI's live categories page places `Photovoltaic Systems` and `Inverters` under `Heating and Electricity Provision`, for which the published minimum expenditure is €1,000.
- SEAI's photovoltaic criteria define eligible PV modules and inverters and require specific technical and documentary evidence. The technology category alone does not make every quoted panel, inverter, battery, mounting component, labour line or system cost eligible.
- Revenue's Finance Act 2025 Part 9 guidance states that capital allowances are generally computed on expenditure net of grants received. Revenue's company guidance also says capital allowances are generally calculated on the asset's net cost. The exact NDMG, VAT and component allocation for a real claim still requires the customer's accountant or Revenue; this note does not infer it.
- NDMG terms prohibit cumulating that grant with another Irish Government or State Agency **grant-aid** scheme. ACA is described by SEAI and Revenue as a tax incentive, but the extracted NDMG documents do not themselves provide a complete ruling on the combined treatment. The receipt must therefore show the interaction as a professional-confirmation field and must not promise “stacked” support.

These sources establish a real eligibility and evidence question. They do not prove that a current Evolv customer pays enough tax to benefit, that the timing improves cash flow materially, that the quote contains listed products, or that a buyer will pay for the receipt.

## Assumptions to falsify

- Evolv has a current, permissioned commercial rooftop opportunity that is still before equipment purchase and whose owner would use the system in a trade.
- The equipment purchaser will own the qualifying equipment rather than lease, let or hire it in a structure that fails the published rule.
- The ordinary quotation does not already provide exact model codes, current Triple E evidence and accountant-ready cost allocation.
- At least one quoted PV module or inverter can be matched unambiguously to a live Triple E entry by exact code, not brand resemblance.
- The relevant category expenditure meets the current threshold after excluding unsupported lines and accounting for grants and reclaimable VAT as professionally confirmed.
- The buyer has taxable trading or professional income and values the timing difference between first-year ACA and ordinary wear-and-tear allowances.
- The receipt can improve a purchase or financing decision without Evolv presenting tax advice or delaying the installation.

## Smallest validation test

Do not open a customer quote, contact an accountant or use client records until Sam approves the exact opportunity and the conflict, confidentiality and IP boundary.

If a suitable current opportunity exists:

1. Freeze the official Revenue and SEAI pages by title, visible date or version, URL and retrieval date. Preserve the live Triple E search result for each exact proposed product code.
2. In an approved client workspace, extract only the minimum fields from the quote and datasheets: controlled site ID, purchaser role, ownership or finance structure, manufacturer, model, product code, component price, grant amount or status, VAT treatment question, purchase date and the expected provided/first-used accounting period. Keep the customer identity, MPRN, full quote and credentials out of Ground Zero.
3. Match every relevant module and inverter to the live Triple E register. Treat missing, expired, ambiguous or near-match codes as `NOT ESTABLISHED`; do not substitute a similar model.
4. Produce a component-level cost bridge from gross quote to the provisional accountant-review basis. Show grants, potentially reclaimable VAT, unsupported equipment, labour and other lines separately; do not calculate a tax saving.
5. Compare the receipt with the ordinary quote and [[briefs/2026-09-23-evolv-commercial-solar-grant-grid-readiness-receipt]]. Record whether it exposed a model, ownership, timing or cost-basis assumption that would otherwise have reached purchase untested.
6. With separate approval, ask the customer's accountant to confirm only the claimant, ownership, trade-use, accounting-period, grant-net, VAT and eligible-cost treatment. Ask the installer or SEAI only about exact product evidence if the live register is ambiguous. Evolv does not make either determination.
7. Return one outcome: **discard**, **retain as a manual appendix**, or **seek approval for one second quote test**. Do not build a calculator, portal or automated tax workflow from one result.

### Pass gate

Retain the manual appendix only if:

- every included product and cost line is tied to an exact source or visibly unresolved;
- a qualified accountant confirms the claim boundary before the buyer relies on it;
- preparation takes no more than 30 additional minutes once the quote and model codes are available;
- the receipt catches at least one material product, ownership, timing or cost-basis assumption, or gives the buyer a documented reason not to rely on ACA; and
- the buyer or delivery lead says it materially improved the real purchase or financing decision.

A polished summary that only repeats public guidance or estimates a headline tax saving does not pass.

### Kill gate

Discard or park the wedge if there is no current pre-purchase opportunity, the installer and accountant already provide equivalent exact-product evidence, the quote lacks stable model codes, no material eligibility uncertainty exists, professional review costs more than the decision value, the buyer has no relevant taxable trade income, or the receipt creates pressure to market tax savings before qualification.

## Downside and safeguards

- **Tax-advice risk:** ACA is self-assessed and fact-specific. Evolv can assemble evidence but must not determine tax eligibility or savings.
- **Register drift:** a technology category is not an exact-product match. Preserve product codes, validity dates, URLs and retrieval dates; re-check at the professionally confirmed claim point.
- **Finance-structure risk:** an NDMG-compatible payment or financing arrangement does not by itself prove the ACA ownership rule is met.
- **Grant and VAT risk:** qualifying expenditure can differ from the contract total. Keep grant, VAT, labour and non-listed components separate and obtain accountant confirmation.
- **Timing risk:** the reviewed official pages use both “first provided” and “first used” wording for the accounting-period boundary. Do not reconcile that difference locally or accelerate installation or purchasing for a presumed tax outcome; obtain accountant or Revenue confirmation.
- **Benefit overstatement:** ACA accelerates an allowance that ordinary plant-and-machinery rules may otherwise spread over eight years; it is not automatically an additional deduction or cash grant.
- **Commercial sensitivity:** quotations, financing terms, tax status and product costs are confidential. Minimise fields, use an approved workspace and keep client records out of Ground Zero.
- **Priority drift:** this appendix remains subordinate to the real rooftop sale, the paid 60–90 day owner-side baseline in [[project_state/renew]] and [[items/renew-reporting-source-baseline]].
