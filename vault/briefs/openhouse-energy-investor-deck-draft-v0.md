## Slide 2 - The category is changing

### Headline
**Handover software solves paperwork. OpenHouse solves living with the home.**

### Body copy
Modern homes are no longer simple physical assets. They are becoming connected energy systems with heat pumps, solar, EV charging, ventilation, smart meters, tariffs, and complex warranties.

The current handover model still treats this as a folder of documents.

### Three points

## Slide 3 - The problem

### Headline
**A high-performance home still needs a high-context operator.**

### Body copy
Ireland is moving toward better-rated, more energy-efficient housing, but many occupants are still using new homes with old habits.

An A-rated home can underperform if the person living in it does not understand how to use the systems inside it.

### Examples
- Opening windows to cool a tightly insulated home while heating is active.
- Charging an EV at the wrong tariff window.
- Misunderstanding heat-pump behaviour and raising aftercare issues that are really education gaps.
- Failing to use solar generation when it is most valuable.

### Visual direction
A homeowner inside a modern home surrounded by floating labels: heat pump, tariff, MVHR, solar, bill, warranty, EV charger, smart meter.

## Slide 7 - The product loop

### Headline
**Diagnose. Educate. Remember. Intervene. Escalate.**

### Body copy
OpenHouse is not just a dashboard. It is a feedback loop between the home, the homeowner, and the developer.

### Loop
1. **Diagnose** - what is happening in the home?
2. **Educate** - why does it matter?
3. **Remember** - what is normal for this household?
4. **Intervene** - what should change now?
5. **Escalate** - who needs to act if it is not behavioural?

### Example
A homeowner asks why the bill is high. OpenHouse compares the bill, tariff, recent usage, EV sessions, heat-pump behaviour, solar output, and household history, then explains the likely causes and actions.

### Visual direction
Circular loop around a central home energy model.

## Slide 8 - The language layer

### Headline
**Every answer is explained in money, comfort, and risk.**

### Body copy
Energy data is only useful if people can understand what it means.

OpenHouse translates each issue into the three dimensions that matter.

### Cards
**Money**  
What is this costing or saving?

**Comfort**  
How does it affect the lived experience?

**Risk**  
Is this normal, wasteful, or a sign of a deeper issue?

### Example answer
"Your heat pump used more energy than expected this week. The likely cost impact is about €X. Comfort may drop if the schedule keeps fighting outdoor temperature. If this continues, it may indicate a settings or commissioning issue."

## Slide 13 - Competition

### Headline
**Energy tools monitor. OpenHouse understands.**

### Body copy
Existing products are useful, but fragmented.

Smart meters show what happened. Solar portals show one device ecosystem. Smart-home dashboards require technical setup. Aftercare tools manage tickets. None of them combine build context, household memory, diagnostics, education, and developer reporting.

### Comparison table
| Category | What it sees | What it misses |
|---|---|---|
| Smart meter apps | usage and bills | home fabric, devices, behaviour, action |
| Solar portals | solar hardware | whole-home context and developer view |
| Smart home dashboards | device telemetry | homeowner education and handover proof |
| Aftercare portals | issues and messages | energy diagnosis and system context |
| OpenHouse | home, devices, documents, behaviour, issues | built to become the full context layer |

### Body copy
The first commercial motion stays focused: developers pay for better handover, aftercare, buyer education, and evidence.

Energy intelligence expands the value of that same relationship.

### Revenue layers

2. Energy intelligence add-on for low-carbon home handover.
3. Premium homeowner energy support where appropriate.
4. Aggregated, anonymised scheme reporting for ESG, finance, and performance evidence.

## Appendix B - First 10 questions the product must answer

1. Why was my electricity bill high this month?
2. Is my heat pump running efficiently?
3. Should I charge my EV tonight or later?
4. Did my solar panels save me money this month?

6. What changed compared with last month?
7. Is this a behaviour issue or a fault?
