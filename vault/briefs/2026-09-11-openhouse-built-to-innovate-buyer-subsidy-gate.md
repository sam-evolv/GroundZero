# OpenHouse Built to Innovate buyer-subsidy gate

## Bounded proposal

Test whether one eligible Irish residential builder-developer can frame a paid OpenHouse discovery or pilot as a developer-owned digital productivity project under Enterprise Ireland's live **Built to Innovate** programme.

This is a purchase-friction test inside [[items/oh-developer-outreach-proposal-pack]], not a claim that OpenHouse is grant-approved, not a funding application by OpenHouse, and not a reason to broaden the product. The developer must remain the applicant and buyer. OpenHouse would be a possible external provider only if Enterprise Ireland confirms that the current support, activity and provider are eligible before work starts.

## Evidence

Enterprise Ireland's current Built to Innovate page says the programme supports Irish residential construction companies with funding and hands-on support to improve delivery, productivity and quality. It explicitly includes digital tools such as tracking systems and data dashboards, plus pilot projects and workflows. The current page lists three buyer-level eligibility conditions:

- Irish-registered company with at least 10 full-time employees;
- active in construction services or building-product manufacturing;
- at least 20% of revenue from residential construction.

This aligns with OpenHouse's developer handover and aftercare workflows, but alignment is not approval.

An official Digital Discovery reference document provides a useful historical mechanism: external consultancy for discovery, process analysis, data use, digital opportunities and a roadmap. It says installation or configuration of off-the-shelf software, subscriptions and work started before approval are ineligible. It also says the applicant selects the provider and Enterprise Ireland may reject the provider or project.

That document cannot establish the current commercial offer. It is revised from 2022, its Housing for All appendix names a closing date at the end of 2024, and its main grant cap is €5,000 while the appendix separately discusses introductory projects of €10,000 grant aid or less. The live 2026 Built to Innovate page confirms the programme continues, but does not publish a current grant rate, cap, application instrument or vendor-cost rule. These details must be confirmed directly before they enter any buyer message or proposal.

## Smallest validation test

Without contacting anyone, prepare one optional, clearly labelled funding-alignment appendix for a currently qualified developer account:

1. Map one existing handover or aftercare process into `current workflow`, `cost or delay`, `OpenHouse-assisted change`, `measurable housing impact`, and `30-day evidence`.
2. Limit the proposed first phase to discovery and one permissioned workflow proof. Do not include production rollout, subscriptions, migrations or unsupported savings.
3. Add a buyer eligibility checklist using only the three conditions on the current Built to Innovate page. Mark every unverified field `UNKNOWN`.
4. Add four questions for the developer's Enterprise Ireland adviser: current support instrument, eligible activity, eligible provider/cost, and whether approval must precede procurement or work.
5. Compare the appendix against the normal paid-pilot ask. Use it only if it makes the business case clearer without implying a subsidy.

### Pass criteria

- The target developer can be supported by public evidence as Irish residential construction, with employee count and residential-revenue eligibility either verified or explicitly unknown.
- The workflow has one measurable productivity or quality baseline, such as handover preparation time, unresolved evidence items, homeowner query handling time or repeat aftercare routing.
- The appendix can be reviewed in under five minutes and contains no grant amount, approval, reimbursement or eligibility promise.
- A developer could take the four questions to its own adviser without OpenHouse submitting or representing the application.
- The core paid-pilot case still stands if no support is available.

If the developer is ineligible, the adviser says OpenHouse or the activity is ineligible, or the grant process delays a direct paid decision, remove the appendix and keep the normal commercial path. Do not turn grant availability into a substitute for buyer willingness to pay.

## Assumptions to falsify

- At least one qualified developer in the current pipeline meets the live programme's buyer criteria.
- A bounded OpenHouse discovery or pilot can produce a demonstrable homebuilding productivity or quality outcome rather than functioning as general software procurement.
- The programme currently permits the relevant activity and an external provider like OpenHouse.
- Funding alignment shortens or de-risks the buyer decision instead of adding delay and administrative friction.
- The developer will still own and value the outcome without a subsidy.

## Downside

- The historical Digital Discovery document is stale and internally unsuitable as a current pricing source.
- A property developer that does not self-perform construction was excluded by the historical appendix; the live page is less specific. Buyer eligibility can therefore fail even when a company builds homes.
- Grant-led framing can weaken the direct commercial signal Enterprise Ireland previously told Sam he needs.
- Application work can consume scarce founder time, delay a purchase and distract from current security and production gates.
- The project may be treated as off-the-shelf software or routine consultancy rather than eligible process innovation.
- Mentioning funding without adviser confirmation could create a misleading expectation or undermine trust.

## Approval boundary

This note authorises research and preparation of one unsent funding-alignment appendix only. It does not authorise:

- contacting Enterprise Ireland, developers, advisers or intermediaries;
- stating that a developer, OpenHouse, a provider, activity or cost is eligible;
- quoting a grant amount or reimbursement rate as current;
- submitting an application, accepting aid, signing a provider agreement or starting funded work;
- changing production, handling customer data, spending money or altering the paid-pilot price;
- sending or attaching the appendix to any external message.

Sam must approve the exact developer, wording and external contact. The developer and its Enterprise Ireland adviser must confirm current eligibility and approval timing before any work is presented as grant-supported.
