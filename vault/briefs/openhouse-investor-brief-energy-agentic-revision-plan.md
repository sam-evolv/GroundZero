# OpenHouse Investor Brief — Energy + Agentic Revision Plan

This note reviews the current 22-page `OpenHouse — Investor Brief copy.pdf` and identifies the changes needed to absorb the newer OpenHouse strategy: construction-stage home intelligence, agentic energy management, B2C/UK expansion, pricing, and data ownership.

The current deck is already directionally strong: it has moved from handover software toward home energy intelligence. The next version should make the commercial and strategic leap clearer: **OpenHouse is installed at the point of construction to create a living home model, then expands into energy intelligence, subscriptions, agentic control, and UK-scale deployment.**

## Current Deck Read

### What is already working

- The deck already avoids the “chatbot” trap.
- It frames OpenHouse as an intelligence layer for modern homes.
- It has the right core problem: a high-performance home needs a high-context operator.
- It has the right category shift: handover software solves paperwork; OpenHouse solves living with the home.
- It introduces the home model, homeowner education, money/comfort/risk, developer visibility, and the developer-provisioned new-build wedge.
- It already has an expansion path from new builds to retrofit homes to estate/grid network.

### What is missing or underweighted

1. **The construction-stage insight is not yet sharp enough.**
   The strongest line should become: _the best time to make a home intelligent is while it is being built._

2. **Agentic control is not yet explicit enough.**
   The deck should show the path from advisory assistant → assisted control → fully agentic home energy manager.

3. **The pricing model needs to be more realistic and concrete.**
   Current new thinking: €99/home developer provisioning, €9.99/month consumer energy intelligence, higher agentic tiers later.

4. **Data ownership needs its own slide.**
   Investors and developers will ask this early. The deck needs a crisp trust model.

5. **Ireland vs UK scale needs to be clearer.**
   Ireland is the proof market. UK expansion is where the venture-scale ceiling appears.

6. **B2C should be positioned as expansion, not first wedge.**
   Direct-to-consumer can work, but the highest-accuracy product comes from developer-provisioned homes.

7. **The “smart meter context layer” point needs adding.**
   Smart meters show what happened. OpenHouse explains why and what to do next.

## Slide 6 — What OpenHouse knows

### Current direction
Context generic energy tools never see: build spec, devices, usage history, household memory, portfolio intelligence.

### Add stronger “source of truth” hierarchy
Split into four layers:

1. **Build fabric** — walls, windows, insulation, underfloor, airtightness, BER, materials.
2. **Installed systems** — heat pump, MVHR, solar, EV charger, battery, smart meter.
3. **Documents and obligations** — warranties, manuals, commissioning, installer records, handover docs.
4. **Living behaviour** — preferences, routines, questions, maintenance, prior issues, energy patterns.

Suggested line:
> **Generic energy tools see usage. OpenHouse sees the home that created the usage.**

## Slide 7 — Product loop

### Current direction
Diagnose → educate → remember → intervene → escalate.

### Keep, but add “act” ladder
Add autonomy ladder beside the loop:

1. **Advise** — explain what is happening.
2. **Recommend** — suggest the best action.
3. **Ask approval** — “I can change this for you.”
4. **Act within limits** — optimise under user-defined rules.

Suggested line:
> **OpenHouse starts as guidance, then becomes an agentic energy manager as trust and integrations grow.**

## New Slide after Product Loop — Path to Agentic Energy Management

This should be added.

### Headline
**From advice to autonomous optimisation.**

### Three stages

#### 1. Advisory intelligence
- “Your EV is charging during peak tariff.”
- “Your heat pump is working harder than expected.”
- “Your solar generation is lower than similar homes.”

#### 2. Assisted control
- “I can move tonight’s EV charge to the cheaper window. Approve?”
- “I can lower heating by 1°C while the house is empty. Approve?”

#### 3. Agentic home energy manager
- Optimises heat pump, EV charging, solar, battery, hot water and ventilation within homeowner rules.
- Protects comfort boundaries.
- Minimises cost and carbon where possible.

### Guardrail line
> **Control only happens with consent, rules and override. Trust is the product.**

Do not rebuild the whole deck. The current deck is directionally right and visually strong. Instead, revise it with a sharper spine:
