# Ireland-first robotics company opportunity analysis

## Founder constraint

This venture must begin as a nights-and-weekends experiment with limited founder capital. It must not require either founder to resign, design a novel robot from scratch, obtain immediate GMP production access, or carry a large fleet before a customer has paid.

### Current unfair advantages

- Sam: property-development operations, homeowner/aftercare workflow knowledge, AI/software/product execution, evidence and data systems, founder-led commercial strategy.
- Brother: engineer at Pfizer, practical regulated-manufacturing context and a network across pharma and adjacent companies.
- Geography: Cork and the wider South-West industrial/pharma cluster.
- Team shape: one physical-world engineer plus one software/AI/operator founder.

### Important unknown

The brother's exact engineering discipline, validation authority, available time and employer conflict/IP boundaries are not yet recorded. No proposed wedge should depend on unsupported assumptions about those facts.

## Long-term ambition

Build a robot-as-a-service operating company that can eventually deploy and manage fleets across factory, warehouse and other physical work. Humanoids are a later hardware option, not the initial business model.

## Research question

Which Irish physical workflow has:

1. expensive, recurring labour or access pain;
2. a structured enough environment for reliable automation;
3. measurable safety, downtime, quality or compliance value;
4. a reachable buyer through the founders' real networks;
5. a service-first entry requiring modest capital;
6. a path from one deployed machine to recurring fleet/software revenue;
7. limited direct competition in Ireland;
8. a realistic paid proof inside 90 days?

## Prior thesis to retest

Previous research ranked **serviced robotics for no-man-entry maintenance in regulated Irish industry** above generic warehouse robotics, façade cleaning and solar cleaning.

The core reason was not market excitement. It was founder-market access, regulated operational pain, service-first feasibility and the possibility of converting compliance and deployment know-how into a moat.

Previous vendor discovery identified:

- Pharma/confined space: SwabBot, WEDA Robotics and KOKS.
- Solar: SolarCleano, Airtouch and Ecoppia.
- Façade: Skyline Robotics, KITE Robotics, Lucid Bots, Verobotics and hyCLEANER.
- Sewer: Envirosight and IBAK.
- Wind: Aerones, BladeBUG, Rope Robotics, Perceptual Robotics, Windbotix and TSR Wind.

These names demonstrate category existence, not Irish demand or investable opportunity. Current vendor, price and availability evidence must be reverified.

## Evaluation scorecard

Every option will be scored on:

- pain and measurable economic consequence;
- founder access and credibility;
- speed to a paid pilot;
- capital required before payment;
- regulatory and insurance burden;
- reliability requirement;
- Irish serviceable market;
- recurring-revenue potential;
- ability to start with imported/OEM hardware;
- defensibility through workflow data, SOPs, reporting and remote operations;
- expansion into adjacent tasks, sectors and eventually general-purpose robots.

## Options under live research

1. Pharma/biopharma no-man-entry inspection, cleaning and evidence capture.
2. Pharma/industrial wastewater, drain and utility inspection.
3. Utility-scale solar cleaning-as-a-service.
4. Robotic façade and exterior-building cleaning.
5. Sewer/stormwater inspection and maintenance.
6. Warehouse and factory material movement or inventory inspection.
7. Commercial autonomous floor cleaning.
8. Wind-turbine inspection and maintenance.
9. Agriculture and construction/property inspection.
10. Laboratory and hospital automation.

## Decision standard

There is no "no-brainer" until buyers confirm the workflow, baseline cost and authority to run a trial. A category can be technologically inevitable and still be the wrong first business.

The recommended starting point must survive a payment gate. Founder enthusiasm, trade-fair demos, vendor claims and free pilot interest do not count as demand.
