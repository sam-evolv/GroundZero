# YC companies 2021–2026: Ireland founder-fit opportunity screen

## Decision in one minute

### Best opportunity overall

The strongest YC-derived opportunity for Sam is not a new company. It is a sharper version of **OpenHouse's existing B2B direction**:

> An operating system for homebuilders from buyer handover through warranty, defects, contractor coordination and verified resolution.

This combines the strongest elements of:

- [Foundation](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/foundation-2), “Shopify for Homebuilders”;
- [zudo.work](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/zudowork), AI workers for warranty workflows;
- [CentralComs](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/centralcoms), AI agents for property-management communication;
- [Opusense AI](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/opusense-ai), AI-generated field-inspection reports.

It matches Sam’s property-development experience, OpenHouse product, warm access and authentic production workflow. Starting another company to pursue it would create needless fragmentation.

### Best genuinely new venture

If Sam deliberately wants a separate new venture, the best candidate is:

> **Applicant-side AI planning and development assurance for Irish housing and renewable projects.**

It would combine the models of [Permitify](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/permitify), [PermitPortal](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/permitportal), [Spark](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/spark) and selected drawing-review capabilities from [Structured AI](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/structured-ai).

The initial product should not decide whether a project complies or replace an architect, planner or engineer. It should create a source-cited owner-side risk and consistency review before land acquisition or submission.

### Candid recommendation

Do not launch a fourth product now.

Use this screen to make one of two choices:

1. strengthen OpenHouse around builder warranty and aftercare; or
2. run one bounded, paid planning-assurance experiment using public documents only.

Do not build both.

## Scope and methodology

### Directory screen

A live snapshot of YC’s company directory was acquired on 4 August 2026 through YC’s public company endpoint.

- Total directory companies retrieved: **6,123**
- Approximate five-year batch pool, S21 through S26/P26/F26: **3,306**
- Marked active by YC: **2,903**
- Marked active with a listed team size of 40 or fewer: **2,671**
- Marked active with a listed team size of 25 or fewer: **2,544**
- Active companies without a listed team size: **56**

“Not yet big” was operationally defined as active with a YC-listed team size of 40 or fewer. Team size is an imperfect directory field, not an audited measure of revenue, funding or market power.

### Screening stages

Every company in the batch pool was mechanically screened. The following were deprioritised:

- biotechnology and clinical healthcare;
- defence, aerospace, semiconductors and deep hardware;
- consumer social and geography-specific emerging-market models;
- heavily regulated lending, insurance underwriting and financial infrastructure;
- generic AI developer tools and horizontal copilots;
- ideas requiring specialist scientific credentials or major capital;
- models already visibly established in Ireland;
- products with no credible founder-market fit for Sam.

The remaining real-world operations, property, construction, energy, field-work, compliance and service-business companies were manually reviewed. The strongest clusters were then checked against Irish official sources and visible Irish competitors.

### Ireland-presence standard

“No Irish business” cannot be proven from web research alone.

For shortlisted companies, no public Irish office, Irish dataset, named Irish customer or Irish case study was found on the current YC profile and company website unless otherwise stated. That means **no visible Irish presence found**, not verified territorial unavailability. A company may sell remotely or have undisclosed customers.

## Ranked opportunities

## 1. Homebuilder buyer, warranty and aftercare OS

**YC analogues:** Foundation, zudo.work, CentralComs, Opusense AI

**Founder fit:** 9.5/10
**Irish need:** 8.5/10
**Competitive whitespace:** 6.5/10
**Low-capital validation:** 9/10
**Verdict:** Best opportunity overall, but it is OpenHouse rather than a new company.

### Exceptional idea

Foundation positions itself as “Shopify for Homebuilders.” zudo.work automates customer support, authorisation and claims workflows for home and auto warranty administrators. CentralComs automates property-management communications. Opusense converts field observations into inspection reports.

The Irish version should connect:

- homeowner onboarding and handover;
- home documentation and warranties;
- defect intake by text, voice, photograph and video;
- triage and responsibility assignment;
- contractor scheduling and evidence;
- SLA and warranty deadlines;
- repair completion;
- homeowner confirmation;
- recurring defect intelligence across house types, contractors and components.

### Evidence of market

The [CSO recorded 36,284 new dwelling completions in 2025](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-ndc/newdwellingcompletionsq42025/), including 18,308 scheme dwellings and 12,047 apartments. Scheme homes and apartments represented 83.7% of completions.

This does not prove willingness to pay for OpenHouse, but it proves a recurring flow of multi-unit homes requiring handover, documentation and aftercare.

### Competition and gap

Ireland is not empty:

- [Zutec](https://zutec.com/) provides construction documentation, quality assurance, digital handover, O&M manuals and building information management.
- [PlanRadar](https://www.planradar.com/gb/) provides inspections, handovers, claims evidence and maintenance workflows.
- Developers use CRMs, ticketing tools, document stores and contractors.

The defensible wedge is therefore not “digital handover.” It is the homeowner-to-developer operating loop and the verified closure of post-handover obligations.

### Recommendation

Treat the YC findings as validation and product inspiration for OpenHouse. Do not form a second company around the same workflow.

## 2. Applicant-side planning and development assurance

**YC analogues:** Permitify, PermitPortal, Spark, Structured AI, InspectMind AI

**Founder fit:** 9/10
**Irish need:** 8/10
**Competitive whitespace:** 7/10
**Low-capital validation:** 9/10
**Verdict:** Best genuinely new venture.

### Exceptional idea

Permitify’s current product reviews plan sets against applicable requirements, returns cited findings in one to three hours and advertises a $297 one-off review. PermitPortal advertises a professional-engineer-reviewed site-risk brief within 48 hours. Spark creates permitting, zoning, sentiment and site-risk intelligence for energy infrastructure. Structured AI and [InspectMind AI](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/inspectmind-ai) review drawings for coordination or design issues. These are vendor descriptions and prices, not Irish willingness-to-pay evidence.

The Irish product should be owner-side, not council-side. The clearest visible whitespace is not another planning database. It is a document preflight that links every finding to the relevant drawing, condition, policy or requirement:

> A source-cited development assurance brief that identifies planning, document, condition and programme risks before an owner commits land value or submits an application.

### Initial workflow

Ingest only public and customer-authorised material:

- local development plans and zoning;
- planning history and nearby decisions;
- appeal outcomes;
- site notices and application checklists;
- planning statements;
- drawings, schedules and revisions;
- environmental and infrastructure reports;
- conditions and commitments.

Produce:

- site and planning-history chronology;
- policy and zoning references;
- comparable refusal and appeal patterns;
- document-completeness checklist;
- contradictions across statements, schedules and drawings;
- unaddressed conditions or commitments;
- questions for the professional team;
- owner-side programme, cost and residual-land-value risk register.

Every finding must cite its source. A qualified planner, architect, engineer or solicitor remains responsible for professional conclusions.

### Evidence of market

The [Office of the Planning Regulator’s Planning in Numbers 2024](https://www.opr.ie/planning-in-numbers-2024/) reports:

- 32,491 residential units received permission in 2024;
- 5.9% of local-authority decisions were appealed;
- 24% of appealed decisions were reversed;
- appeal rates varied from 1.8% to 14.6% across authorities;
- several key application datasets were unavailable, limiting understanding of system performance.

These figures establish repeated, valuable decisions and fragmented evidence. They do not by themselves prove demand for AI review.

### Visible competition

- [Planning.localgov.ie](https://planning.localgov.ie/) supports national electronic planning applications.
- [EirePlan](https://eireplan.ie/) says it indexes more than 585,000 Irish planning decisions and provides policy-grounded AI and submission workspaces.
- [Planner.ie](https://planner.ie/) advertises a waitlisted €74.99 planning-outcome report.
- [PlanningCheck.ie](https://www.planningcheck.ie/) provides exemption and pre-planning screening.
- [Building Regulations Copilot](https://buildingregulationscopilot.com/) provides a free Irish building-regulations question-and-answer assistant.
- Planning consultants, architects, engineers and solicitors already perform professional review.
- Building-control administration is handled through [BCMS](https://www.nbco.localgov.ie/).

Therefore, do not build another planning search engine or submission portal. The wedge must convert evidence into an owner’s investment and programme decision.

### First paid validation

Sell five manual, asynchronous reviews before developing software.

Suggested test:

- Buyer: small or mid-sized developer, renewable developer, land investor or owner’s representative.
- Input: one proposed site plus a defined document set.
- Output: a cited risk and inconsistency brief within five working days.
- Hypothetical test price: €750 to €2,000, depending on scope. This is a validation price, not market evidence.
- Pass condition: at least three paid reviews and one repeat or portfolio request.
- Fail condition: buyers say existing consultants already provide the same output and will not pay separately.

### Principal risk

Sam must resolve employment, confidentiality, invention-assignment and conflict issues before using Longview, Evolv or customer material. Public planning records can support the first experiment without employer data.

## 3. Renewable development permitting and grid-risk intelligence

**YC analogues:** Spark, [Nira Energy](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/nira-energy), [Plume](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/plume)

**Founder fit:** 8.5/10
**Irish need:** 8/10
**Competitive whitespace:** 6.5/10
**Low-capital validation:** 8/10
**Verdict:** Strong vertical, but narrower and more data-dependent than housing planning assurance.

### Exceptional idea

Spark monitors local permitting and site risk. Nira helps developers identify favourable grid locations. Plume combines renewable project data for site discovery and development.

An Irish managed service could reconcile:

- land and planning constraints;
- grid connection information;
- EirGrid constraint forecasts;
- historical planning and appeal outcomes;
- environmental designations;
- indicative dispatch-down risk;
- project documentation and decision deadlines.

### Why it is credible

EirGrid publishes constraint forecasts and location-sensitive system information. Its [May 2026 ECP-GSS-1 batch](https://cms.eirgrid.ie/sites/default/files/publications/Enduring-Connection-Policy-GSS-1-Final-Batch-May-2026.docx) contained 53 applications totalling 5,640.3 MW, including 2,717.2 MW of solar, 1,147 MW of standalone batteries and 860.1 MW of wind. This is one batch, not the complete national pipeline. The previously reviewed official 2025 report also recorded substantial dispatch-down for wind and solar. Grid and planning risk can materially affect project value.

[Spark](https://www.sparkhq.ai/) says its US-focused platform monitors more than 3,000 counties and serves customers representing over 90 GW of pipeline. [Plume](https://www.plumefinder.com/) says it combines more than 250 data sources and operates in four countries, but it does not identify Ireland. These are company claims. No Irish dataset, customer or office was confirmed for either company.

### Why it is not first

The number of serious Irish renewable developers is limited. They already use grid consultants, planners, GIS teams and engineering firms. High-value data may be incomplete or require expert interpretation. A generic map or copilot will not displace those relationships.

This is best tested as a paid project-development risk brief, not a platform. The brief should identify the site, planning history, relevant policies, environmental designations, nearest public grid evidence, nearby ECP projects, documented constraint warnings, previous decisions, community concerns and questions requiring the client’s planner or grid engineer. It must not present public evidence as engineering, legal or connection advice.

## 4. Construction evidence and drawing-quality agent

**YC analogues:** Opusense AI, Structured AI, InspectMind AI, [Karmen](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/karmen)

**Founder fit:** 8/10
**Irish need:** 7.5/10
**Competitive whitespace:** 5/10
**Low-capital validation:** 7/10
**Verdict:** Real workflow, crowded category. Only pursue a narrow repeated-housing wedge.

### Potential Irish wedge

- Convert site voice notes, photographs and videos into structured daily or inspection reports.
- Compare repeated house-type drawings, room schedules, specifications and revisions.
- Identify missing evidence for quality, handover or defect closure.
- Maintain traceable issue ownership and close-out evidence.

### Existing Irish and international competition

- [Boxcore](https://www.boxcore.com/) provides Irish construction safety, workforce, document, inspection and audit workflows.
- [GoContractor](https://gocontractor.com/) provides contractor onboarding, document and safety compliance.
- Zutec covers site diaries, QA, handover and building records.
- PlanRadar covers inspections, reporting, evidence and claims.

A broad construction compliance platform is not whitespace. A repeated-house-type inconsistency checker or developer-side evidence QA service may be.

### Better use

This functionality is more valuable as an OpenHouse developer module than as another standalone startup.

## 5. AI-native owners’ management company operator

**YC analogues:** [Assembly HOA](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/assembly-hoa), [Aldara](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/aldara)

**Founder fit:** 7.5/10
**Irish need:** 9/10
**Competitive whitespace:** 6/10
**Low-capital validation:** 5/10
**Verdict:** Strong need and recurring revenue, but poor initial lifestyle and operational fit.

### Exceptional idea

Do not merely sell software to property managers. Operate the management company with a lower administrative cost base and better governance:

- service-charge budgeting and collection;
- resident and director communication;
- contractor procurement;
- maintenance evidence;
- insurance and fire-safety records;
- sinking-fund plans;
- AGM and annual-report packs;
- issue triage and transparent close-out.

### Evidence of Irish need

The [SCSI’s 2024 apartment-maintenance report](https://scsi.ie/press-release-scsi-launches-real-cost-of-apartment-block-maintenance-report/) found:

- 88% of surveyed property managers managed MUDs without adequate sinking funds;
- up to 9% managed MUDs with no sinking fund;
- typical contributions were €200 to €300 per unit annually;
- case studies indicated roughly €2,000 per unit annually was required;
- the survey represented 495 MUDs and about 38,000 apartments.

The [Housing Agency’s OMC report](https://www.housingagency.ie/publications/owners-management-companies-sustainable-apartment-living-for-ireland/) also identifies service-charge collection, sinking funds, volunteer directors, reporting, communication and regulation as persistent issues.

### Why not recommend it first

- Property management is licensed and regulated.
- Resident complaints and urgent maintenance are daytime and out-of-hours operations.
- The company must manage client money, contractors, insurance, arrears and governance.
- Established Irish managing agents and MRI software already serve the market.

This can become a good operating company, but it conflicts with Sam’s preference for discreet, after-hours validation and low operational burden.

## 6. Field-to-report intelligence for renewables and infrastructure

**YC analogues:** [Rimba](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/rimba), Opusense AI

**Founder fit:** 8.5/10
**Irish need:** 7.5/10
**Competitive whitespace:** 5.5/10
**Low-capital validation:** 9/10
**Verdict:** Useful module within the renewable owner-side assurance proposition, not a separate company.

Rimba turns fragmented field data into reports for heavy industry. Opusense turns inspection observations into structured output.

The Irish renewable version would convert alarms, photographs, contractor notes, invoices and plant data into traceable event, work-order, evidence and recovery records.

That directly supports the previously recommended renewable revenue and claims service. It does not justify launching another generic reporting copilot.

## 7. Construction finance and material procurement automation

**YC analogues:** [inBuild](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/inbuild), [DigiBuild](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/digibuild), [Concord Materials](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/concord-materials)

**Founder fit:** 7/10
**Irish need:** 7/10
**Competitive whitespace:** 5/10
**Low-capital validation:** 6/10
**Verdict:** Valuable but integration-heavy, margin-sensitive and operationally demanding.

The appealing wedge is invoice-to-budget reconciliation for housebuilders:

- extract subcontractor invoices;
- compare them with purchase orders, budget lines and progress;
- identify duplicates, variation leakage and missing approvals;
- prepare the RCT and payment workflow for human approval.

Ireland’s [Relevant Contracts Tax](https://www.revenue.ie/en/self-assessment-and-self-employment/rct/index.aspx) creates specific compliance steps for principal contractors. But accounting integrations, payment risk, supplier relationships and ERP incumbents make this a difficult side venture.

Coperniq provides CRM, proposals, design, project management, service and finance workflows for solar and energy contractors.

## Companies and models rejected after review

### Public-sector permitting software

[Verdant](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/verdant) sells AI-native land-use permit management to local government. Ireland already has national planning and BCMS infrastructure. Public-sector sales are slow, procurement-heavy and inconsistent with Sam’s preferred validation path.

### Broad subcontractor compliance

[Dili](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/dili) now focuses on US prevailing-wage, apprenticeship and certified-payroll compliance. A literal Irish copy would not translate because the underlying federal rules are different. Boxcore and GoContractor already visibly cover contractor records, documents, onboarding, inspections and audit readiness. A broad Irish construction-compliance clone lacks whitespace.

### Generic construction contract management

[Provision](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/provision) applies AI to construction contracts and risk. Irish tender products including [Ailtir](https://ailtir.ai/), [BidManager](https://bidmanager.ie/) and [Tendr](https://www.tendr.ie/) already address requirement extraction, bid production or response workflow. The remaining owner-side obligation-review wedge is narrower, while carrying legal-reliability and enterprise-sales burdens.

### Generic infrastructure project management

[Clad](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/clad) and established products such as Cora Systems address infrastructure project delivery. Broad project management is not a credible entry point.

### Full-service renovation owner’s representative

[WeReno](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/wereno) is an AI-supported owner’s representative for commercial construction and renovation. It may work as a local service, but it is meeting-heavy, geographically constrained and dependent on daytime project operations.

## Final recommendation

### If optimising for expected founder outcome

Focus on OpenHouse.

Use the YC patterns to sharpen the B2B product into:

> The developer operating system for handover, warranty, defects and verified homeowner resolution.

The immediate buyer is a residential developer. The initial economic claim is lower aftercare administration, faster defect closure, better contractor accountability and fewer unresolved homeowner escalations.

### If testing one new opportunity

Run a **planning and development assurance audit** for one public or customer-authorised site.

Do not build a planning platform. Produce the brief manually, price it, see whether an owner values it and obtain one repeat request.

### Decision gate

Proceed only if all of the following occur:

- one buyer pays for the first audit;
- the buyer grants lawful access to the required documents;
- the review identifies information or inconsistencies the existing workflow did not surface;
- the buyer asks for another site or continuing monitoring;
- employment, confidentiality, IP and conflict boundaries are clear.

Otherwise, stop and return focus to OpenHouse.
