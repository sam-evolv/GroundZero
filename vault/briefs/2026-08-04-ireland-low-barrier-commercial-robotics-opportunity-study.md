# Ireland low-barrier commercial robotics opportunity study

**Date:** 4 August 2026  
**Decision:** Which off-the-shelf commercial robot service can two part-time founders launch in Ireland with limited capital, earn recurring revenue from, and later expand into a managed fleet?

## Executive answer

The best starting point is **managed autonomous mowing on controlled private sites**.

The second-best commercial problem is **floor cleaning**, but it is not the best first market for these founders because credible Irish suppliers already have machines, local support and named customer case studies.

The strongest combined sports proposition is **mowing plus line marking**, but the two machines should not be bought together. Start with mowing. Add a line marker only after a route of paying clubs, schools, universities or grounds contractors can support a €20,050 ex-VAT machine.

The best additional low-barrier categories are:

1. Remote-controlled slope and embankment mowing as an operator-led service.
2. Mobile pallet wrapping for warehouses and manufacturers.
3. Golf driving-range ball collection.
4. Farm feed-pushing or manure-scraping robots, if a dealer partnership can be secured.
5. Outdoor yard and car-park sweeping.

Solar-panel cleaning remains a **targeted per-job service**, not a general national subscription thesis. Ireland has a growing solar asset base, but rainfall and low measured soiling on many sites can make routine cleaning uneconomic.

Commercial window and façade cleaning is real pain, but the machines that materially change access economics are not genuinely low-barrier. A Lucid Sherpa cleaning drone starts at US$45,750 or US$2,950 per month, needs a two-person crew and Irish aviation compliance. Suction robots still require roof access, tethering, exclusion zones and rescue planning. This should be a later operating line, not the first purchase.

## Recommended order

### 1. Launch test: managed autonomous mowing

Target bounded private sites where the customer already pays at least approximately €10,000 to €12,000 per year for the mowing hours the robot can absorb.

Best initial buyers:

- Hotels and resorts with large but uncomplicated lawns.
- Business parks and private campuses.
- Nursing-home groups with several sites.
- Golf facilities for roughs, practice areas, driving ranges and selected fairway work.
- Private schools or colleges with a facilities budget.
- Grounds contractors willing to use the brothers as a technology-enabled subcontractor.

Avoid as first customers:

- Open housing estates with uncontrolled children, dogs and vandalism risk.
- Councils and universities where a public tender is the only route.
- Clubs whose mowing is mostly volunteer labour.
- Championship turf where mowing is only one element of a specialist agronomy programme.

### 2. Parallel commercial test: floor-cleaning niche discovery

Do not buy a floor robot first. Determine whether there is a segment or geography the incumbents are not serving well.

Possible niches:

- West Cork and Munster multi-site retail.
- Overnight warehouse cleaning.
- Dealership showrooms.
- Nursing-home corridors and communal areas.
- Short-term event and seasonal deployment.
- A white-label or local-operator arrangement with an existing supplier.

### 3. Add-on after route proof: sports line marking

Sell the service before buying the machine. The machine is attractive only when several sites are geographically clustered and pay cash rather than valuing volunteer time.

## The operating company

The company is not a robotics manufacturer. It is an equipment-hire and managed-operations business.

It provides:

- Site assessment.
- Machine selection.
- Installation and mapping.
- Remote monitoring.
- Scheduled cleaning and servicing.
- Consumables.
- Fault response.
- Temporary replacement where commercially justified.
- Customer reporting.

The customer buys a result such as grass kept cut, floors kept clean or pitches kept marked.

## Hard first-machine gates

Do not buy the first machine unless all are true:

- Ten relevant buyer conversations have been completed.
- Three physically compatible sites have the same recurring problem.
- The customer’s present narrow task cost is documented.
- Insurance, transport, connectivity and service costs are quoted.
- A European parts and warranty route is confirmed.
- One paid pilot, deposit or conditional order exists.
- Conservative direct-cost payback is no longer than 24 months.
- The robot can be redeployed if the first customer cancels.
- The customer agreement states access, theft, vandalism and damage responsibilities.

## Scoring method

Each opportunity was scored from 0 to 5 on ten factors and converted to 100 points:

- Customer pain and current spend: 15 points.
- Entry simplicity: 15 points.
- Capital and conservative payback: 15 points.
- Recurring utilisation: 10 points.
- Buyer accessibility and sales speed: 10 points.
- Part-time operating fit: 10 points.
- Irish serviceable market and density: 10 points.
- Safety and failure consequences: 5 points.
- Competition and service differentiation: 5 points.
- Fleet and expansion value: 5 points.

A hard gate overrides the score. A tiny national market, mandatory specialist operation, unavailable pricing, regulation or an irredeployable machine can demote an opportunity even if other factors look good.

## Overall ranking

### Tier 1: test now

#### 1. Managed commercial autonomous mowing: 83/100

- Best combination of low capital, recurrence, remote monitoring and redeployability.
- Hardware is available in Ireland from approximately €5,500 to €8,995 for medium commercial machines.
- A larger Kress KR236E is listed in Britain at £7,249.17 ex VAT and is rated for 24,000 m² on a 48-hour cycle.
- The principal risk is not technical. It is finding sites where mowing is a sufficiently large paid cost and where theft, obstacles and public access are controlled.

#### 2. Commercial floor cleaning: 77/100, demoted to second because of competition

- Strongest verified customer-cost problem.
- Recurring daily work and clear before-and-after result.
- Irish supplier case studies claim €26,000 to €27,000 of annual cleaning cost at individual retail sites.
- Glanaco says it has more than 50 Irish deployments. Cleaning Robotics Ireland has named Irish customer cases and several robot brands.
- A new entrant needs a niche, local-operating partnership or meaningfully better service rather than a generic robot-rental offer.

#### 3. Mowing plus line marking: 75/100

- Raises revenue per sports or campus site.
- Mowing can recur continuously while line marking produces additional route revenue.
- The capital stack is approximately €30,000 or more before a van, spares and working capital if both machines are bought.
- The bundle is commercially stronger than standalone line marking, but only after mowing customers exist.

### Tier 2: validate before capital

#### 4. Remote slope and embankment mowing: 68/100

- Strong safety and labour problem on steep banks, solar farms, road embankments, industrial sites and difficult estates.
- Machines such as Green Climber, Spider, Raymo and Energreen Robogreen are proven product categories.
- It is operator-led rather than remotely unattended, so it is a managed contracting service, not passive recurring revenue.
- It deserves discovery because customers may pay day rates that support an expensive machine, but actual Irish quotes and insurance are needed.

#### 5. Standalone robotic line marking: 68/100

- Proven technology and a clearly measurable result.
- TinyLineMarker Sport is publicly listed in Ireland at €20,050 ex VAT, takes approximately 27 minutes for an 11-a-side football pitch and includes five years of service and updates.
- The machine is portable and reusable across many sites.
- The weaknesses are travel, seasonality, volunteer labour and constrained club budgets.

#### 6. Mobile pallet wrapping robots: provisional 66/100

- Warehouses and manufacturers already pay staff to wrap repeated pallets.
- Mobile wrappers are mature, portable and require far less integration than an AMR or conveyor system.
- The customer can often buy directly, so the service must include seasonal rental, maintenance and rapid replacement.
- Validate with food, drink, pharmaceutical logistics and third-party logistics operators before pricing a machine.

#### 7. Outdoor car-park and yard sweeping: provisional 65/100

- Clear repetitive work at warehouses, retail parks, depots and large dealerships.
- Adjacent to the floor-cleaning operating model but less crowded indoors.
- Larger autonomous sweepers can be expensive, and uncontrolled outdoor environments reduce reliability.
- A compact machine with a controlled private yard is the correct pilot, not a public street.

#### 8. Golf driving-range ball collection: provisional 64/100

- A robot can run frequently on a bounded, revenue-generating site.
- Belrobotics and related suppliers sell robotic ball pickers and compatible mowing systems.
- Golf Ireland states that more than 376 clubs are affiliated across the island, but only a subset has a driving range with enough ball volume.
- Attractive operational fit, but the Irish obtainable market is probably tens of sites rather than hundreds.

#### 9. Farm feed pushing and manure scraping: provisional 61/100

- Daily task with genuine labour and animal-husbandry value.
- Commercial products already exist through companies such as Lely and DeLaval.
- Ireland had approximately 133,200 farms in the 2023 Farm Structure Survey, but the relevant market is only suitable housed dairy and livestock units.
- Established agricultural dealer networks and farm-service expectations make partnership more credible than a stand-alone newcomer.

#### 10. HVAC duct-cleaning robots: provisional 61/100

- Teinnova sells robots for duct cleaning, inspection and sealing.
- Commercial kitchens, hotels, hospitals, offices and industrial facilities have a real hygiene and fire-risk problem.
- The equipment is standard, but the work still requires specialist cleaning practice, access, extraction equipment, reporting and an operator.
- Better as an acquisition or partnership with an existing duct-cleaning contractor than a first autonomous fleet service.

### Tier 3: later or narrow niches

#### 11. Pipe and drain inspection crawler: 59/100

- Clear hazardous-access problem and portable single-operator equipment exists.
- Commercial crawlers inspect roughly 4-inch to 84-inch pipes, with larger systems extending much further.
- The robot is only one part of the service. Customers pay for diagnosis, defect coding, reporting and repair recommendations.
- Existing drainage contractors already own cameras, and municipal work can require procurement and qualifications.

#### 12. Construction layout printing: 60/100

- HP SitePrint can deliver up to 10-times productivity gains in suitable layouts and HP cites customer cost reductions of 34% and 86% in specific projects.
- HP offers pay-as-you-go with support, repairs and next-business-day swap.
- It requires CAD preparation, survey control points, a robotic total station and a trained site operator.
- Strong problem, but not a low-specialisation first business. Sam’s property network could make a paid demonstration possible later.

#### 13. Solar-panel robotic cleaning: 57/100

- Large and growing physical asset base.
- Stronger on agricultural, coastal, bird-fouled, industrial or construction-adjacent sites.
- Weak general thesis in a wet climate where natural rainfall may already control most soiling.
- Equipment prices inspected on Irish supplier pages were RFQ-only.
- Sell measurement and a paid test before a cleaning subscription.

#### 14. Hospitality delivery robots: provisional 54/100

- PUDU and Keenon have mature off-the-shelf products, and Cleaning Robotics Ireland already advertises PUDU delivery robots.
- Installation is relatively straightforward on a single level.
- The labour saving is often weaker than the demonstration value, and lifts, doors, room-service workflows and customer acceptance introduce friction.
- More suitable as resale or short-event rental than as the first managed service.

#### 15. Ground-level or low-rise window robots: 51/100

- Window cleaning is recurring and commercially understood.
- For accessible glass, water-fed poles are already cheap, flexible and fast.
- Suction robots need power, water, tethering, repositioning and a human operator. They often move the labour rather than eliminate it.
- Only pursue a façade with repeated large uniform panes where conventional access is genuinely expensive.

#### 16. Cleaning drones for roofs, façades and windows: 56/100 but hard-gated to later

- Lucid Bots lists the Sherpa drone from US$45,750 or US$2,950 per month.
- It can work up to 150 feet, cover more than 300 square feet per minute and supports soft wash or pressure up to 4,500 PSI.
- The supplier says a job needs one pilot and one ground-crew member.
- In Ireland, aviation rules, insurance, building proximity, public exclusion zones, water hose behaviour, wind and falling-object risk must be resolved.
- This is a compelling later service once the company has operating capital and safety systems.

#### 17. Warehouse inventory scanning: 49/100

- Corvus, Gather AI and Dexory provide mature products.
- The pain is real in large warehouses, but the machine depends on customer data, warehouse processes and systems integration.
- Customers will expect inventory accuracy guarantees and enterprise support.
- Not low-barrier despite attractive recurring utilisation.

#### 18. Security patrol robots: 45/100

- Security is a large recurring labour budget.
- Robots do not replace alarm response, judgement, intervention or licensed guarding.
- Integration with a control room, privacy, reliability, vandalism and liability are substantial.
- Later fleet opportunity, not a bootstrap first machine.

#### 19. Commercial pool cleaning: 42/100 after market-size override

- Excellent technical simplicity and recurring use.
- Ireland’s market is small. A 2017 industry estimate cited approximately 425 pools, mostly private or hotel-owned, while a 2026 public-pool report found roughly one public pool per 81,000 people.
- Customers can already buy pool robots directly with little setup.
- Too narrow and too easy for customers to self-serve as a primary company thesis.

## Opportunity sheet 1: managed commercial mowing

### Problem and current solution

Grass is cut by grounds staff, landscaping contractors, ride-on mowers or volunteers. Irish secondary salary sources place gardeners and groundskeepers around €30,000 to €35,000 and greenkeepers around €32,000 to €38,000 annually before employer costs, equipment, fuel and management.

This is not the replaceable amount. A mower performs only the repeated cutting task. Staff still handle edges, weeds, fertiliser, drainage, pitch repair, trees, litter, hedges and machinery.

Public procurement proves that organisations spend real money on grounds maintenance:

- A 2026 Garda Limerick garden-maintenance notice was reported with an estimated annual value of €30,000, including grass, hedges, weeds and seasonal work.
- A 2026 SETU Carlow procurement covered seven grass pitches plus general grounds maintenance and carried an estimated €350,000 tender value in a tender aggregator.
- Councils in Cork, Waterford and Wicklow publish recurring grass-cutting procurements.

These bundled contracts do not reveal the mowing-only amount, but they establish budget and recurrence.

### Machines

For medium controlled sites:

- Segway Navimow Terranox CM120M1: approximately €5,500 observed Irish public price.
- Segway Navimow Terranox CM240M1: approximately €7,000.
- Husqvarna 535 AWD EPOS: approximately €6,290.
- Husqvarna 580 EPOS: approximately €8,995.
- Kress KR236E: £7,249.17 ex VAT in Britain, recommended for 24,000 m² on a 48-hour cycle, 35 cm cut, 40% maximum slope, 4G and anti-theft controls.

For major sites:

- Husqvarna CEORA 546 EPOS: approximately 50,000 to 75,000 m² depending configuration and mowing cycle.
- Irish official module prices include €9,990 for the CEORA RZ 43L sports/fairway cutting deck and €2,990 for the CS4 charging station. The drive unit, reference station, installation and service are additional, making CEORA unsuitable for an unfunded first pilot.
- ECHO or Belrobotics TM-2050 and BigMow: large-area machines for sports, driving ranges and public space. Pricing remains dealer/RFQ-led.

### Operational constraints

- RTK sky visibility and obstruction by buildings or trees.
- Charging point, electricity and connectivity.
- Strimming and edge work remain manual.
- Objects, branches, footballs and temporary furniture cause downtime.
- Wet ground may be physically mowable but still vulnerable to rutting or turf damage.
- Public access, pets, children, theft and vandalism.
- Blade inspection, cleaning and winter storage.
- A backup arrangement during failure.

Husqvarna’s commercial platform provides fleet monitoring, remote start and stop, location, theft alerts, GPS tracking, temporary exclusion zones and over-the-air firmware.

### Commercial model

Base scenario, excluding VAT:

- Mower: €5,500 to €8,995.
- RTK or reference equipment: zero to approximately €1,299 depending model.
- Installation and mapping assumption: €750 to €1,500.
- Initial spares, security and failure reserve: €1,000 to €1,500.
- First-year deployed cash: approximately €8,000 to €13,000.
- Annual service, insurance, connectivity and site visits: model €1,800 to €3,000 until quoted.
- Customer price: approximately €650 to €1,100 per month depending area and service level.
- Conservative direct-cost payback: approximately 18 to 30 months.

The customer case works where the narrow mowing task costs at least approximately €10,000 to €12,000 per year, or the robot prevents an additional seasonal hire or contractor increase.

### Market boundary

Known site-count anchors include more than 376 Golf Ireland affiliated clubs across the island, 19 publicly funded higher-education institutions listed by the HEA and hundreds of hotels, schools, nursing homes and private campuses.

A useful planning model is:

- Technical Irish site pool: 1,000 to 2,500 controlled commercial sites with sufficient grass.
- Budget and geometry-qualified serviceable pool: 300 to 700.
- Cork and Munster obtainable market in two years: 10 to 25 sites.
- Ten sites at €850 per month: €102,000 annual recurring revenue before VAT.
- Twenty-five sites at €850 per month: €255,000 annual recurring revenue.

These are planning scenarios, not measured market sizes. The first ten customer interviews must replace them with site-level evidence.

### Decision

**Pursue first.** Secure one paid controlled-site pilot before purchasing.

## Opportunity sheet 2: floor cleaning

### Problem and current cost

The job is frequent, visible and already budgeted. Cleaning Robotics Ireland publishes two useful supplier case studies:

- A SuperValu case states €500 per week or €26,000 per year in part-time cleaning.
- A West Cork Centra case states €7,000 per year for contract sweeping plus €20,000 in staff overtime for mopping.

The supplier claims first-year and sub-16-month payback respectively. These figures are not independently audited, but they are stronger Irish demand evidence than exists for most other categories.

### Robots

Irish suppliers advertise:

- PUDU CC1 and CC1 Pro.
- SPARKOZ TN10 Pro and TN70 Pro.
- Gausium Phantas, Beetle and Scrubber 50.
- ZACO commercial vacuums.
- Larger Avidbots, Tennant, Kärcher and LionsBot machines internationally.

Public Irish purchase prices were not found on the inspected pages. Treat all hardware economics as RFQ-only.

### Competition

- Glanaco says it has more than 50 nationwide deployments and 15 years of cleaning experience.
- Cleaning Robotics Ireland publishes named retail installations and has a Claregalway office.
- Rentabot advertises rental and managed support, but its displayed inventory and operating scale remain unverified.
- Traditional cleaning-machine suppliers also have established repair networks.

### Commercial boundary

Illustrative quote gate:

- Hardware and deployment must be below approximately €25,000 to €35,000 for a compact site, or be supplier-financed.
- Customer current floor-cleaning cost should exceed approximately €20,000 per year.
- Managed price likely needs to be approximately €1,500 to €2,500 per month depending machine, service and backup.
- Target direct-cost payback should remain below 24 months.

Do not rely on removing all cleaning labour. Corners, shelves, toilets, spills, waste and detailed cleaning remain human work.

### Decision

**Validate a niche or partnership, but do not buy the first machine speculatively.**

## Opportunity sheet 3: mowing plus line marking

### Problem

Sports pitches need frequent mowing and repeated line marking. Manual line marking consumes staff or volunteer time, requires measurements and creates rework when lines drift.

### Machine and price

Turfy Ireland publicly lists TinyLineMarker Sport at:

- €20,050 ex VAT.
- €24,661.50 including VAT.
- Approximately 27 minutes for an 11-a-side pitch.
- Five hours of battery runtime.
- Five years of service and updates included.

Turf Tank says its current machine has more than 120 layouts and more than 1,400 customers globally. Pricing is quote or subscription-led.

### Service economics

Illustrative model:

- Service price: €100 to €175 per standard pitch visit depending travel, repainting and paint.
- Contribution after travel, operator time and paint: approximately €60 to €110 per visit until actual routes are tested.
- Machine payback in 24 months requires approximately 8 to 14 paid pitch visits per month at that contribution range.
- A multi-pitch campus or cluster of nearby clubs is materially better than scattered single pitches.
- A seasonal monthly contract may range from approximately €300 to €900 depending pitch count and frequency.

### Buyer quality

Strongest:

- Universities and large schools.
- Local-authority or leisure-centre complexes.
- Grounds contractors serving many clubs.
- Multi-pitch football and rugby facilities.
- Better-funded GAA county grounds and clubs.

Weakest:

- Single volunteer-run clubs.
- Sites marking only occasionally.
- Clubs where an unpaid volunteer already performs the work reliably.

### Decision

**Sell a route first. Buy only after at least 10 recurring monthly pitch visits are contracted.**

## Opportunity sheet 4: solar-panel cleaning

### Asset base

SEAI reported 753.32 MW of grid-scale solar and 570.08 MW of rooftop capacity at the end of 2024. Solar Ireland later reported 2.7 GW of connected capacity by May 2026 using ESB Networks data.

This proves growth, not cleaning demand.

### Economic boundary

Using 2024 utility generation of approximately 0.728 TWh:

- A 0.5% recoverable soiling loss equals roughly 3,641 MWh.
- At an illustrative €50 to €100 per MWh, that energy is worth approximately €182,000 to €364,000 nationally before cleaning cost.
- A 3% recoverable loss equals approximately €1.09 million to €2.18 million.

These are boundary scenarios, not measured Irish losses. They demonstrate why cleaning must be justified site by site.

### Best targets

- Bird-fouled roofs.
- Farms and feed or grain facilities.
- Coastal salt exposure.
- Construction or quarry dust.
- Industrial particulates.
- Arrays where monitoring already shows a persistent performance gap.
- Large contiguous roofs or ground-mounted arrays with straightforward access.

### Machines and competition

Solar Clean Robotics Ireland advertises SolarCleano machines, training, repairs and cleaning services. Other Irish solar cleaners also advertise robotic methods. The inspected M1 Mini and F1 pages did not show public prices.

### Commercial model

Start with measurement and per-job work:

- Pre-clean inspection and generation baseline.
- Small paid test area.
- Post-clean comparison against an untreated control.
- Quote per project, not a national generic monthly plan.
- Do not buy a dedicated robot until repeat sites demonstrate at least 10 to 15 profitable jobs per year.

### Decision

**Targeted test only. Do not assume routine national demand.**

## Opportunity sheet 5: window and façade cleaning

### Current solution

- Water-fed poles for accessible low and mid-rise glass.
- Mobile elevated work platforms.
- Rope access.
- Cradles, scaffolding or engineered building-maintenance units.

The robot creates value only if it removes expensive access or materially increases crew output.

### Accessible suction robots

These still require:

- Roof or edge access.
- Tethers and anchors.
- Power and water.
- Repositioning.
- Exclusion zones.
- A human operator and rescue plan.

For ordinary glass, a water-fed pole is often simpler and cheaper.

### Cleaning drones

Lucid Bots publicly states:

- Sherpa starts at US$45,750.
- Subscription starts at US$2,950 per month.
- Maximum building height of approximately 150 feet.
- More than 300 square feet per minute.
- One pilot and one ground-crew member.
- Soft washing and pressure up to 4,500 PSI.

Supplier ROI claims are based mainly on US operations and do not replace Irish compliance or customer evidence.

### Irish barriers

- IAA and EASA drone-operation category.
- Flying near people and buildings.
- Commercial insurance.
- Wind and hose dynamics.
- Falling-object and chemical exposure.
- Public exclusion zones.
- Building-owner consent.
- Water containment and runoff.
- Façade warranty and material compatibility.

### Decision

**Later-stage service. First validate through an existing cleaning contractor rather than purchasing equipment.**

## First two-year market model

### Conservative case

- Three mowing customers by the end of year one.
- Average €800 monthly recurring revenue per site.
- One machine per site.
- Year-one run-rate: €28,800.
- Year-two average of eight sites: €76,800 annual recurring revenue.
- No employee until contracted gross contribution supports one.

### Base case

- Five sites by the end of year one.
- Ten to twelve sites by the end of year two.
- Average €850 monthly recurring revenue.
- Year-two exit run-rate at twelve sites: €122,400.
- Add line-marking route only when it can generate at least €1,250 monthly revenue in season.

### Expansion case

- Twenty-five managed mowing sites at €850 monthly: €255,000 annual recurring revenue.
- One line-marking route producing €40,000 to €70,000 annual revenue.
- One technician plus founder oversight.
- Begin testing a second category only after mower uptime, service cost and churn are known.

These are operating scenarios, not forecasts.

## First-customer sequence

### Interview set 1: private controlled turf

Complete ten interviews across:

- Two hotels or resorts.
- Two business parks or private campuses.
- Two golf facilities.
- Two nursing-home or residential-care groups.
- Two grounds contractors.

Ask for:

- Mowable square metres.
- Current mowing frequency.
- Employee and contractor hours.
- Machine ownership and fuel cost.
- Seasonal labour.
- Strimming and edge-work requirement.
- Public access and vandalism history.
- Current annual mowing-only cost.
- What a 30-day paid pilot would need to prove.

### Interview set 2: floor cleaning

Complete five interviews with large floor areas and ask:

- Floor square metres.
- Cleaning hours and shifts.
- Overtime and contractor spend.
- Existing machine and labour mix.
- Obstacles, aisle changes and spill frequency.
- Whether Irish robotic suppliers have already quoted.
- Required fault-response time.

### Interview set 3: line marking

Complete five interviews with multi-pitch sites or contractors and obtain:

- Pitches and sports types.
- Marking frequency.
- Paid versus volunteer labour.
- Paint consumption.
- Current time per pitch.
- Travel between sites.
- Maximum monthly service budget.

No outreach has been authorised or performed as part of this study.

## Final decision

The first proposition should be:

> **Commercial lawns kept consistently cut for a fixed monthly fee. We install, monitor, maintain and replace the robot. The customer avoids the capital purchase, technology selection and repair burden.**

The first machine should be a medium commercial mower in the approximate €5,500 to €9,000 hardware band, not a CEORA-scale system.

The first customer should be a bounded private commercial site with a named facilities budget, a documented mowing-only cost above approximately €10,000 per year and a paid commitment before purchase.

Floor cleaning is the best evidence that the broader managed-robot model works in Ireland, but it is already the most obviously competitive category. Treat incumbents as proof of demand and possible partners, not as evidence of an easy opening.

The sequence is:

1. Sell one paid mowing pilot.
2. Operate it through one full growing season.
3. Document uptime, callouts, manual finishing and customer savings.
4. Add three to five similar sites.
5. Add line marking only when a contracted route exists.
6. Hire a technician when service contribution supports the role.
7. Build internal fleet software only after actual fleet data exists.
8. Move into harder cleaning, inspection and regulated work after capital and references exist.
