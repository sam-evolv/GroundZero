# Ireland low-barrier commercial robotics opportunity study

**Date:** 4 August 2026  
**Decision:** Which off-the-shelf commercial robot service can two part-time founders launch in Ireland with limited capital, earn recurring revenue from, and later expand into a managed fleet?

## Core company thesis

The initial company is not a robotics manufacturer or enterprise systems integrator. It is a commercial robot operator.

It buys or leases proven machines, installs and monitors them, handles maintenance and replacement, and charges customers per job or per month. Customers purchase the completed outcome and one accountable Irish support relationship rather than a robot.

The long-term path is:

1. One narrow commercial task.
2. One repeatable installation and service process.
3. Ten paying sites.
4. Multiple machines and spare capacity.
5. Remote monitoring and maintenance operations.
6. Cross-vendor scheduling and reporting software.
7. Expansion into larger industrial and regulated workflows.

## Hard entry filters

An opportunity cannot be the first deployment if it depends on any of the following:

- Proprietary robot development.
- Material customer IT integration.
- Cleanroom, ATEX or pharmaceutical validation before payment.
- High-rise engineered access as the only use case.
- Public procurement as the only route to a customer.
- A continuous specialist operator at every deployment.
- More than approximately €50,000 of founder capital before a paid commitment.
- A machine that cannot be redeployed if the first customer cancels.
- Unavailable European parts, service or warranty support.

Passing these filters does not make an opportunity commercially attractive. It only makes it possible to test.

## Weighted scoring model

Each opportunity receives a score from 0 to 5 for each factor. Weighted scores are then converted to a total out of 100.

### 1. Customer pain and current spend: 15 points

- **0:** No clear budget or manual alternative.
- **1:** Infrequent annoyance with little measurable spend.
- **3:** Repeated paid task with identifiable labour or contractor cost.
- **5:** Frequent, expensive, dangerous or unreliable task with a named budget owner.

### 2. Entry simplicity: 15 points

- **0:** Bespoke engineering, validation or major integration.
- **1:** Specialist team and long deployment.
- **3:** Training and site preparation, but standard hardware.
- **5:** Proven equipment, installation in one day or less and ordinary service controls.

### 3. Capital and conservative payback: 15 points

- **0:** More than €100,000 before revenue or no credible payback.
- **1:** €50,000 to €100,000 with uncertain utilisation.
- **3:** €15,000 to €50,000 and payback within approximately 24 to 36 months.
- **5:** Less than €15,000 and payback within approximately 18 to 24 months under conservative utilisation.

### 4. Recurring utilisation: 10 points

- **0:** One-off or unpredictable use.
- **1:** Annual or highly seasonal work.
- **3:** Monthly or seasonal recurring work.
- **5:** Daily or weekly use with a credible multi-year contract.

### 5. Buyer accessibility and sales speed: 10 points

- **0:** Central public procurement or regulated enterprise programme only.
- **1:** Long committee-led purchase.
- **3:** Reachable facilities, property or operations buyer.
- **5:** Owner-managed or local budget holder who can approve a paid trial quickly.

### 6. Part-time operating fit: 10 points

- **0:** Continuous founder attendance or daytime emergency response.
- **1:** Specialist operator required for most productive hours.
- **3:** Periodic site visits and predictable support.
- **5:** Remote monitoring, scheduled servicing and many machines per operator.

### 7. Irish serviceable market and density: 10 points

- **0:** Fewer than approximately 25 plausible sites nationally.
- **1:** Small dispersed niche.
- **3:** Hundreds of plausible sites or a dense regional cluster.
- **5:** Thousands of plausible sites with strong geographic density and expansion potential.

Sector counts alone are not TAM. The model must apply physical compatibility, buyer budget, adoption appetite and reachable geography.

### 8. Safety, insurance and failure consequences: 5 points

- **0:** A failure can cause severe public harm or site shutdown.
- **1:** Specialist engineering and high liability.
- **3:** Manageable exclusion zones, training and commercial insurance.
- **5:** Controlled site, low-energy machine and easy manual recovery.

### 9. Competition and service differentiation: 5 points

- **0:** Commodity market dominated by national incumbents with strong local support.
- **1:** Several credible managed-service fleets and weak differentiation.
- **3:** Hardware competition exists but service coverage or target segment is fragmented.
- **5:** Proven hardware exists and no credible local managed operator serves the chosen segment.

Competition validates demand but must not be mistaken for an open market.

### 10. Fleet and expansion value: 5 points

- **0:** Isolated equipment with no adjacent fleet opportunity.
- **1:** Narrow one-product resale business.
- **3:** Additional robots can serve the same buyer or operating process.
- **5:** Strong route into multi-site fleets, recurring data, central monitoring and harder commercial workflows.

## Commercial evidence required for every use case

Each opportunity will be evaluated using the same evidence chain.

### The problem

- Exact task being performed.
- Frequency and seasonality.
- Current labour, equipment, access, contractor and management burden.
- Consequences when it is delayed or performed poorly.
- Named person who owns the budget.

### Current solution

- Internal employee, contractor, volunteer or capital equipment.
- Fully loaded annual cost rather than salary alone.
- Number of people and hours involved.
- Existing service price where published.
- Why customers have not already automated it.

### Proposed managed robot service

- Exact machine and complete deployment stack.
- Public list price, RFQ price or used-market evidence.
- Installation, transport, consumables, software, servicing, insurance and failure reserve.
- Human oversight still required.
- Customer-facing price and contract form.
- Gross contribution before founder salaries and tax.

### Market

- Total physically compatible Irish sites.
- Serviceable sites with a likely budget and buyer appetite.
- Obtainable regional market for the first two years.
- Current competitors and substitutes.
- Route to the first ten customers.

### Decision economics

- Upfront cash requirement.
- Monthly fixed and variable cost.
- Break-even utilisation.
- Base, conservative and downside payback.
- Ability to redeploy the robot.
- Paid commitment required before purchase.

## Preliminary evidence already verified

- Leixlip Tool Hire lists commercial robotic mowers suitable for hotels, campuses and sports facilities. Public prices observed include approximately €5,500 for a Segway Navimow Terranox CM120M1, €7,000 for a CM240M1, €6,290 for a Husqvarna 535 AWD EPOS and €8,995 for a Husqvarna 580 EPOS. The page advertises eight commercial models. These are hardware prices, not all-in deployed costs.
- Rentabot.ie advertises an Irish robot-rental model with flexible terms and support across cleaning, windows, mowing, delivery, security and other categories. Its directory is quote-only. The displayed catalogue does not prove that every listed machine is physically held in Ireland or actively rented.
- Glanaco, Cleaning Robotics Ireland and other suppliers advertise commercial floor-cleaning robots in Ireland. Competition and installed-customer evidence must be checked before selecting that category.
- Sports Line Services Ireland advertises the RM21 combined robotic mower and line marker. Irish robotic line-marking services and equipment suppliers already exist.
- Solar Clean Robotics Ireland sells SolarCleano machines and related cleaning systems and advertises operator training, repairs and commercial solar-cleaning services. Several other Irish providers advertise robotic solar cleaning.
- A current Irish greenkeeper salary estimate found through SalaryExpert is approximately €37,992, while a golf-course groundskeeper estimate is approximately €30,334. These are secondary salary estimates and do not equal the full grounds operation cost.
- Irish commercial-cleaning providers publicly advertise rates from approximately €49 per hour or €0.14 to €0.29 per square foot, but scope, location and frequency vary. These provider claims require triangulation and cannot be applied directly to robotic floor-cleaning savings.
- UCD and other public bodies procure grounds and sports-pitch maintenance through formal frameworks. This establishes real institutional spend but also indicates slower procurement and incumbent contractor relationships.

## Interpretation rule

A robot does not replace a full greenkeeper, cleaner or facilities employee unless it performs the complete role. The economic case must use the narrow labour hours, contractor visits, access equipment or overtime that the machine actually removes. Claims based on replacing an entire salary will be rejected unless the role genuinely disappears.

## Purchase gate

No first machine should be purchased until all of the following are true:

- At least ten relevant buyer conversations have been completed.
- At least three physically compatible sites have the same recurring task.
- Current customer cost has been documented.
- Insurance, transport and operating requirements have been priced.
- A local or European support and parts route exists.
- At least one paid pilot, deposit or conditional order exists.
- Conservative utilisation recovers direct deployment cost within approximately 24 months.
- The robot can be redeployed to another site if the first customer cancels.

## Research in progress

The study will apply this model to:

1. Commercial autonomous mowing.
2. Robotic mowing plus sports-pitch line marking.
3. Commercial floor-cleaning robots.
4. Solar-panel cleaning by job.
5. Accessible window and façade cleaning.
6. Additional low-barrier robot hire or managed-service categories identified through market screening.

The final decision will rank opportunities by cash-efficient entry and realistic obtainable revenue, not by theoretical technology sophistication or global market headlines.
