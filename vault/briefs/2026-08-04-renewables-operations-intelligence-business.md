# Renewable infrastructure operations intelligence

## Executive verdict

There is a credible business here, but it is not “buy a solar-cleaning robot and become a cleaner”, and it is not “build another renewable dashboard”.

The strongest entry wedge is an **independent owner-side operations and assurance service for solar portfolios**. It would combine:

- read-only ingestion from SCADA, inverter portals, meters, weather and market data;
- generation and export reconciliation;
- underperformance and fault triage;
- an asset register that links alarms, components, work orders, photographs, interventions, warranty claims and recovered output;
- monthly investor, lender and management reporting;
- coordination and verification of specialist field contractors;
- evidence-triggered thermal drone inspections, vegetation work and panel cleaning;
- later integration of satellite, drone and robotic data where each source is technically useful.

The customer buys **fewer unexplained losses, better control of contractors, faster fault resolution and audit-ready operating evidence**. The dashboard is only the interface.

This is more attractive than robotic mowing as the first business because the recurring monitoring, reporting and coordination work continues through winter. Mowing robots can become a site-maintenance module later.

The opportunity is real but not uncontested. Ireland already has broad O&M and asset-management firms, specialist cleaning and grounds contractors, drone-inspection providers and international software platforms. The opening is not that nobody does the work. It is that fast-growing Irish and UK portfolios can still have fragmented data, weak links between alarms and field work, manual reporting, multiple OEM portals, and poor owner-side visibility across service providers.

## The candid answer

### What is attractive

- Ireland had 2.7 GW of connected solar by May 2026, including 1.59 GW of utility-scale solar. The utility segment has grown from almost nothing to material infrastructure quickly.
- Ireland targets 8 GW by 2030 and Solar Ireland estimates another 0.8–1.3 GW may need to be added annually.
- Utility solar assets operate for 30 years or more. Operations and maintenance is the longest phase of the lifecycle.
- Monitoring, reporting, fault triage, contract oversight and work-order evidence are recurring and year-round.
- Sam already has renewable-industry context, warm relationships and an existing Evolv data/reporting base. That is a meaningful distribution and execution advantage if ownership and conflict boundaries are clean.
- The UK creates a much larger adjacent market. DESNZ reported 22.8 GW of UK solar capacity by June 2026 and estimated roughly 59% was ground-mounted.

### What is not a defensible thesis

- “Renewables, AI and robotics are topical” is not a business model.
- A generic custom dashboard is not novel. Power Factors, GreenPowerMonitor, Clir, Raptor Maps, Sitemark and other platforms already cover substantial parts of monitoring, asset management, reporting, inspections and field work.
- A broad “one-stop shop” from day one is not credible for a part-time two-founder company. Full O&M requires qualified electrical and HV capability, safe systems of work, spare-parts access, field-response coverage, cybersecurity, insurer acceptance and contractual availability obligations.
- Solar cleaning alone is unlikely to support an attractive nationwide recurring business in Ireland. Cleaning frequency is site-specific and rainfall can reduce demand.
- Wind O&M is not a low-barrier adjacency. Ireland already has sophisticated asset managers, 24/7 control services, OEM teams, technicians and drone inspectors.
- Drones can lift panels to roofs, but that is not the same as installing, fastening, weatherproofing, wiring, testing and certifying a rooftop system.

## The actual customer problem

A solar owner may have all of the following without having one reliable operating picture:

- SCADA and data-loggers;
- inverter and tracker manufacturer portals;
- fiscal and export meters;
- irradiance, temperature, wind, rainfall and soiling sensors;
- market, PPA and curtailment data;
- an EPC warranty pack;
- an O&M contractor ticketing system;
- thermal-inspection PDFs and image files;
- vegetation and cleaning contractors;
- planning, ecology and biodiversity obligations;
- insurer, lender, board and investor reporting requirements;
- spreadsheets used to reconcile all of the above.

The expensive problems are not limited to dirty panels. They include:

- unnoticed inverter, string, communications or sensor failures;
- false or badly configured alarms;
- underperformance that is detected but not localised;
- time lost proving whether a fault sits with the EPC, OEM, O&M contractor, grid or owner;
- poor handoff from an alarm to a field ticket and back to verified recovery;
- incomplete asset and serial-number records;
- delayed warranty claims and missing evidence;
- inconsistent performance-ratio and availability calculations;
- weak attribution of curtailment, grid outages and maintenance downtime;
- fragmented portfolio reporting across mixed equipment;
- vegetation shading, drainage, erosion, fencing, access and biodiversity issues;
- cyber and data-access risk when systems and contractors change.

SolarPower Europe’s 2025 O&M guidance explicitly says alarms and maintenance should be linked through a work-order system, interventions should include site photographs, owners should retain the records, and historical O&M data, workflows and alarms should be connected for tracing and future predictive maintenance. This is close to the proposed wedge.

## How the market currently solves it

### Full O&M and asset management

Established providers already offer combinations of monitoring, field maintenance, compliance, reporting and asset management.

- **Galetech** publicly offers wind, solar and storage O&M, 24/7 monitoring, asset management, inspection and compliance.
- **EnergyPro** offers wind asset management, monitoring, reporting, operational control and its GenWatch availability and trader-data product.
- **Optinergy** maintains wind fleets across Ireland and the UK, with regional technicians, parts stores, major component changeouts and 24-hour response.
- Large owners, EPCs and international O&M providers can also manage assets in-house or under long-term contracts.

These firms make “we are a one-stop shop” a weak differentiator.

### Irish solar field services

Visible domestic competitors already cover several practical tasks.

- **Clean Solar Solutions Ireland** markets solar cleaning, cleaning robots, thermal imaging, servicing, repairs, bird proofing and grounds maintenance. It also markets wind-turbine cleaning.
- **Glás Services** markets complete solar-farm grounds maintenance and management, including grass cutting, hedges, ecology and panel cleaning coordination.
- **SGR Solar** markets proactive O&M, inspections, issue resolution and advanced monitoring.
- **Gener8 Engineering** markets solar monitoring, inspections, performance evaluation and cleaning across residential, commercial and agricultural projects.
- **Engineers With Drones** markets solar-farm, rooftop, wind-turbine, grid and other asset inspections.

The field-service market is not empty. A new entrant needs a portfolio-level owner proposition, a segment the incumbents underserve, or materially better evidence and response orchestration.

### International software

- **Power Factors Unity** covers SCADA, plant control, asset performance management, portfolio oversight, invoices and field-service work orders.
- **GreenPowerMonitor** provides renewable monitoring, control and asset-management workflows.
- **Clir** markets investor-grade portfolio reporting, benchmarking, anomaly detection and contractual-availability reconciliation across more than 350 GW of renewable assets.
- **Raptor Maps** combines aerial, ground, sensor and equipment data in a map-based solar intelligence platform, including autonomous drone products.
- **Sitemark** combines solar inspections, work management, asset management, construction progress, commissioning, drones and analytics.

The software gap is therefore not “nobody has built monitoring”. The possible gap is **deployment, integration, operation and accountability for Irish and smaller UK portfolios that will not buy or successfully operate a large enterprise stack**.

## Recommended entry wedge

## Owner-side solar operations desk

Start as the owner’s independent operating evidence and contractor-assurance layer. Do not become operator of record and do not promise 24/7 response initially.

### Initial scope

- Connect read-only data from existing portals and meters.
- Build and verify the asset register, hierarchy and source-of-truth identifiers.
- Establish expected-output, performance-ratio and availability baselines.
- Reconcile generation, export, market and curtailment information.
- Review alarms and anomalies during agreed business hours.
- Create owner-visible tickets with fault class, affected capacity, likely loss, responsible party and required action.
- Link every intervention to photographs, components, technician/contractor, cost, downtime and post-fix validation.
- Produce monthly operating and investor reports.
- Track O&M response, resolution, preventive-maintenance completion and repeat faults.
- Recommend inspections, cleaning or vegetation work based on evidence rather than calendar habit.

### What to exclude initially

- 24/7 control-room duty;
- switching, plant control or emergency shutdown;
- HV electrical work;
- guaranteed contractual availability;
- carrying spare-parts risk;
- direct wind-turbine maintenance;
- BVLOS autonomous-drone operations;
- residential callouts;
- unproven proprietary cleaning or installation hardware.

This scope is compatible with part-time validation. Full O&M is not.

## The differentiated operating loop

The proposed product should connect five things competitors and contractors often leave separated:

1. **Signal:** SCADA, meter, inverter, weather or image data identifies an anomaly.
2. **Diagnosis:** the system estimates affected capacity, likely cause, urgency and probable revenue impact.
3. **Action:** a named owner, O&M contractor, OEM or specialist receives a work order.
4. **Evidence:** the intervention adds photographs, readings, parts, serial numbers, cost and completion evidence.
5. **Verification:** production and availability data confirm whether the problem was actually solved and quantify recovery.

The long-term data asset is not a dashboard. It is an asset-to-fault-to-action-to-recovery history across a portfolio.

## Where AI genuinely helps

AI should remove operating labour, not decorate the product.

Useful applications include:

- normalising alarm descriptions across inverter and SCADA vendors;
- prioritising faults by lost-MWh exposure and safety category;
- detecting persistent and comparative underperformance;
- summarising work orders and engineering notes;
- matching repeat faults to prior resolutions;
- extracting serial numbers, components and evidence from reports;
- drafting monthly operating reports with source links;
- checking whether contractor evidence satisfies the agreed scope;
- forecasting likely maintenance demand and spare-parts needs.

Fail-safe boundaries:

- no autonomous switching or plant control;
- no AI-only electrical diagnosis presented as fact;
- source-linked claims and human review for owner reports;
- explicit confidence and missing-data flags;
- read-only integrations in the pilot;
- contractual KPIs calculated from agreed definitions, not a generic model.

## Solar cleaning: a module, not the company

### The real decision

SolarPower Europe says cleaning frequency should be decided site by site. Soiling depends on proximity to agriculture, rail, industry and other local conditions. Bird fouling and lichen can become persistent, but regular blanket cleaning is not automatically economic.

For Ireland, rainfall makes “every farm must be cleaned on a fixed schedule” an unsafe premise. The correct offer is:

- establish a clean reference or use a soiling sensor;
- compare string/inverter performance and rainfall;
- inspect for bird fouling, lichen or localised contamination;
- estimate recoverable production before dispatch;
- clean only when expected value exceeds mobilisation, water, labour, warranty and downtime costs;
- verify post-cleaning improvement.

### Robotics

Robotic cleaning can reduce labour and improve consistency on suitable array geometries. It adds constraints:

- compatibility with table gaps, tilt and tracker geometry;
- robot transfer between rows;
- pure-water and runoff requirements;
- module pressure and manufacturer warranties;
- edge/fall risk;
- storage, charging and transport;
- operator oversight and recovery from stoppages.

The first commercial move should be to subcontract or rent appropriate equipment for a measured pilot, not buy a fleet.

## Vegetation and grounds

Vegetation can cause disproportionate shading losses, hot spots, access problems, erosion and community complaints. SolarPower Europe notes it can be a significant share of operating costs.

This creates a better version of the original mower idea:

- mowing is sold as one line in a solar-site annual maintenance plan;
- revenue is supported by the year-round owner-side monitoring contract;
- robotic mowing is used only on sites whose geometry, drainage, obstacles and vegetation type suit it;
- manual contractors, grazing or selective treatment remain available where robots are unsuitable;
- biodiversity and planning obligations are tracked as part of the evidence layer.

This turns a seasonal asset into a complementary service rather than the whole company.

## Drones, satellites and Google tools

### Thermal and RGB drones

Drone thermography is operationally useful because it can localise module-, substring- and string-level defects faster than manual inspection. It is best used:

- at commissioning and handover;
- after severe weather;
- when monitoring identifies unexplained losses;
- periodically for high-value portfolio assurance;
- to verify vegetation, drainage, fencing and access conditions.

SolarPower Europe notes that aerial inspections can process several MW per hour and detect faults down to submodule or cell level, but scheduled inspections are snapshots and are often disconnected from continuous monitoring. The proposed service should connect those two worlds.

Initially use qualified specialist drone partners. Buying a thermal drone does not create the diagnostic, regulatory, insurance or engineering capability on its own.

### Planet

PlanetScope’s near-daily 3.7 m imagery is useful for macro site change, not panel diagnosis. Potential applications include:

- broad vegetation growth patterns;
- flooding or drainage change;
- access-road condition at a coarse level;
- neighbouring construction or land-use changes;
- construction progress and site-boundary monitoring;
- prioritising where a drone or person should inspect.

SkySat’s 50 cm imagery provides more detail but still lacks thermal information and is affected by tasking economics, weather and cloud.

Planet should be tested only where it eliminates a site visit or catches a material issue. Do not assume a satellite layer is valuable because it looks sophisticated.

### Google Earth and Google Solar API

- Google Earth imagery is useful for context and historical visual review, not reliable operational monitoring cadence.
- Google Earth Engine can analyse public satellite data, but Sentinel-2 at 10 m and Landsat at 30 m are too coarse for module faults. They can support broad vegetation, flooding or land-change analysis.
- Google’s Solar API is aimed primarily at building geometry and rooftop solar potential. It is more relevant to installation sales and design than to utility-farm O&M.

## Predictive maintenance

The useful predictive inputs are principally operational, not satellite:

- inverter and string current/voltage;
- alarms and reset history;
- module and ambient temperature;
- irradiance and expected yield;
- communication failures;
- tracker behaviour;
- weather and lightning events;
- component model, age and serial-number cohorts;
- previous faults and repair effectiveness.

The first product should be anomaly triage and evidence capture. Remaining-useful-life models need enough clean history and repeated component cohorts. Calling a simple threshold dashboard “predictive maintenance” would overstate the product.

## Wind: later, not first

### What owners actually pay for

- turbine availability and fault response;
- OEM contract oversight;
- SCADA and market reporting;
- blade inspection and damage classification;
- lightning damage and leading-edge erosion;
- gearbox, bearing, generator and major-component work;
- statutory, safety and grid compliance;
- access control and emergency response;
- spare-parts and technician logistics.

### Do blades need cleaning?

Sometimes, but cleaning is not the universal high-value wedge. Insects, salt, oil or contamination can affect surfaces, and towers or nacelles may need cleaning, but owners care more about erosion, structural damage, lightning, availability and safe repair.

### Competition and barriers

EnergyPro, Galetech and Optinergy already show deep domestic capability. Engineers With Drones and other inspection firms already market wind inspection. International players such as SkySpecs, Cyberhawk, Sulzer & Schmid, Clobotics and Aerones operate in inspection or robotic maintenance.

Wind should be phase three:

- begin by adding wind data/reporting only for a solar customer that already owns wind;
- partner for inspections;
- never present image capture as an engineering blade assessment without qualified review;
- do not buy hardware until repeated paid demand exists.

## Installation robotics and drones

### Current reality

Utility-scale solar construction robotics is real. AES Maximo, Terabase Terafab, Cosmic Robotics and Charge Robotics are examples of systems intended to reduce labour in handling, placing or assembling modules on ground-mounted projects.

This does not mean autonomous rooftop installation is ready.

A heavy-lift drone can deliver a panel to a roof, but installers still need to:

- establish structural capacity;
- design wind-uplift and mounting loads;
- fit rails, anchors or certified adhesive systems;
- preserve weatherproofing and roof warranties;
- provide fall protection and exclusion zones;
- wire strings, isolators, inverters and protection;
- test, certify and commission the electrical installation.

Search results and vendor material did not establish a mature, mass-market drone system that independently completes Irish residential rooftop installation. Treat online videos as panel-lifting demonstrations unless independently proven otherwise.

### Strategic posture

- Track heavy-lift rooftop logistics, adhesive/flexible PV, building-integrated PV and utility construction robots quarterly.
- Look for CE-marked systems with insurer, OEM and warranty acceptance and repeat commercial deployments.
- Become an Irish deployment or service partner only after the technology is proven.
- Do not build proprietary installation robotics.

## Market and economic scenarios

### Connected asset base

- Ireland: 1.59 GW of utility-scale solar by May 2026.
- UK: 22.8 GW total solar by June 2026. DESNZ estimated roughly 59% was ground-mounted, implying about 13.45 GW.
- Combined directional ground/utility base: about 15.0 GW.

The UK percentage is an official estimate applied to total capacity, not a precise site-level inventory. The Ireland and UK categories are not perfectly identical.

### Full O&M spending envelope

A current commercial European benchmark page reports utility-scale solar OPEX around €7–€13/kWp/year in several mature European markets. A 2023 Wood Mackenzie result reported an average US utility O&M contract price of $7.90/kW in 2022. Neither is an Irish quote.

Applying €7–€13/kWp/year only as a scenario gives:

- Ireland utility solar: approximately €11.1m–€20.7m annual full OPEX;
- Ireland plus estimated UK ground-mount: approximately €105m–€196m.

This is the total operating-cost envelope, not the revenue available to a software-led newcomer. It includes field maintenance, cleaning, vegetation, spares, insurance and other costs depending on the source and contract.

### Owner-side assurance envelope

At a hypothetical €500–€2,000 per MW per year for independent reporting, contractor assurance and data operations:

- Ireland TAM scenario: approximately €0.8m–€3.2m annually;
- Ireland plus UK ground-mount scenario: approximately €7.5m–€30.1m.

This is a useful niche service and potential software-enabled business, not proof of a giant Ireland-only SaaS market.

### Customer value test

At an 11% capacity factor and €70–€100/MWh realised value:

- a 10 MW site generates roughly 9.6 GWh and €0.67m–€0.96m annually; a 1% recovery is worth about €6.7k–€9.6k;
- a 50 MW site generates roughly 48.2 GWh and €3.37m–€4.82m annually; a 1% recovery is worth about €33.7k–€48.2k;
- a 100 MW site generates roughly 96.4 GWh and €6.75m–€9.64m annually; a 1% recovery is worth about €67.5k–€96.4k.

This makes independent assurance easier to justify for larger sites and portfolios. Small sites need shared portfolio pricing or additional administrative and compliance value.

### Test pricing, not a quote

- 60–90 day operational baseline and leakage audit: €4,000–€8,000 per site.
- Managed owner-side operations desk: €750 monthly base plus €20 per MW per month, with a sensible minimum.
- Example annual fees under that formula:
  - 10 MW: €11,400;
  - 20 MW: €13,800;
  - 50 MW: €21,000;
  - 100 MW: €33,000.
- Specialist field inspections, cleaning and vegetation: pass-through contractor cost plus a disclosed coordination or assurance fee.

Five 30 MW sites at the formula above produce about €81,000 annual recurring revenue. At a 55%–75% contribution margin before founder salaries and tax, that leaves roughly €44,500–€60,800. This is meaningful validation income, not yet a two-founder full-time company.

## Best initial customer

The first buyer should have:

- more than one operational site or a portfolio above roughly 20 MW;
- mixed portals, contractors or equipment;
- recurring manual monthly reporting;
- an O&M provider whose work still needs owner-side oversight;
- no desire to staff a full internal asset-management team;
- decision rights over data access;
- measurable downtime, reconciliation or evidence pain;
- willingness to fund a paid diagnostic before a software build.

Large integrated utilities may already have mature stacks and procurement barriers. Residential owners are too fragmented. The initial sweet spot is an independent Irish or UK owner, investment vehicle, developer retaining assets, or fund with a small-to-mid portfolio.

## Route to the first ten customers

Do not begin with cold national outreach or equipment purchases.

### Validation sequence

1. Run three confidential problem interviews through warm renewable contacts, subject to conflict approval.
2. Ask for redacted examples of the current monthly pack, alarm workflow, contractor ticket and unresolved discrepancy.
3. Quantify hours spent, faults missed, disputes, reporting delay and annual service spend.
4. Secure one paid 60–90 day diagnostic on historical/read-only data.
5. Demonstrate a before-and-after operating pack and at least one closed signal-to-action-to-verification loop.
6. Convert the first site to a 12-month owner-side desk contract without 24/7 or plant-control obligations.
7. Add a second site from the same owner before seeking unrelated customers.
8. Use the first reference to approach similar independent portfolio owners in Ireland.
9. Add UK portfolios through existing industry relationships or a field-service partner.
10. Only after repeated work, decide whether to licence an enterprise platform, keep a thin internal layer, or build reusable product components.

### The questions that matter

- Who looks at alarms today, and on what schedule?
- What cannot be seen from the current portal?
- How many portals and spreadsheets are used monthly?
- How are lost MWh, curtailment and grid downtime attributed?
- Can every alarm be linked to a completed work order and verified recovery?
- How long do warranty claims take and what evidence is commonly missing?
- Which contractor KPIs are disputed?
- What are the three most costly failures in the last 24 months?
- When was cleaning last approved, and what measured evidence justified it?
- What information does the board, lender or investor ask for that is hard to produce?
- What would the owner pay to remove the current reporting and contractor-control burden?

## 90-day no-build test

### Days 1–14

- Confirm conflict, confidentiality, moonlighting and IP boundaries.
- Conduct three to five owner interviews.
- Collect only redacted or explicitly approved materials.
- Map the existing data and operating workflow.

### Days 15–30

- Produce a manual baseline report using existing Evolv capabilities where ownership is clean.
- Build the asset, alarm and work-order evidence model in a simple database.
- Test generation/export reconciliation and anomaly triage on historical data.
- Price a paid pilot.

### Days 31–60

- Run the pilot manually with read-only access.
- Measure owner hours removed, issues surfaced, response delays, missing evidence and potential recovered value.
- Use partners for any field inspection.

### Days 61–90

- Convert to recurring monthly service.
- Refuse a custom software build unless the same requirement appears across at least three customers.
- Decide pursue, narrow or stop using payment and operational evidence.

## Kill criteria

Stop or materially narrow the idea if:

- owners say their current O&M provider and software already deliver trusted owner-side evidence;
- no buyer will pay for a diagnostic;
- the only pain is “dashboard aesthetics”;
- data access requires unsafe control privileges or breaches existing contracts;
- every customer needs bespoke integrations with no reusable core;
- a credible service requires 24/7 staffing before recurring revenue supports it;
- margins depend on claiming unmeasured energy recovery;
- the first five prospects want only low-frequency cleaning;
- the opportunity conflicts with Sam’s employment, confidentiality or IP obligations.

## Recommendation

**Pursue validation, not build-out.**

The working thesis should be:

> An independent owner-side operations and assurance layer for Irish and UK solar portfolios, connecting plant data, contractor work and field evidence so owners can find losses, prove action and run assets with fewer blind spots.

The initial innovation is the integrated operating loop and local delivery, not a new robot. Robotics, thermal drones, satellite imagery, vegetation management and cleaning become modular tools triggered by evidence. Wind comes later through an existing customer rather than as a second market launched in parallel.

This is a better founder wedge than seasonal mowing, but it still needs payment evidence. The next decision gate is not whether to code a dashboard. It is whether one warm portfolio owner will pay for an owner-side operational baseline and allow read-only data access.

## Governance and conflict boundary

Because Sam works with or has relationships involving renewable assets:

- review employment, consultancy, confidentiality, invention-assignment and moonlighting terms first;
- do not use Tempore, Evolv or client data, code, documents, credentials or contacts outside authorised scope;
- do not approach an employer-linked owner or vendor until the conflict position is explicit;
- keep new-company code and data in a separately documented environment;
- obtain written permission where the opportunity overlaps existing work;
- do not imply independent ownership of capabilities built for another company.
