# Evolv EPBD solar-trigger decision receipt

## Bounded proposal

Test whether Evolv can add a one-page, source-linked **EPBD solar-trigger decision receipt** to one real Irish commercial-building opportunity before design, roof works or purchasing decisions are fixed.

The receipt would record only:

- whether the building is new or existing, public or private, and non-residential or mixed-use;
- useful floor area, the permit or works trigger, and the relevant application or works date;
- which EU Article 10 threshold appears potentially relevant;
- the current Irish transposition source and whether national implementation criteria or exemptions are final;
- unresolved technical, structural, economic, functional, planning, grid and ownership questions;
- whether the existing SEAI Non-Domestic Microgen Scheme can apply; and
- one controlled outcome: `POTENTIAL EU TRIGGER — IRISH/PROFESSIONAL CONFIRMATION REQUIRED`, `MONITOR TRANSPOSITION`, or `NO TRIGGER ESTABLISHED ON RECORDED FACTS`.

This is not legal, planning, architectural, engineering or compliance advice. It must not say that a customer is currently obliged to install solar. The commercial question is narrower: **does an early, evidence-linked trigger screen expose a material design, timing or funding assumption before the ordinary solar quote would?**

If useful, this should become a small pre-design appendix to [[briefs/2026-09-23-evolv-commercial-solar-grant-grid-readiness-receipt]], not a new product, portal or compliance consultancy.

## Why this is genuinely new but bounded

Ground Zero contained EPBD context and a commercial Renovation Passport proposal, but no visible note matching the Article 10 dates, the 250 m² new non-residential threshold, Ireland's incomplete-transposition boundary or a solar-trigger screen when checked on 1 October 2026.

The existing grant-and-grid receipt starts from a proposed solar project. This proposal starts one decision earlier: whether a new building, major renovation or permitted roof/technical-system work may fall within a forthcoming solar obligation and whether the roof and evidence should be designed accordingly.

The evidence also exposes a useful funding boundary. SEAI's Non-Domestic Microgen Scheme guide version 1.4 says eligible premises must have been built and occupied on or before 31 December 2020. A new non-residential building potentially within the 2026 Article 10 timetable therefore cannot be assumed eligible for that grant.

## Verified evidence

- Article 10(1) of Directive (EU) 2024/1275 requires Member States to ensure that all new buildings are designed to optimise solar-generation potential so later solar installation is cost-effective.
- Article 10(3) sets EU implementation dates, subject to technical suitability and economic and functional feasibility: by 31 December 2026 for new public and non-residential buildings larger than 250 m²; by 31 December 2027 for existing public buildings larger than 2,000 m²; and by 31 December 2027 for existing non-residential buildings larger than 500 m² where a major renovation or permit-requiring building, roof or technical-system action occurs. Later dates apply to smaller public buildings, new residential buildings and adjacent roofed car parks.
- Article 10(4) requires each Member State to publish practical implementation criteria and possible building-type exemptions, taking account of technical and economic potential, building characteristics, structural integrity, green roofs and roof or attic insulation. The EU thresholds alone do not decide whether one Irish building is obliged to install solar.
- The European Commission's current explainer says a new business building above 250 m² is within the solar timetable where suitable and feasible, and presents the new-building trigger as applying to permit applications submitted from 1 January 2027. It also says existing business buildings above 500 m² enter the timetable when specified permit-requiring work occurs from 1 January 2028. National criteria still govern practical application.
- On 15 July 2026, the Commission opened infringement procedures against all 27 Member States for failure to fully transpose the recast EPBD by 29 May 2026. Its notice expressly includes solar energy in buildings among the new requirements.
- Ireland has partially transposed the Directive. S.I. No. 168/2026 expressly transposes parts of Articles 11 and 19, while S.I. No. 195/2026 expressly transposes Article 19(2). Neither instrument says it transposes Article 10.
- Ireland's Autumn 2026 Government Legislation Programme, current to 10 September, places the **Energy Performance of Buildings Directive Bill** in priority drafting with `Heads in preparation`. This is strong evidence that full Irish implementation remained unfinished at that cutoff, not evidence of the final Article 10 test.
- The current Irish non-residential BER changes introduce A0 and ZEB Renovate information, but those BER changes do not by themselves supply the missing Article 10 practical criteria or decide a building-specific solar obligation.
- SEAI's Non-Domestic Microgen Scheme Application Guide version 1.4, dated 16 July 2026, supports systems up to 1 MWp but says the premises must have been built and occupied on or before 31 December 2020, requires grant approval before works, and leaves necessary permissions and statutory approvals with the applicant.

These sources establish a real and time-sensitive decision boundary. They do **not** establish that any current Evolv prospect is covered, that Ireland will implement every option exactly as the Commission explainer describes, that a specific roof is suitable, or that a buyer will pay for this receipt.

## Assumptions to falsify

- Evolv has one current or warm, permissioned opportunity involving a new non-residential building above 250 m² or an existing non-residential building above 500 m² facing renovation, roof or technical-system work.
- The building's classification, floor area and permit or works date can be established from authoritative project records without Evolv making a legal determination.
- The ordinary architect, planning, BER or solar workflow does not already provide an equivalent dated Article 10 and funding-boundary record.
- A decision maker would use the result to preserve roof load, orientation, routes, metering space, programme or evidence before those choices become expensive to change.
- A qualified project professional can confirm the Irish legal and technical boundary without the receipt becoming a substitute design or compliance service.
- The additional screen can be prepared manually in no more than 30 minutes once the approved project facts are available.

## Smallest validation test

Do not access a client file, contact a professional or attach this to a quote until Sam approves the exact opportunity and the conflict, confidentiality and IP boundary.

If a suitable opportunity exists:

1. Freeze the official sources by title, version or publication date, URL and retrieval date. Re-check for a published Irish EPBD Bill, regulations, guidance and national Article 10 criteria immediately before using the receipt.
2. In an approved client workspace, capture only the minimum project facts: controlled site ID, building use, new/existing status, public/private ownership, useful floor area, permit/application date, proposed roof or technical-system work, and current design stage. Keep addresses, drawings, credentials, quotes and client records out of Ground Zero.
3. Map those facts to the EU timetable, but label the result `potential` until the current Irish implementing measure and project professional confirm it.
4. Record, without deciding, the unresolved suitability fields: structure, usable roof area, green roof or insulation interaction, planning or Solar Safeguarding Zone, electrical demand, grid route, ownership and economics.
5. Separate funding from obligation. Check the live NDMG rules and show `not established` or `apparently outside the pre-2021 premises gate` where appropriate; do not turn regulatory timing into a grant or savings promise.
6. Compare the receipt with the ordinary quote and [[briefs/2026-09-23-evolv-commercial-solar-grant-grid-readiness-receipt]]. Record whether it exposes a material design, permit, timing or funding assumption that neither document otherwise catches.
7. With separate approval, ask the project's architect, assigned certifier, planning adviser or other qualified owner-side professional to confirm only the building classification, trigger date and applicable Irish requirement. Technical solar suitability remains with the competent design team.
8. Return one outcome: **discard**, **retain as a manual pre-design appendix**, or **monitor Irish transposition and retest one named project**. Do not build a rules engine, customer portal, compliance database or automated prospect list.

### Pass gate

Retain the manual appendix only if:

- every project fact and regulatory statement is tied to a dated source or visibly unresolved;
- the current Irish implementing source and national criteria are identified before any customer reliance;
- a qualified project professional confirms the applicability boundary;
- the receipt catches at least one material design, timing, permit or funding assumption before it becomes costly to change;
- preparation takes no more than 30 additional minutes; and
- the buyer or delivery lead says the result changed a real next decision.

A summary that merely repeats the EU timetable, implies mandatory solar from the Directive alone, or generates a sales lead without a project decision does not pass.

### Kill gate

Discard or park the wedge if there is no suitable live opportunity, Ireland's final rules make the screen irrelevant, the project's existing professional team already provides an equivalent receipt, the building is clearly outside the trigger, no decision changes, or the legal/professional review burden exceeds the value of an early solar conversation.

## Downside and safeguards

- **Legal and transposition risk:** the EU timetable is not a complete Irish building-specific rule. Ireland's criteria, exemptions, measurement basis and permit treatment can change the result. Never advertise “solar is mandatory” from this note.
- **Technical-suitability risk:** floor area and project type do not establish structural, fire, roof, electrical, grid or economic feasibility.
- **Funding confusion:** a regulatory trigger does not create grant eligibility. The current NDMG pre-2021 premises condition may exclude the new-building cases nearest the first deadline.
- **Timing drift:** the Commission explainer expresses dates through permit submissions after the deadline, while the Directive lets Member States define practical criteria. Re-check final Irish text at use time.
- **Professional-boundary risk:** Evolv can assemble evidence and flag questions; it cannot replace the architect, assigned certifier, BER assessor, planning adviser, structural engineer, electrical designer or lawyer.
- **Commercial-pressure risk:** a compliance-sounding receipt can become fear-based selling. It must lead with uncertainty, suitability and confirmation, not urgency or penalties.
- **Priority drift:** this remains subordinate to the real rooftop sale, the paid 60–90 day owner-side baseline in [[project_state/renew]] and [[items/renew-reporting-source-baseline]].
