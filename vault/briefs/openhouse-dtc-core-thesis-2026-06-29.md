# OpenHouse DTC core thesis

## Decision

DTC should no longer be treated only as a backup route. It should be treated as a core company thesis that runs in parallel with developer, funding, UK and US channel work.

Developer sales remain useful, especially for new-build data and institutional proof, but the larger venture-scale opportunity is retroactively creating a living home model for any home after handover.

## Core belief

Every home will eventually have an agent that knows the property, its systems, documents, devices, warranties, history, energy behaviour, and homeowner needs.

Cars have become intelligent. Work has become intelligent. Devices have become intelligent. The home is the largest purchase most people ever make, yet it is still handed over with PDFs, paper folders, scattered manuals, and unread documentation.

OpenHouse should aim to be the first premium, usable, consumer-grade home agent.

## Why DTC matters

Developer-led new-build distribution is powerful but slow and politically difficult, especially in Ireland. Direct-to-consumer unlocks every existing home, not just future developer completions.

The DTC opportunity is harder because context acquisition and consumer distribution are difficult, but it is more sustainable if the product becomes genuinely compelling.

DTC expands the market from:

> homes controlled by developers willing to cooperate

into:

> every homeowner who wants their home to become easier, cheaper, safer, and smarter to run

## The DTC promise

OpenHouse should become the homeowner partner that helps with:

- reducing electricity bills
- understanding energy usage
- heat pump, solar, EV, battery and tariff advice
- bills and smart-meter interpretation
- maintenance reminders
- warranty and manual retrieval
- diagnosing issues from photos
- explaining how to fix simple household tasks
- storing the home’s documents and history
- creating a living home file that persists across years

The house-context layer can deepen over time. The first wedge does not need perfect context from day one. It needs enough value around energy, maintenance, documents, photos and reminders that people return and gradually build the home model.

## Product principle

Do not wait until OpenHouse knows everything about a home before making it useful.

Start with high-frequency / high-anxiety homeowner problems:

1. Why is my bill high?
2. Is my heat pump working properly?
3. What does this warning light / noise / crack / leak mean?
4. What should I do this month to maintain the house?
5. Where is the manual / warranty / receipt?
6. Can I fix this myself or do I need a tradesperson?
7. What upgrade would actually save me money?
8. What should I ask a contractor before I pay them?

Then use every interaction to build the living home model.

## Data compounding thesis

The long-term asset is not just documents. It is the behavioural and operational corpus of homes:

- what homeowners ask
- which systems confuse them
- which issues repeat
- what breaks after handover
- which documents are missing
- what energy patterns produce bill shock
- which maintenance tasks get ignored
- which advice prevents escalations

This creates a unique dataset around home operation.

That dataset can later support:

- better energy intelligence
- better product recommendations
- better warranty / defect prediction
- better insurance / mortgage / retrofit evidence
- better homeowner support automation
- future robotics context, because household robots will need structured knowledge of the home, rooms, devices, objects and routines

## DTC positioning

Avoid:

> Ask anything about your house.

This sounds generic and ChatGPT-like.

Use:

> Give your home a brain.

or:

> The AI partner for running your home.

or:

> Your home’s memory, maintenance brain and energy coach.

or:

> Turn your home from a folder of papers into an intelligent assistant.

## Strategic stance

OpenHouse should pursue three routes at once:

1. **Funding / legitimacy** — NDRC, EI PSSF, HBAN, Furthr, strategic backers.
2. **B2B channels** — Ireland proof, UK aftercare/compliance, US builder warranty discovery.
3. **DTC core product** — energy + maintenance + home memory wedge for existing homes.

The DTC route should get more product attention immediately because it represents the largest market and reduces dependency on conservative developers.

## Near-term DTC build direction

The first DTC version should not try to be the complete home agent. It should be a wedge that feels obviously useful within minutes.

Recommended first package:

### Home Energy + Maintenance Copilot

Core modules:
- bill upload / bill explainer
- tariff and usage advice
- heat pump / solar / EV / battery context capture
- appliance/manual/warranty scanner
- photo issue diagnosis
- maintenance schedule
- document vault
- home profile completeness score
- “what should I do next?” weekly/monthly assistant

Artifacts:
- Home Health Snapshot
- 90-day home action plan
- Energy savings checklist
- Warranty / maintenance timeline
- Contractor brief for bigger work

## Key risk

The DTC product must be more than a chat interface. Generic AI will absorb shallow Q&A. OpenHouse wins only if it has persistence, source-backed context, reminders, structured memory, local rules/data, exportable artifacts and a premium homeowner experience.
