# Primary entry

## Digital Transformation / AI Leader of the Year

### Question 1: Executive Track Record & Enterprise Impact

Maximum 350 words. Draft: 336 words.

I am the founder and CEO of OpenHouse AI, but my route into digital transformation came from operating inside residential development rather than from a conventional technology career.

I am one of three people running Longview Estates, an Irish property developer that has delivered more than 200 homes. Our current development programme includes 753 homes, with a further 260-plus homes in planning, as well as retail, healthcare, schools and a crèche. My role spans financing, design-team coordination, sales, handover, aftercare and day-to-day development operations. That gave me a direct view of a recurring problem: valuable information is created while a home is designed and built, but it is often fragmented across drawings, specifications, manuals, emails and individual people by the time the homeowner needs it.

I began building OpenHouse in October 2025 to change that. I designed and led the transition from static handover information towards a living digital record for each home. The platform gives homeowners a practical assistant based on their own home record, while giving the developer a structured view of recurring questions and information gaps. It is not a generic chatbot placed on top of documents. The transformation is the creation of an operating feedback loop between construction knowledge, homeowner support and developer learning.

I have built the product largely solo, using AI development tools under my own product, technical and governance responsibility, while continuing my operating role at Longview. It is now used across four Longview developments. After removing an identifiable internal QA outlier, a read-only production audit recorded 259 property-specific questions and corresponding answers across 79 home contexts, including 245 questions typed by users rather than selected from built-in prompts.

The measurable impact at this stage is service innovation and organisational capability rather than claimed cost savings. OpenHouse has turned information that previously ended at handover into a live service and a new source of operational insight. My next responsibility is to prove that impact with an external developer and measure changes in repeat enquiries, response quality and onboarding time.

### Question 2: Leadership Judgement, Governance & People Impact

Maximum 250 words. Draft: 232 words.

My leadership approach is to begin with the operating problem, make a responsible change, and keep a human accountable for the outcome.

OpenHouse deals with homes, personal information and questions that can affect safety, warranties and household costs. I have therefore treated governance as part of the product. The assistant is designed to use the relevant home record, state uncertainty, distinguish guidance from confirmed fact and escalate when a qualified person or developer decision is required. Consent, source provenance, role-based access and human override are design requirements.

I have also led a methodical security and release-readiness programme. Privileged actions are being moved behind verified role and tenant boundaries, purchaser access is bound to validated credentials, and releases are held when the evidence does not support the claim. This has meant slowing launches and refusing to present promising prototypes as production-ready. I see that as leadership judgement, not lost momentum.

The people impact comes from translating technology into workflows that property teams and homeowners can use. I have built around existing handover and aftercare processes rather than asking people to adopt AI for its own sake. Developers can see recurring topics and missing information; homeowners receive help in the context of their property. This builds capability on both sides without removing human responsibility.

As sole founder, I remain directly accountable for product decisions, privacy boundaries, technical quality and every claim the company makes.

### Question 3: Strategic Execution & Digital Innovation

Maximum 250 words. Draft: 239 words.

Over the past 12 months I have taken OpenHouse from a first-hand operational problem to a live AI product used across four residential developments.

The core innovation is a living digital record for each home. Construction-stage information is structured around the development, house type and property. The homeowner can ask practical questions based on that record, while the developer sees aggregate topics and knowledge gaps rather than losing every interaction across separate emails and calls. The same architecture supports document access and structured issue intake.

I built the platform largely solo while working full-time in property development. OpenHouse AI Limited was incorporated in February 2026 and has been self-funded with approximately €10,000. It has no outside investment and no revenue yet, so I separate operational proof from commercial traction.

The strongest production evidence is a conservative read-only audit completed on 30 July 2026. After excluding one identifiable internal QA home, the assistant had answered 259 property-specific questions across 79 home contexts in four Longview developments. Of those, 245 were typed rather than chosen from built-in prompts, with activity across 38 calendar days.

I have also built the developer workflow for scheme setup, home imports, document organisation and homeowner access, while subjecting the product to security and release-readiness review. The next milestone is commercial and measurable: onboard one external developer scheme, time the full setup, and measure repeat enquiries, response quality and support demand before claiming broader productivity gains.

### Question 4: Distinction & Broader Leadership Contribution

Maximum 250 words. Draft: 233 words.

What distinguishes my leadership is the combination of direct responsibility for a large physical development programme and hands-on responsibility for building the technology intended to improve how homes are handed over and supported.

I am not approaching housing as an external software vendor. I work across the financing, design, sales, handover and aftercare of a large development. I have seen where information is created and where it is lost. I then taught myself the product, AI and software skills to build and operate the response rather than remaining at strategy level.

I can test AI decisions against the realities of a development site, homeowners and a developer's governance obligations, then hold the system to clear evidence and safety boundaries.

OpenHouse is still early, and I do not claim external revenue or savings that have not been measured. The broader contribution is a practical Irish model for responsible AI in the built environment: preserve authoritative construction information as a living home record, help residents understand their property, and turn privacy-safe patterns into better service and future housing decisions.

I have self-funded the company, built the product largely during evenings and weekends, and progressed from an internal operating problem to a live deployment and an NDRC pre-accelerator interview. The formal NDRC decision remains pending. My focus now is converting that proof into an external developer result that can be independently measured and repeated.

My people approach is equally practical. I build around how developers, sales teams and homeowners already work. The goal is not to replace people with AI. It is to remove avoidable information loss, give staff a clearer view of recurring demand and help homeowners get a useful first answer before a human intervention is needed.

I have also exercised judgement about focus. OpenHouse has several credible extensions, but the immediate priority is one buyer and one measurable developer handover and aftercare loop. That protects limited capital and makes every product decision answerable to a real result.
