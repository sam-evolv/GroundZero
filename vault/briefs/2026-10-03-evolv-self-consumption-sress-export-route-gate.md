# Evolv self-consumption versus SRESS export-only route gate

## Bounded proposal

Test whether Evolv should add a one-page **self-consumption versus SRESS export-only route receipt** before quoting or fixing the design of one real Irish solar opportunity greater than 50 kW whose commercial route is genuinely unresolved.

The receipt would record only:

- the proposed applicant and ownership class, site, technology and capacity band;
- whether the buyer's primary job is to reduce on-site electricity purchases or to export all generation as a separate income-producing project;
- the minimum permissioned load, generation and roof/site facts needed to establish whether that distinction is material;
- the current self-consumer NDMG/CEG route and its live governing documents;
- the current SRESS export-only route, tariff category and review status;
- the applicable grid path, planning evidence and project-stage blockers; and
- one controlled outcome: `SELF-CONSUMER ROUTE FITS RECORDED PURPOSE`, `EXPORT-ONLY FEASIBILITY REVIEW MAY BE WARRANTED`, or `NO ROUTE ESTABLISHED — PROFESSIONAL CONFIRMATION REQUIRED`.

This is not an application, financial model, investment recommendation, PPA, planning or grid opinion, and it must not claim that SRESS improves one project's economics. The commercial question is narrower: **can a dated route-choice receipt prevent Evolv or a buyer from treating a self-consumption grant/CEG project and an export-only 15-year tariff project as interchangeable before design and connection decisions harden?**

If useful, this should become a short front-end section of [[briefs/2026-09-23-evolv-commercial-solar-grant-grid-readiness-receipt]], not a new product, portal or SRESS consultancy.

## Why this is genuinely new but subordinate

A vault-wide text check on 3 October 2026 found no existing SRESS or export-only route note. The existing grant-and-grid receipt assumes a self-consumption commercial rooftop, while [[briefs/2026-10-02-evolv-ndmg-grant-uplift-transition-gate]] addresses a temporary change within that route. Neither distinguishes the mutually exclusive project models now stated on the Department's live SRESS page.

The new evidence establishes a consequential fork:

- the self-consumer route is for electricity used by the farm, business or community building, with surplus eligible for storage or sale through the CEG subject to the governing rules; and
- the SRESS export-tariff route requires the project to export all generated electricity, cannot use generation for the site's own demand, is not eligible for CEG, and does not accept autoproducer applications.

The distinction can affect design, metering, connection, planning, ownership, PPA and timing before price comparison is meaningful. It does not justify diverting Evolv from its current owner-side self-consumption and assurance thesis. The July-recorded Renew state is stale, so this note does not assert that the live install, the site in survey or any warm prospect is greater than 50 kW or suitable for SRESS.

## Verified evidence

- The Department's SRESS page was last updated on 12 June 2026. It describes two options and says the self-consumer grant and export tariff are not available to the same project. An export-tariff project must export all generated electricity, cannot use it for its own consumption and is not eligible for the Clean Export Guarantee.
- The same page says the self-consumer route is available through the Non-Domestic Microgen Grant for renewable self-consumers above 50 kW and up to 1 MW, while surplus may be stored or sold if electricity generation is not the applicant's primary business.
- The current SEAI NDMG Application Guide, Version 1.4 dated 16 July 2026, says the installation must be at a self-consumption site with electricity demand and is not for an export-only grid-connected project. It also currently requires the premises to have been built and occupied on or before 31 December 2020 and requires a grant offer before purchase or works. The announced NDMG uplift and transition rules remain separately unresolved in [[briefs/2026-10-02-evolv-ndmg-grant-uplift-transition-gate]].
- The Department says the SRESS export tariff is open for export-only solar projects greater than 50 kW and up to 1 MW regardless of applicant type, and for eligible SME, farmer and Renewable Energy Community projects up to 6 MW. It opened on 27 January 2025 and provides a 15-year, non-auction, two-way feed-in premium.
- At the 3 October cutoff, the live page publishes SME solar rates of €130/MWh for projects greater than 50 kW and up to 1 MW and €120/MWh above 1 MW and up to 6 MW. The REC rates shown are €150/MWh and €140/MWh respectively. These are current published support rates, not a project revenue forecast or proof of eligibility.
- The Department began a SRESS tariff review in February 2026 and says current rates remain available until the review ends; rates may be retained, modified or removed. Its page says that, as of June 2026, more than 50% of capacity remained in every tariff category. Neither statement guarantees capacity or a rate for a later application.
- The SRESS 1 Terms and Conditions say applications are not accepted for autoproducer projects. They require new equipment, and define a New Project to include at least €300/kW of project investment made after receipt of the Letter of Offer. They also prohibit concurrent or prior support from another PSO-funded scheme for the same project.
- For SME and other export projects, the SRESS Terms require an accepted grid-connection offer and a Grid Connection Agreement at application. The Department's practical guide says projects below 200 kW use the small-scale-generation route and cannot have maximum export capacity above the site's maximum import capacity; projects above 200 kW use the ECP route.
- The SRESS Terms require separate official metering and all project generation to pass across the project meter. They also require a licensed supplier and PPA for the support mechanism.
- There is an official-source tension that must remain open: the Department's practical guide says some rooftop solar may be exempt from planning permission, while the current SRESS Terms and application materials require full and final planning permission and a planning reference. The receipt must not resolve that conflict by inference; current written confirmation is required before reliance.

These sources establish a real route-choice boundary. They do **not** establish that a current Evolv opportunity is eligible, that either route is financially superior, that the published tariff will survive the review unchanged, that grid capacity or planning evidence exists, or that a buyer will pay for this receipt.

## Assumptions to falsify

- Evolv has one real, permissioned Irish opportunity greater than 50 kW whose purpose has not already been fixed as self-consumption or export-only.
- The opportunity is early enough that no purchase, works, grid commitment, grant step or support application makes the route comparison academic or unsafe.
- Minimum interval/load evidence and a credible generation estimate can establish whether on-site use or full export is the real commercial job without collecting unnecessary customer data.
- The applicant, project ownership and capacity band can be established without Evolv making a legal, SME-status or community-status determination.
- The ordinary installer, energy consultant, planning adviser, grid adviser, supplier or financier does not already provide an equivalent dated route record.
- A competent adviser can resolve the planning-permission conflict and confirm the applicable grid and support route before any customer reliance.
- The receipt changes a real design, evidence or go/no-go decision rather than adding paperwork to an already obvious self-consumption project.

## Smallest validation test

Do not access a client file, contact any authority or attach this to a quotation until Sam approves the exact opportunity and the conflict, confidentiality and IP boundary.

If a suitable opportunity exists:

1. Freeze the governing public sources by title, visible version/date, URL and retrieval date. Re-check the SRESS tariff review, capacity statement, terms, application pack and NDMG rules immediately before use.
2. In an approved client workspace, capture only a controlled site ID, proposed applicant/owner class, technology, proposed capacity, project stage, building age/status, planning status, MIC/MEC or known connection stage, one representative load period and one versioned generation estimate. Keep addresses, MPRNs, credentials, complete bills, drawings, quotes and personal data out of Ground Zero.
3. Record the buyer's actual job in its own words: reduce on-site imports, monetise an export-only asset, or unresolved. Do not infer export-only intent from unused roof area or surplus generation.
4. Map the recorded purpose to the two official route definitions. Treat self-consumption plus surplus export as distinct from a SRESS project that exports all generation.
5. Build no headline ROI comparison. Instead, list the inputs a competent adviser would need: avoided import value, current CEG terms, current SRESS tariff and review status, generation, curtailment/export limits, connection and metering cost, PPA/supplier terms, planning, financing, tax, maintenance and support interactions. Mark every missing value `unknown`.
6. For the SRESS route, record the applicant category, new-project timing, grid agreement, planning evidence, site control, separate metering, PPA and all-export requirements. Preserve the official planning-source conflict rather than deciding it.
7. Compare the receipt with the ordinary quote and the existing grant-and-grid receipt. Record whether it prevents an incompatible route assumption, exposes a material missing dependency or changes the next professional question.
8. Return one outcome: **discard**, **retain as a manual pre-quote gate**, or **seek separately approved professional confirmation for this one project**. Do not apply, contact, purchase, build software or market SRESS from one case.

### Pass gate

Retain the manual gate only if:

- every project fact and scheme statement is source-linked or visibly unresolved;
- the current governing documents, tariff-review status and project stage are rechecked at use time;
- a competent energy/grid adviser and, where relevant, planning and financial/tax advisers confirm the route before reliance;
- the receipt catches at least one material design, connection, timing, support or ownership assumption before commitment;
- preparation takes no more than 45 additional minutes after approved records are available; and
- the buyer or delivery lead says the result changed a real next decision.

A generic SRESS explainer, an unsupported tariff comparison or a polished document with no changed decision does not pass.

### Kill gate

Discard or park the wedge if there is no suitable live opportunity; the project is 50 kW or below; the buyer clearly wants on-site self-consumption; purchase or works have already closed the New Project/support timing question; grid, planning, PPA or ownership constraints make export-only infeasible; an existing professional already supplies the same route record; no decision changes; or the advisory burden exceeds the value of one early classification.

## Downside and safeguards

- **False equivalence:** avoided retail electricity cost, CEG surplus payments and a two-way SRESS tariff are different economic objects. Do not compare headline rates as though they were interchangeable revenue.
- **Self-use breach:** SRESS export projects cannot use generated electricity for their own consumption. The receipt must not describe ordinary surplus export as SRESS eligibility.
- **Tariff drift:** the 2026 review may retain, change or remove categories. Re-check before every use and never promise the current rate or remaining capacity.
- **Grid feasibility:** below 200 kW, MEC cannot exceed MIC under the cited small-scale route; above 200 kW, the ECP path applies. A paper classification is not a connection offer.
- **Planning conflict:** current practical guidance and binding application materials are not aligned on rooftop exemptions. Obtain current written confirmation; do not invent a workaround.
- **Premature commitment:** SRESS New Project timing and NDMG pre-purchase rules differ. Do not purchase, start works, cancel or switch an application from this receipt.
- **PPA and supplier risk:** a 15-year support headline is not a completed PPA, supplier offer, settlement model or bankable revenue stream.
- **Data minimisation:** load profiles and site economics can reveal commercial operations. Use minimum permissioned records and keep client data outside Ground Zero.
- **Priority drift:** this gate remains subordinate to a real rooftop sale, [[items/renew-reporting-source-baseline]] and the paid 60–90 day owner-side baseline in [[project_state/renew]].
