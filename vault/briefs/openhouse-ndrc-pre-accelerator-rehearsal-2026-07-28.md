# OpenHouse NDRC pre-accelerator interview rehearsal

## Interview format

- Three-minute pitch with slides.
- Twelve-minute panel Q&A.
- Panel: Dushyant Singh, Deirbhile Gorman and Joe Gorman.

## Core pitch

OpenHouse helps residential developers deliver a better handover and reduce aftercare burden. Every homeowner receives a home-specific assistant grounded in the documents, systems and context of their actual home. Developers receive privacy-safe evidence of what homeowners need, what is unclear and where recurring aftercare problems are emerging.

## Strongest proof points

- Sam is a working Cork property developer on a 750-home scheme, with roughly 200 homes completed.
- OpenHouse was born from the real handover problem: expensive, unread paper folders and repeated homeowner questions after key collection.
- The current product is live across roughly 150 homes in Sam's own scheme.
- One homeowner had 77 assistant interactions on their first day, a depth of engagement that would never occur through email or phone support.
- The developer price anchor is €99 per home, deliberately below the approximate €100 cost of producing a paper handover pack.
- The product can turn repeated homeowner questions into privacy-safe operational insight, improved documentation and better future handovers.

## What to say in three minutes

1. **Founder and problem** - Sam builds homes and saw that key handover is still a folder of PDFs that buyers do not read.
2. **Product** - OpenHouse turns the handover into a home-specific assistant. A homeowner can ask about their own documents, rooms, systems, warranties and common aftercare concerns.
3. **Developer value** - fewer repeated aftercare questions, better homeowner confidence, clearer evidence of engagement and a feedback loop showing recurring documentation, system or handover gaps.
4. **Why now** - new homes increasingly contain heat pumps, underfloor heating, ventilation, solar and EV infrastructure that first-time buyers are not equipped to operate confidently.
5. **Proof and ask** - live founder deployment, early engagement, and the need to turn founder proof into a repeatable developer-sales motion.

## Important framing

- Lead with developers as the next six-month customer and commercial focus.
- Present direct-to-consumer as a future extension of the same Home Record, not as a hedge if developers do not buy.
- The moat is the permissioned, structured Home Record and the workflow built around it, not the language model.
- Describe learning as privacy-safe, aggregated patterns that improve support and documentation. Do not use "data is the new oil".
- Do not claim the assistant guarantees savings, diagnoses a defect conclusively from a photo or has autonomous device control. It offers grounded guidance, confidence states and escalation when appropriate.

## Likely panel questions and concise answer shape

### Why does a developer pay?

Developers pay for a better handover and lower aftercare burden. OpenHouse handles repeated homeowner questions, captures structured issue evidence and shows where the handover, documents or systems are causing confusion. It is also cheaper than the existing paper handover pack at the starting price point.

### What traction do you have?

The product is live in Sam's own scheme across approximately 150 homes. The founder environment is being used deliberately to harden the product before external rollout. The next proof is one external paid developer, then reuse on that developer's second scheme.

### What is the six-month milestone?

Land one external paid developer, expand to a second scheme or repeat developer use, and grow from approximately 150 to 500 active homes. Measure engagement, repeated-question deflection, verified aftercare outcomes and the developer's decision to reuse the product.

### Who is the competition?

Existing portals and defect tools manage parts of the process. General AI can answer a question, but it does not own the permissioned, structured facts of a specific home. OpenHouse can later be accessed through whichever general assistant a homeowner prefers; its durable value is the trusted Home Record and workflow.

### How is privacy protected?

The household record is private by default. Developer insight is aggregated and privacy-safe, with opt-in consent for use of homeowner questions in aggregate analysis. Individual household behaviour is not sold or exposed as a developer surveillance product.

### What does Sam need from NDRC?

Help turning founder-built proof into a focused, repeatable and investable company: sharpen the first commercial offer, build a disciplined developer-sales route, validate pricing and milestones, and create a credible path to go full-time.

## Rehearsal feedback

### Keep

- The authentic founder story and the "folder of PDFs" image.
- The 77 first-day interactions example.
- The A-rated home versus A-rated homeowner framing, used carefully and without blaming homeowners.
- The “cheaper than paper” price anchor.

### Cut or shorten

- Maps, noticeboards and broad feature lists from the main pitch.
- Detailed consumer subscription mechanics from the first three minutes.
- UK and US expansion until the core Irish developer motion is clear.
- Personal burnout and employer-conflict detail. State the professional need for a path from founder proof to full-time execution instead.
- Claims that revenue is unimportant. Commercial proof is material to the panel.
