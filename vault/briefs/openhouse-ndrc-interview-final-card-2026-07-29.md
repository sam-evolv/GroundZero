### Slide 1: Built from inside the problem

Good afternoon. I am Sam Donworth, the sole founder of OpenHouse.

I work directly in residential development and was part of a three-person team that took Longview Estates from greenfield to more than 200 completed homes within a planned development of over 750 homes.

That gave me direct exposure to sales, construction, handover and aftercare. I kept seeing the same structural problem.

While a home is being built, the developer knows almost everything about it. The drawings, systems, warranties, specifications and decisions all exist. But at handover, that context fragments into PDFs, manuals, emails and individual people.

The homeowner receives the keys, but not a home they can properly understand.

That is where OpenHouse came from.

### Slide 2: A home that can explain itself

I started by building a better digital handover and homeowner-support experience.

OpenHouse now creates a structured, living record for each home. Homeowners can ask practical questions about their actual property, documents and installed systems. Developers get a clearer handover process, structured issue evidence and visibility into where information or aftercare is repeatedly failing.

I built the product largely solo using AI-assisted development. It is operationally live across four founder-affiliated developments, covering 281 home records and 143 timestamped handover agreements. Eighty-five homes are linked to homeowner accounts.

I am careful about what that proves. It proves I can build and deploy the product in a real operating environment. It does not yet prove that an unrelated developer will pay.

The initial business model is simple: developers pay from €99 per home, beginning with handover and aftercare.

The deeper insight is that the chatbot is not the asset. The structured, permissioned Home Record is the asset. The assistant is how the homeowner uses it.

### Slide 3: Useful for the life of the home

The long-term opportunity is for every home to have a living record created when the best information is available and retained throughout ownership.

It begins with handover, then becomes useful for questions, maintenance, warranties, upgrades and eventually energy intelligence.

The next commercial milestone is deliberately narrower: sign the first independent paid developer, deploy on a defined scheme and prove one measurable reduction in handover or aftercare workload.

That is why I want NDRC.

I do not need help generating more product ideas. I need disciplined commercial challenge, a stronger developer-sales route and the external network to turn founder-built proof into a repeatable company.

OpenHouse knows the home and helps the homeowner run it properly.

## Likely Q&A

### What exactly does a developer buy?

A per-home handover and aftercare system. OpenHouse organises the home record, gives the homeowner grounded support and structures issue evidence for the developer.

### What is the price?

The starting anchor is €99 per home. The external pilot will test the final package and pricing against developer workload, paper-pack cost and aftercare value.

### Who is the buyer?

Initially the residential developer, likely through customer care, sales, operations or the managing director depending on company size.

### What problem is painful enough to create budget?

Repeated handover administration, fragmented documentation, recurring homeowner questions, weak issue evidence and avoidable aftercare handling.

### What is the six-month milestone?

One independent paid developer, a defined live scheme, repeat use or expansion, and one measured operational outcome.

### Who is the competition?

Developer portals and defect tools solve pieces of the workflow. Generic AI can answer general questions but does not own the permissioned, structured facts of a specific home. Foundation is the closest product overlap and proves the category is real. OpenHouse must win through the developer handover wedge, trusted source-backed answers and the persistent Home Record.

### Why will developers distribute it?

They already control the handover moment and need to transfer information to every purchaser. OpenHouse improves that required workflow and gives them a better aftercare channel.

### Why will homeowners use it?

It answers practical, home-specific questions at the moment of need. The product should not depend on habitual daily engagement. Utility at high-friction moments is the relevant behaviour.

### What evidence of homeowner use do you have?

There are 85 homes linked to homeowner accounts. One homeowner generated 77 assistant interactions on their first day. Treat that as a useful behavioural example, not proof of retention or broad product-market fit.

### What are you measuring next?

Activation, useful questions, repeat use, grounded answer rate, issue submissions, developer handling time and the developer's decision to reuse OpenHouse on another scheme.

### Why not sell directly to homeowners?

The developer has the cleanest distribution and the best source information at handover. Direct-to-consumer can later extend the same Home Record, but it is not the first commercial focus.

### What stops ChatGPT or a portal vendor copying this?

The moat is not access to a language model. It is the permissioned home-specific record, ingestion and verification workflow, developer distribution, provenance, safety controls and accumulated structured context around each home.

### How do you protect privacy?

The household record is private by default. Developer insights are aggregated and privacy-safe. Individual household behaviour is not exposed or sold as a surveillance product.

### How do you avoid unsafe AI answers?

The assistant is grounded in source material, distinguishes confidence, clarifies ambiguity and escalates unsupported or safety-sensitive questions. It should not diagnose defects conclusively or invent specifications.

### Why are you solo?

The current evidence shows unusually high solo execution. Do not defend being solo as inherently superior. Say the next hires or cofounder decision will be driven by the commercial and technical bottleneck, and that NDRC can help identify where complementary strength creates the most leverage.

### Are you full-time?

Answer the present legal and employment position exactly. Then say the goal is to reach the commercial proof and funding required to make OpenHouse the clear full-time path. Do not give a vague commitment you cannot honour.

### How large can this become?

New-build handover is the wedge, not the ceiling. A trusted Home Record can become infrastructure used by developers, homeowners and future service providers across the life of a property. Keep the answer tied to a believable sequence rather than generic global TAM.

### What will NDRC change?

A sharper commercial offer, independent customer access, disciplined milestones, investor readiness and a credible route from founder proof to a focused company.
