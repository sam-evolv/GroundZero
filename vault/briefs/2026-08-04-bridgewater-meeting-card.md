# Bridgewater meeting card

## The outcome

Understand Bridgewater's current handover and aftercare workflow, show only the relevant OpenHouse flow, and agree one concrete next step.

Best next step: a second workflow session with the operational owner using one real upcoming scheme.

## Opening

> Thanks for making the time. Carol mentioned she shared the interview, so rather than repeat all of that, I will give you the short version. I am one of three people running Longview Estates and I built OpenHouse from dealing directly with sales, handover and homeowner questions across our own developments. It gives each completed home one living record for its documents, systems, warranties and history, with a homeowner interface and developer visibility behind it. I would like to understand how Bridgewater currently handles that journey and then show you the parts that are actually relevant.

## Ask these first

1. Can you walk me through what happens from sale agreed to the homeowner receiving the keys?
2. Who assembles the final handover information and what does the buyer receive?
3. Where do homeowner questions, snags and warranty requests go after completion?
4. Who triages, assigns and closes those actions?
5. How are homeowners updated and what evidence is needed before an item is regarded as resolved?
6. Is the workflow consistent across schemes and selling agents?
7. Which part creates the most repeated work or customer friction?
8. Which upcoming scheme would give us the cleanest place to test an improvement?

Follow the real friction. Do not force every question.

## Show this

1. One authorised homeowner entering one exact home.
2. The home's documents, systems, warranties and history.
3. One certified home-specific question, preferably the AquaBox warranty-date example.
4. Developer visibility into recurring question topics and missing information.
5. Photo or aftercare evidence only if David says defect or contractor coordination is a real problem.

Do not show the broad Agent portal, Care, inactive My Home V2 energy features or a generic feature tour.

## Proof points

- Four Longview developments with operating data.
- 259 property-specific questions across 79 home contexts.
- 245 questions were typed rather than selected from built-in prompts.
- The controlled exact-home reviewer environment contained 59 documents and one video.
- The reviewer environment passed the recorded privacy, access and grounding certification on 1 August 2026.

Do not call the 79 contexts unique homeowners, active users or customers. Do not claim measured accuracy, workload reduction, satisfaction or external revenue.

## If he asks what makes it different

> A normal portal stores information. OpenHouse connects it to the individual home, lets the homeowner use it through practical questions and workflows, and turns those interactions into structured feedback for the developer.

## If there is a fit

> I would not suggest trying to roll this across Bridgewater immediately. The sensible next step would be to take one upcoming phase, map the exact handover and aftercare workflow with the person who owns it, and agree what we would need to prove before either of us commits to anything larger.

> Great. I do not need your full system or every historical document to get started. Give me one scheme, one operational contact, a clean unit list and the approved handover documents. I will prepare the first test home for you to validate before any homeowners are invited.

Ask for:

- one candidate scheme;
- one Bridgewater operational owner;
- one clean unit list;
- one approved document folder;
- a date for the first-home review.

> The current working model is a setup fee plus a per-home fee, with roughly €99 per home as the initial hypothesis. I would want to understand the exact scheme, data and aftercare scope before putting a firm pilot price in front of you.

Do not offer an open-ended free pilot.
