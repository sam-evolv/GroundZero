# OpenHouse HPI v3.1 Home User Guide proof

## Bounded proposal

Test whether one existing OpenHouse Home Record can be mapped into the Irish Green Building Council's official Home User Guide template and the Home Performance Index v3.1 QA 8.0 evidence requirements for one permissioned Longview home or house type.

The output is a static, source-linked guide plus an evidence-gap matrix. It is not an HPI assessment, certification service, product build or new sales claim. The commercial question is narrow: can OpenHouse turn evidence a developer already holds into a home-specific handover guide that is easier to assemble, maintain and evidence than the current folder or manual?

## Verified evidence

- IGBC published a peer-reviewed Home User Guide template for home-builders and developers providing guides to people moving into newly built or renovated homes. The template was developed through a Construct Innovate project funded with Cairn Homes.
- The template covers how occupants use and maintain their home and installed systems. It is explicitly adaptable to a specific home, but warns that changed or newly added text is not covered by its Clarity Mark without further review.
- HPI Technical Manual v3.1 lists the Home User Guide template as a main change and states that it can be used to gain points within QA 8.0, Consumer Information and Aftercare.
- QA 8.0 is worth up to 2% of the HPI assessment. The base 1% requires a home-specific guide covering the listed topics and provision in print or online. Additional evidence can support up to 1% for in-home training, at least five years of online availability, and a Plain English mark or equivalent.
- The required subject areas include ventilation, renewable energy, heating, lighting, smart metering, water efficiency, dwelling maintenance and ecology. The guide must be specific to the home and installed systems, use concise accessible language, and include operating, maintenance and support information.
- HPI v3.1 applies to projects registered on or after 20 January 2026. The manual also says HPI v4 was intended for 2026, so the current criterion and evidence list must be rechecked before any external proposal.
- HPI certification remains a developer and assessor process reviewed by IGBC. A guide generated through OpenHouse cannot itself prove that points or certification will be awarded.

## Assumptions to falsify

- The selected home or house type has enough authoritative source material to make the guide genuinely specific rather than a re-skinned generic manual.
- OpenHouse can bind each operational statement to the correct installed system, document, issuer and date.
- The existing Home Record structure can represent all QA 8.0 topics without inventing facts or treating document presence as proof of compliance.
- A developer operator finds the source-linked guide or gap matrix materially easier to review than the current handover bundle.
- The guide creates paid-pilot value only when HPI, quality assurance or homeowner aftercare is already a live buyer requirement.

## Smallest validation test

Use one legitimately permissioned Longview home or one house type whose source documents are already available to OpenHouse. Keep all work local and static.

1. Freeze the current IGBC Home User Guide template and HPI v3.1 QA 8.0 source documents with retrieval dates.
2. Inventory the exact-home or house-type evidence already held: ventilation, heating, renewables, lighting, smart meter, water fixtures, maintenance, ecology, manuals, contacts and warranties.
3. Build a deterministic matrix with: QA 8.0 topic, proposed guide statement, source document, page or section, home/system binding, owner, confidence and missing field.
4. Populate one static guide only from that matrix. Preserve blanks and unknowns instead of drafting plausible technical text.
5. Produce a separate evidence checklist for the HPI items that OpenHouse could support, while labelling developer letters, training attendance, five-year hosting commitments and Plain English review as external evidence not yet satisfied.
6. Compare elapsed operator time, unresolved gaps and review corrections against the current manual or handover-folder process.
7. Decide **discard**, **retain as a manual paid-pilot appendix**, or **seek approval for one assessor-reviewed validation**.

### Pass gate

Retain the proof only if:

- every required QA 8.0 topic is linked to authoritative source evidence or visibly marked missing;
- all installed-system claims are specific to the selected home or house type and no compliance, certification, performance or savings conclusion is inferred;
- the static guide and evidence matrix can be assembled in no more than 90 minutes once the source bundle is available;
- an internal developer operator identifies at least one meaningful reduction in review effort, missing-evidence chase or homeowner support ambiguity; and
- the output is clearly more useful than placing the unchanged IGBC template beside the existing document folder.

### Kill gate

Discard or park the wedge if the available evidence is mostly generic, home/system binding is weak, the operator sees no advantage over the current guide, the mapping depends on manual rewriting that will not repeat, or no credible buyer is actively pursuing HPI or equivalent handover evidence.

## Downside and safeguards

- The IGBC template may reduce differentiation because any developer can download it.
- HPI v4 or later guidance may change QA 8.0 before a pilot is sold.
- A polished guide can create false confidence if the underlying manuals, commissioning records or maintenance contacts are incomplete.
- Template text changed by OpenHouse is not automatically covered by the template's Clarity Mark.
- HPI points and certification are determined through the formal assessor and IGBC review process. OpenHouse must not imply endorsement, partnership, point eligibility or certification.
- Longview evidence and homeowner data remain permissioned material. Use the smallest necessary source set and do not upload it to an unapproved service.
- This proof must not compete with the current paid-developer validation objective or the P0 market-readiness gates. It is an appendix to a live buyer need, not a speculative build.

## Approval boundary

This note authorises desk research and one static local mapping using evidence OpenHouse already has legitimate permission to use. It does not authorise:

- contacting IGBC, HPI assessors, Cairn, developers, homeowners or any third party;
- claiming HPI points, certification, compliance, endorsement or EU Taxonomy alignment;
- modifying production code, schemas, websites or customer data;
- creating a hosted guide, promising five-year availability or providing in-home training;
- spending money, registering a project, buying training or certification, or offering the service for sale;
- changing current OpenHouse project state, WIP priority or outreach approvals.

Any assessor review, external pilot, sales appendix, production implementation or buyer communication requires Sam's explicit approval.
