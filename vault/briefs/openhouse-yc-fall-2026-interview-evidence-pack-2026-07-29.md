## The one-sentence company

> OpenHouse gives residential developers one system to hand over homes, answer homeowner questions from property-specific information and manage aftercare.

Short description:

## Thirty-second answer

> Residential developers still hand over complex new homes through PDFs, email and staff memory. Homeowners then ask repetitive questions or report issues without the right property context. I built OpenHouse inside the developer where I work. It gives each home a developer-sourced record and uses it for handover, source-backed homeowner answers and structured aftercare. The product is live internally across four developments with 281 home records. I am now converting that operational proof into the first independent paid developer deployment.

## Ten-minute interview structure

### Minute 0 to 1: What do you do?

> OpenHouse gives homebuilders one system for handover and aftercare. The developer provisions the source information for each home, the homeowner can ask property-specific questions and report issues with context, and the developer receives structured aftercare instead of scattered PDFs and email.

### Minute 1 to 2: Why this problem?

> I experienced it directly while helping run a residential development. The developer holds the richest information a home will ever have during construction, but most of it is lost or fragmented at handover. The same questions and issues then return through customer-care inboxes without the correct property context.

### Minute 2 to 3: What have you built?

> A live multi-tenant product with developer and homeowner workflows, home records, handover information, contextual assistance, issue intake and operational visibility. It is deployed internally across four developments with 281 home records.

### Minute 3 to 4: What have you learned?

> Homeowners do not always know how to describe a problem. When they tried to explain settlement cracks and snags in text, I added photo input so the evidence arrived with the correct home context and could be triaged or escalated.

### Minute 4 to 5: Who pays?

> Residential developers. The initial hypothesis is a setup fee, €99 per provisioned home and recurring fees during active aftercare. The price is not externally validated yet.

### Minute 5 to 6: Why will you win?

> I combine direct operational access to the problem with the ability to build and deploy the software myself. I am not learning homebuilder handover from interviews alone. I have lived the workflow across more than 200 completed homes.

### Minute 6 to 7: Why now?

> Homes are becoming more technically complex while handover and aftercare remain fragmented. Developers face rising expectations around quality, evidence and post-completion support. Modern multimodal models make property-specific support and evidence intake practical, but only when grounded in the correct home record.

### Minute 7 to 8: How do you acquire customers?

> Start with a tightly scoped paid scheme launch for a mid-sized residential developer, then expand to its additional schemes. The developer provisions many homes at once, so distribution is B2B2C rather than one homeowner at a time.

### Minute 8 to 9: Is this venture scale?

> Handover is the wedge, not the ceiling. The developer creates the persistent home record at the moment when verified source information is richest. That record can later support warranties, maintenance, energy performance and other services throughout the home's life.

### Minute 9 to 10: Why YC and commitment?

> YC would help me move from strong internal proof to external commercial proof and build the US homebuilder route quickly. If accepted, I will leave Longview, work exclusively on OpenHouse and move to San Francisco if needed.

## Rapid partner questions

### Is Longview a customer?

> No. It is an internal deployment inside the developer where I work. It proves the product can operate in the real workflow, but it is not independent revenue.

### Why has nobody paid?

> I prioritised building and proving the workflow inside the environment I controlled. That gave me product depth but not commercial validation. The next milestone is deliberately a paid external scheme, not another internal feature cycle.

### Are the 85 linked accounts active users?

> I do not know. They are homes linked to homeowner accounts, not a verified active-user metric.

### Are 143 agreements completed handovers?

> No. They are timestamped purchaser-agreement records across 143 distinct homes. I do not treat them as completed handovers without a separately verified completion state.

### Is this just a portal?

> The portal is the interface. The product's value is keeping approved property context attached to the individual home and using it across homeowner questions, issue evidence and developer aftercare.

### Why cannot a CRM add this?

> A CRM is organised around leads and contacts. OpenHouse is organised around the home: its specification, systems, documents, warranties, questions and aftercare history. The home remains after the sale closes.

### Why will developers buy it?

> The hypothesis is that replacing fragmented handover and aftercare reduces repeated questions, missing evidence and manual triage. That still needs validation through the first paid external scheme.

### What if developers resist another system?

> The first launch is narrow. Import the information they already use, provision one homeowner cohort and prove a measurable handover or aftercare outcome before expanding.

### Who owns the data?

> The application should not use the phrase “we own homeowner data.” Developers supply property information and OpenHouse processes platform data under its published terms and privacy policy. The exact controller and processor roles should be formalised contractually for each external deployment.

### Is the Longview deployment authorised?

> Yes operationally. It is within my remit, and the other principals knew about and fully backed it. There is no separate written customer contract or DPA.

### Does Longview own the code?

> My employment contract contains no IP or invention-assignment clause. I built the product primarily on my own time using my own equipment and accounts. Longview has not been promised ownership.

### Does the company own the code?

> I personally control it and own 100% of OpenHouse Ai Limited. I have not yet executed a formal founder-to-company assignment and will complete that before taking investment.

### Why are you solo?

> I have been able to combine the domain, product and technical work and ship using AI coding agents. I am not adding a cofounder for optics. I would consider an exceptional person with proven execution, strong alignment and a relevant network.

### Are you technical?

> I am the sole human builder and responsible for product and technical decisions. I use AI coding agents extensively, then review, test and deploy the work. The result is a live TypeScript and Next.js product on Supabase and Vercel.

### What stops a competitor?

> The software can be copied. The harder part is becoming the trusted home record through the developer workflow, then accumulating property-specific context, issue evidence and lifecycle integrations. I still need to prove that advantage through external deployments.

### Why €99?

> It is an initial anchor around the cost of the printed handover pack it can replace, for a home worth roughly €500,000. It is not validated pricing. I will test volume and recurring models with external developers.

### How big can this be?

> A 500-home developer at €99 per home represents €49,500 before setup or recurring fees. The larger opportunity is the recurring home record across warranties, maintenance, energy and other post-handover services. The application presents that as expansion logic, not realised revenue.

### What would make you stop?

> If external developers will not pay for a bounded scheme launch after seeing the live workflow and a clear operational outcome, the current B2B wedge would be wrong. The next test is paid external adoption, not more internal usage.
