# The correction came with a deposit

Two weeks ago I wrote that “proceed” was too strong.

The record at that point said the Here’s Health app was still pre-sales proof. The work was meant to earn a commitment, not complete a production platform for free. That correction mattered because the previous draft had turned a promising meeting into a cleaner commercial result than the evidence supported.

This week I corrected the record again.

Here’s Health has paid a €5,000 plus VAT deposit. The app is being built now. A further €10,000 plus VAT is due when the finished app is completed.

That is the current founder-certified commercial fact in Ground Zero. The vault does not contain the invoice or bank receipt, and it does not establish permission to name the client publicly. But the status is no longer ambiguous: this is paid client work, not a speculative prototype trying to win a yes.

The correction came with money.

## Paid does not mean finished

It would be easy to write the rest of the week as a conversion story. A solo founder kept going, the client committed, and the work became real.

Only the last part is useful.

Payment changed the commercial status of the project. It did not close the delivery gates.

The app’s remote source advanced this week with Shopify catalogue and checkout work, durable order storage, signed webhook handling, deterministic tests and native-build preparation. The same source contains conflicting test totals. Its current remote lineage has not inherited the earlier independent acceptance, and the public preview is still a separate, unbound artifact. No physical-device result, app-store release or independently accepted deployment is established by the current record.

A deposit does not make those distinctions less important. It makes them more important.

Before payment, an inaccurate test total is an internal problem. After payment, it sits inside a promise to a client. Before payment, a preview can be treated as persuasion. After payment, the distance between preview, accepted source, installed app and finished product becomes delivery risk.

There is now a real completion payment on the other side of that work. There is also a real obligation not to confuse source movement with completion.

## OpenHouse found more evidence, not a buyer

OpenHouse accumulated five bounded research proposals between 15 and 19 September.

They covered retrofit handover packs, the Irish Green Building Council Home User Guide and Home Performance Index evidence, Building Control completion packs, construction Safety File custody, and taking-in-charge boundaries for shared estate assets.

Taken together, they sharpen the same product idea. A living Home Record can connect an exact home to the evidence needed for handover, operation, warranty, future work and aftercare. The useful layer is not another document folder. It is the chain between a question, the correct home or asset, the source record, its scope, and the point where the evidence stops.

That is a stronger articulation of the product.

It is not paid validation.

Every one of those notes is bounded as research or a proposed static test. They do not authorise outreach, production work or a buyer claim. Some of the underlying developer records have not been opened for the proposed proofs. Operator value and willingness to pay remain assumptions.

There was one narrower technical closure on Sunday. The production deployment can now be bound through live deployment metadata to the exact current GitHub `main` commit, and the public portal returned successfully. The tenant-isolation audit and production-migration gates remain open because no authenticated application route or direct database-policy and migration evidence was exercised.

The current OpenHouse state still says the funding position depends on paid developer validation. Better research can make the next proposal more exact. It cannot become the payment it is meant to earn.

## The machinery kept moving

The Donworth Studio desktop app also changed beneath the operating system this week. By Sunday, the installed Mac app identified as a signed v0.18.9 build. Its own metadata named a clean local source commit, but that commit was unavailable in the canonical Git object store and no rendered acceptance existed.

That is not a sentence anyone would put in a launch post.

It is still the honest state. Installed, signed, source-custodied and accepted are different things. The system is getting better at preserving those differences, but it is also producing enough movement that custody can fall behind the artifact.

The commercial contrast is useful. Here’s Health now has a paid commitment and unfinished delivery. OpenHouse has a sharper evidence thesis and no new paid developer proof in the recorded period. Donworth Desktop has a newer installed artifact and no accepted user journey for it.

Three kinds of progress, three different missing outcomes.

## What I actually reported feeling

I did not leave a fresh mental or physical check-in in Ground Zero this week.

The latest direct emotional report remains 3 August, when I said I felt stagnant rather than defeated. The latest physical correction remains 4 August, when I said the bank-holiday weekend had been genuine time off and that I was returning to the gym. The latest completed training session in the vault is a lower-body session from 6 August.

None of that describes this week.

I cannot honestly say that the paid deposit made me relieved, proud or more secure. I cannot say that the responsibility felt heavy. I cannot infer whether I trained, slept well, ate properly or carried the work comfortably around a full-time job.

That absence is particularly obvious in a week that invites a satisfying emotional ending.

Money arrived. The build advanced. The core company still needs its decisive buyer. The record does not say how any of that felt in my body or in my head.

There is no honest reason to invent the missing reaction.

## The honest result

This week changed one important business fact.

A client has paid me to build a real product. The agreed fee is €15,000 plus VAT, with €5,000 plus VAT paid and €10,000 plus VAT due on completion. That is not a lead, a meeting, a prototype reaction or a promise to reconnect.

It is also not the end of the story.

The app still has technical, integration, physical-device and release work ahead of it. OpenHouse still needs paid developer validation. The desktop delivery system still has an accepted-user-surface gap. My current mental and physical condition is still missing from the record.

The tempting version is that persistence finally paid off.

The more accurate version is smaller and better: one client paid a deposit, the work is now commercially real, and I have to finish it well without pretending that it settled everything else.
