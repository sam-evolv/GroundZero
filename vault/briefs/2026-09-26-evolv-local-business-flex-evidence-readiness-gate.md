# Evolv Local Business Flex evidence-readiness gate

## Bounded proposal

Test whether one existing Evolv commercial-rooftop customer could benefit from a **Local Business Flex evidence-readiness screen** once ESB Networks publishes the final product, selected substations, delivery windows, prices and tender documents.

The screen would establish whether the exact non-domestic MPRN, meter history, connection limits, operating pattern and genuinely controllable load, storage or generation can satisfy the final eligibility and delivery terms. Evolv's role would be evidence preparation and owner-side decision support. It would not make Evolv an electricity supplier, aggregator, Flexibility Service Provider, trading party or controller of the customer's equipment.

The commercial question is narrow: **can the existing reporting baseline identify a real, low-friction flexibility-revenue opportunity for one owner without buying hardware, changing operations or overstating a consultation-stage product?**

This is a watch-and-validate gate, not an active market offer. As checked on 26 September 2026, ESB Networks still labels Local Business Flex **closed under review**, says it is engaging with the CRU on final product design and has not published procurement locations or final prices.

## Why this is genuinely new but subordinate

Ground Zero contained no match for `Local Business Flex`, `Scheduled Utilisation`, `SU Firm`, `SU Flex` or `MCC12` when checked on 26 September 2026.

The existing Evolv evidence notes address different boundaries:

- [[briefs/2026-09-06-evolv-customer-authorised-meter-data-reconciliation-proof]] reconciles physical import, generation, export and supplier evidence for one closed period.
- [[briefs/2026-09-09-evolv-duos-group-public-benchmark-gate]] tests public DUoS context, not dispatchable flexibility.
- [[briefs/2026-09-23-evolv-commercial-solar-grant-grid-readiness-receipt]] checks pre-install grant and connection evidence.
- [[briefs/2026-09-25-evolv-electricity-carbon-claims-evidence-boundary]] separates physical electricity, contractual attributes and factor claims.

Local Business Flex introduces a distinct decision: whether a site can **change active power relative to an evidence-based baseline during fixed local delivery periods** and be measured and paid under a future final contract. The same permissioned meter history contemplated by [[items/renew-reporting-source-baseline]] may make the screen cheap, but this proposal does not justify a separate portal, battery project or market-participation business.

## Verified evidence

### Current programme status

- ESB Networks' live consultation page says the consultation is closed, final product design remains under review with the CRU, and stakeholders will be notified before procurement launches.
- The live consultations index describes a proposed H2 2026 launch, direct-award procurement, an application window of approximately one year and contracts intended to run to the end of 2030. It also says selected local areas will be published ahead of launch.
- ESB Networks' commercial flexibility page describes the Demand Flexibility Product as a service still being developed to avoid overloads on the local network.

These live pages support a readiness watch. They do not establish that procurement is open, that the proposed terms are final or that any Evolv site is in an eligible location.

### Consultation-stage product design

The April 2026 official proposal describes two upward-flexibility products:

- `SU Firm`: fixed two-hour delivery periods, proposed minimum four days per week, with no payment below 80% of contracted delivery for a period.
- `SU Flex`: fixed two-hour delivery periods, proposed minimum two days per week, plus possible higher-priced Local Peak Events; payment is based on delivered flexibility without the proposed SU Firm underperformance threshold.

Both products are proposed as utilisation-only payments in €/MWh, with no availability payment. Prices, locations, service windows, delivery periods and required MW/MWh were still to be published.

The proposal's non-exhaustive eligibility design includes:

- a non-domestic customer at an existing distribution-connected MPRN;
- connection to a selected substation;
- the ability to reduce demand or increase injection from generation or storage without breaching the connection agreement or Distribution Code;
- a quarter-hourly meter or an ESB Networks half-hourly smart meter configured as MCC12;
- at least 12 months of meter readings for baselining;
- at least 2 kW of flexible capacity; and
- delivery measured against a quarterly refreshed historical baseline.

The May 2026 official Q&A adds that aggregation is permitted, site eligibility is still assessed by MPRN, ESB Networks can verify substation linkage during the application process, capacity is intended to be awarded in application order until a location's requirement is filled, and a site must remain within MIC, MEC, its connection agreement and the Distribution Code. It also says solar-plus-battery interactions and final contract details would be addressed in tender documents.

These are proposal-stage facts, not final eligibility or revenue terms.

## Assumptions to falsify

- A current, permissioned Evolv rooftop customer has an existing non-domestic MPRN and is willing to consider a no-control evidence screen.
- The final procurement includes the site's actual upstream substation and retains terms that make a small commercial asset eligible.
- The site has QH or MCC12 metering and at least 12 months of usable interval data before the proposed contract start.
- The customer has at least 2 kW of **controllable and repeatable** upward flexibility during the final delivery periods. Normal solar generation or an attractive generation profile is not, by itself, dispatchable flexibility.
- The flexible action can occur without breaching MIC, MEC, the connection agreement, the Distribution Code, equipment warranties, safety controls, production requirements or other market commitments.
- The reporting baseline can separate ordinary site behaviour from the proposed flexible action well enough to avoid false performance claims.
- Final utilisation revenue exceeds metering, control, staff, aggregator, legal, procurement, operational and underperformance costs by enough to matter to the owner.
- An approved aggregator or specialist can carry market and control obligations if direct participation would be disproportionate.

## Smallest validation test

### Gate 0 — wait for authority

Do not use customer data or contact anyone while the product remains under review. Revisit only when ESB Networks publishes the final procurement notice, selected substations, prices, contract, QSQ and measurement rules. Freeze those exact sources by title, version, URL and retrieval date, and compare them with this consultation-stage note before relying on any field.

### Gate 1 — one-site static screen

If a plausible selected location exists:

1. Obtain Sam's approval for the exact site and conflict, confidentiality and IP boundary, then obtain the registered customer's approval for the exact records and purpose.
2. Work only in an approved client workspace. Keep the full MPRN, interval files, contracts, credentials, plant identifiers and commercially sensitive records out of Ground Zero.
3. Build a source-linked matrix for: non-domestic status; existing MPRN; final selected substation; meter type; available interval-data period; MIC; MEC; connection and market restrictions; candidate controllable action; control owner; final service window; delivery period; minimum days; minimum flexible capacity; baseline method; published price; aggregator or direct route; and unresolved professional confirmation.
4. Mark every field `SOURCE CONFIRMED`, `CUSTOMER CONFIRMATION REQUIRED`, `ESB NETWORKS CONFIRMATION REQUIRED`, `QUALIFIED TECHNICAL CONFIRMATION REQUIRED`, `FINAL TENDER REVIEW REQUIRED`, `MISSING` or `NOT APPLICABLE WITH SOURCE`. Do not infer substation, meter configuration or controllability from address, solar size or invoices.
5. Use historical data only to run a no-control counterfactual: compare the final delivery periods with ordinary demand, generation and export, and test whether the proposed flexible action would have produced at least the final minimum capacity consistently. Do not issue commands or change equipment schedules.
6. Calculate a conservative owner economics range from the final published price and measured candidate delivery. Deduct all known incremental staff, metering, control, aggregator, professional, procurement and expected underperformance costs. Show unknowns; do not present gross utilisation revenue as profit.
7. Have the customer's qualified electrical or energy professional confirm that the proposed action is technically controllable and consistent with the connection and safety boundary. Have the relevant procurement, legal or market specialist confirm contractual obligations before any application.
8. Return one outcome: **ineligible / discard**, **watch pending one named missing fact**, or **seek separate approval for a proving test or aggregator discussion**. Do not apply, contract or operate from this note.

### Pass gate

Seek approval for a proving test or specialist handoff only if:

- final procurement is open and the exact MPRN is confirmed in a selected location;
- the final rules still admit the relevant asset type and route;
- required interval metering and history are complete and source-linked;
- a qualified party confirms at least the final minimum flexible capacity is controllable inside the site's technical and connection limits during the required periods;
- the no-control historical screen shows credible repeatability rather than coincidental solar output;
- conservative net value remains material after all known participation and operational costs; and
- the owner says the opportunity is worth the operational constraints.

A theoretically eligible MPRN with no controllable action, no material net value or no owner appetite does not pass.

### Kill gate

Discard or park the wedge if procurement does not launch, the site is outside selected locations, final terms exclude the asset, the meter/history requirement fails, the apparent capacity depends on uncontrollable sunshine or baseline artefacts, delivery would compromise business operations or safety, the connection boundary is unclear, final economics are immaterial, or a specialist already provides the same evidence and participation support more efficiently.

## Downside and safeguards

- **Consultation risk:** every product characteristic in the April/May documents can change. The live status page and final tender control.
- **False dispatchability:** installed solar capacity is not evidence that additional export can be delivered on demand. Treat weather-dependent output as non-controllable unless the final method and a qualified party establish otherwise.
- **Baseline error:** seasonality, outages, production changes and the solar installation itself can distort historical baselines. Preserve actual, estimated, excluded and adjusted intervals.
- **Operational risk:** fixed two-hour periods across multiple days may conflict with the customer's real production, comfort, food-safety, equipment or staffing needs.
- **Underperformance risk:** proposed SU Firm terms would pay nothing below 80% delivery for a period. Final penalties, exit rights and liability are not yet known.
- **Double payment and stacking:** the proposal allows stacking only where the same MW is not paid twice. All existing market and supplier arrangements require review.
- **Data sensitivity:** interval data can reveal operating schedules and commercial activity. Minimise purpose, period, access and retention.
- **Hardware drift:** do not turn eligibility research into an unevidenced battery, controls or metering purchase.
- **Role confusion:** do not imply ESB Networks or CRU endorsement or present Evolv as an FSP, aggregator, supplier, engineer or market adviser without the corresponding real authority and contract.
- **Priority drift:** this screen remains subordinate to the paid 60–90 day owner-side baseline in [[project_state/renew]] and the existing manual workflow proof in [[items/renew-reporting-source-baseline]].

## Approval boundary

This note records public-source research and proposes one later, static, permissioned screen. It does not authorise:

- customer, ESB Networks, CRU, supplier, aggregator, installer, engineer or third-party contact;
- access to an MPRN, meter portal, interval file, connection agreement, plant controls, credentials or client records;
- an eTenders registration, QSQ, tender, application, contract, market registration or commitment;
- a proving test, load reduction, export increase, battery dispatch, equipment schedule, connection change or production mutation;
- a battery, meter, controller, software build, API integration, scheduled ingestion or paid professional engagement;
- publication, outreach, spending, legal advice, procurement advice, engineering advice or a revenue claim.

Sam must approve the exact site and evidence scope after conflict, confidentiality and IP review. The registered customer must approve the records and purpose. Qualified technical and contract specialists must confirm their respective boundaries. Any contact, application, proving test, equipment control, purchase, contract or paid pilot requires separate approval against the final procurement documents.
