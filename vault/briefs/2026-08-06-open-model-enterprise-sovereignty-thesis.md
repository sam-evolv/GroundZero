# Open-model enterprise sovereignty thesis

## Why this matters

Sam marked this take as worth preserving. It sharpens the long-term architecture thesis behind the personal agent: models will become increasingly replaceable and enterprise value will move into the proprietary harness, company intelligence, deployment controls and managed infrastructure around them.

This is a strategic signal, not a verified forecast. Andrew Amann predicts the shift will be visible across the Fortune 500 by the end of 2027. The direction is more useful than the exact deadline.

## Source 1: David Sacks screenshot

David Sacks argues that most of the technology industry now supports open-source AI, with Anthropic as the exception. His warning is that restrictions may be framed as something other than an outright ban while producing the same practical effect:

> “Nobody is trying to ban open source.”
>
> “We just want to limit who can use it.”
>
> “We just want to limit who can contribute to it.”
>
> “We just want to limit how powerful those models can be.”
>
> “We just want to make sure the guardrails (we lobbied for) can’t be removed.”

His conclusion is that the rest of the industry should watch efforts that could weaken open source through access, contribution, capability or control restrictions.

The screenshot is dated 26 July 2026. This note preserves the argument as commentary, not as an independently verified account of Anthropic’s lobbying position.

## Source 2: Andrew Amann’s enterprise prediction

Andrew Amann predicts that by 2027 Coca-Cola, Berkshire Hathaway and much of the Fortune 500 will have organisation-specific open-source or open-weight models built for them. His core argument:

- leading technology companies already own the hardware and engineering capacity;
- they can use open-weight models, large prompt sets and distillation to create private derivatives;
- employee workloads will shift from paying frontier-model providers for every token toward a larger proportion of open-model inference;
- consulting and infrastructure providers will package this capability for non-technology enterprises;
- the commercial end state is open weights plus proprietary models, proprietary harnesses and managed infrastructure;
- the strategic prize is control of the organisation’s AI stack and intelligence, not permanent dependence on one model vendor.

His strongest formulation is: **the next wave of AI is proprietary harnesses on managed infrastructure.**

## The durable insight

The genius in the take is not that every large company will train a foundation model. It is that the model layer is likely to commoditise while the surrounding system becomes the defensible asset.

The enterprise control point becomes:

- proprietary context and company intelligence;
- task-specific evals and quality thresholds;
- routing across hosted, open-weight and local models;
- permissions, approvals and action boundaries;
- deployment provenance and auditability;
- workflow integration and accumulated operating data;
- the ability to replace models without rebuilding the product.

Owning weights alone is not sovereignty. A company can self-host a model and still lack control over data exposure, tool permissions, deployment authority or consequential actions. Real sovereignty is a model-agnostic control layer that can verify what model is running, what it may access, who authorised it and what evidence is required before it acts.

## Implication for the personal agent venture

This strengthens the runtime-first decision in [[decisions/2026-08-06-personal-agent-runtime-first-sequence]]. Hermes, Ground Zero, approvals, skills, evals and invisible routing are the strategic core. The first product should not be coupled to OpenAI, Anthropic or any single open model.

Practical architecture rule:

1. Keep the harness and user context provider-neutral.
2. Measure real workloads before changing the model mix.
3. Route bounded, repetitive and privacy-sensitive work toward cheaper or local/open-weight models when quality is proven.
4. Preserve frontier APIs for the minority of tasks where their capability earns the cost.
5. Treat model replacement as an internal infrastructure change, not a user-facing migration.

This does not justify premature self-hosting. The existing gate in [[briefs/2026-08-05-personal-assistant-research-codex]] remains sound: general self-hosting should wait for sustained volume and a fully loaded cost advantage at equivalent quality and service levels.

## Strategic distinction

- **Weak thesis:** every company needs to build its own model.
- **Strong thesis:** every serious AI-enabled company needs to own the intelligence, eval, routing, permission and workflow layer around replaceable models.

The strong thesis is directly aligned with [[companies/personal-agent]] and [[context/agentic-value-creation-mission]]. It protects the venture from provider churn and turns accumulated context and workflow evidence into the durable asset.
