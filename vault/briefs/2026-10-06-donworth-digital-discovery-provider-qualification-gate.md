# Donworth Digital Discovery provider-qualification gate

## Bounded proposal

Test whether Donworth Studio can package one **sector-specific, vendor-agnostic Digital Discovery engagement** for an eligible Irish company with one measurable operating bottleneck.

The proposed engagement would use a T-shaped structure: assess the buyer's wider digital, data, people and process readiness, then go deep on one workflow where Sam already has credible delivery context — property handover and aftercare, renewable-installation operations, or food/retail order and reporting integrity. The output would be a 12–18 month digital roadmap plus a defined 4–6 month action plan, not software implementation.

This is a provider-qualification and purchase-friction test inside Donworth Studio, not a new company, a generic “AI strategy” offer, an Enterprise Ireland endorsement, or a grant-funded implementation promise. It remains subordinate to the paid Here’s Health delivery and [[items/donworth-publishable-and-outreach]].

## Why this is genuinely new but bounded

[[briefs/2026-09-11-openhouse-built-to-innovate-buyer-subsidy-gate]] already tests a buyer-side funding appendix for one residential developer, using a then-current Built to Innovate page and a historical Digital Discovery reference document. It does not establish a current cross-sector Donworth service offer.

Enterprise Ireland now publishes a 2026 Digital Discovery brochure and current FAQ that define an active, buyer-funded external-consultancy route: 3–7 expert days over 8–12 weeks; up to 80% of project cost; a €5,000 maximum grant; and eligible cost capped at €900 per day. The current FAQ says the applicant chooses the provider, no approved-provider list exists for Digital Discovery, and Enterprise Ireland can still reject the provider or project. A separate current AI Discovery page says its specialist lane uses fully briefed consultants, while recommending Digital Discovery when the buyer needs a wider choice of consultant.

That creates a narrower opportunity than generic consulting: prove whether Donworth can qualify for one exact discovery engagement by combining sector evidence, a compliant roadmap method and a measurable workflow baseline. It does not justify joining a panel, contacting buyers, quoting grant support, or selling implementation before the buyer and Enterprise Ireland confirm eligibility.

## Verified evidence

Official sources reviewed on 6 October 2026 establish that:

- Enterprise Ireland's current Access Advice page lists Digital Discovery as support for eligible companies to hire a digital expert to introduce new technologies and solutions, with a maximum €5,000 grant.
- The 2026 Digital Discovery brochure states that eligible activity includes analysis of digital, data or automation systems, exploration of integration opportunities, and guidance on digital, automation or AI solutions. It names data analytics and AI, process automation, IoT and digital twins as focus areas.
- The same brochure states that support can cover up to 80% of project cost, capped at a €5,000 grant; engagements run for 3–7 expert days across 8–12 weeks; eligible cost is capped at €900 per day including travel and subsistence.
- The current FAQ says eligible applicants include Enterprise Ireland client companies with at least 10 full-time employees, HPSU clients with the stated staffing threshold, and potentially non-client Irish companies with at least 10 full-time employees in manufacturing or eligible internationally traded services. LEO, Údarás na Gaeltachta and IDA clients are directed to their own advisers.
- The FAQ requires a strategic digital roadmap covering at least 12–18 months plus a defined 4–6 month action plan. Implementation, product/service/website development, software configuration, routine audits, training, finance, HR, sales and marketing work are ineligible.
- The applicant must identify and engage the provider. There is no approved-consultant list for Digital Discovery, but Enterprise Ireland may reject a provider or project; buyers are advised to compare up to three providers and choose one with sector understanding, relevant expertise, grant-rule awareness and technology-agnostic practice.
- Work may begin only after the buyer receives and signs its Letter of Offer. The buyer later claims using the provider invoice and proof of payment. Grant availability is therefore not guaranteed and does not remove the buyer's cash-flow or approval burden.
- Enterprise Ireland's current AI adoption surface separates exploration, planning and implementation. Its specialist AI Discovery route uses fully briefed consultants; it explicitly points buyers wanting unrestricted consultant choice toward Digital Discovery's more open structure.
- The 2 October 2026 appointment of Ireland's first AI Sectoral Champions confirms a live government push toward sector-specific, responsible adoption and practical business outcomes. It is market-timing evidence only, not demand proof for Donworth.

The sources do **not** establish that Donworth is an acceptable provider, that an existing Donworth prospect is eligible, that funding remains available for a future application, or that any buyer wants this engagement.

## Assumptions to falsify

- At least one warm or already-researched account is an Enterprise Ireland-eligible manufacturing, internationally traded services or Built to Innovate residential company; eligibility must be confirmed by the buyer's adviser.
- Donworth can evidence genuine sector understanding without relying on confidential client material or overstating OpenHouse, Evolv or Here’s Health outcomes.
- A strategic discovery engagement is valuable to the buyer even though implementation is excluded from the grant.
- Donworth can stay vendor-agnostic and recommend a non-AI or external route when it is better than a Donworth build.
- One painful workflow can anchor the roadmap while still meeting the programme's wider people, process, data and governance requirements.
- The administrative cycle and buyer pre-funding requirement do not make a direct fixed-scope paid audit faster and more attractive.
- Preparing the provider pack does not displace Here’s Health delivery, existing outreach proof or current release gates.

## Smallest validation test

### Gate 0 — one eligible account, no contact

Select only one warm or already-researched account whose public evidence plausibly fits an official eligibility route. Record company type, agency relationship if public, employee threshold evidence, sector and the exact unknowns. Do not infer eligibility from company size, brand or funding history.

If no existing account is plausibly eligible, stop. Do not create a new prospecting lane to justify this note.

### Gate 1 — one unsent provider pack

Prepare one five-page maximum pack in a private Donworth workspace:

1. **Buyer problem and baseline:** one current workflow, recent observed behaviour, business consequence and measurable baseline; no synthetic savings.
2. **Discovery method:** systems/data map, people and process constraints, non-AI alternatives, governance and security questions, and one deep workflow analysis.
3. **Outputs:** a 12–18 month roadmap, a 4–6 month action plan, named buyer owners, decision gates, KPIs and explicit no-build recommendations.
4. **Provider evidence:** only source-bound Donworth experience and public proof relevant to the sector; confidential client material, unsupported outcomes and logos remain excluded.
5. **Programme boundary:** strategic discovery only; no software build, configuration, training, marketing, grant application or guaranteed reimbursement. Work starts only after a signed Letter of Offer.

Use the current official eligible-cost ceiling only as a scope constraint. Do not set a customer price, advertise a buyer contribution, or describe any amount as approved before tax/VAT treatment, exact days and adviser confirmation are established.

### Gate 2 — approval-gated qualification check

Only if Gate 1 produces a credible pack, ask Sam to approve the exact account, pack and one bounded contact path. The buyer — not Donworth — must confirm its eligibility, project option, provider suitability, application route and approval timing with its Enterprise Ireland or other relevant agency adviser before any funded work, contract or external grant wording.

### Pass gate

Retain this as a Donworth offer only if:

- one existing account is adviser-confirmed eligible and has a recent, measurable operating problem;
- Enterprise Ireland or the relevant agency accepts the proposed project scope and provider before work starts;
- the buyer values and will pay for the roadmap without relying on guaranteed reimbursement;
- the pack can be reviewed in under ten minutes and at least 70% of its method is reusable in the same sector;
- delivery stays within 3–7 expert days over the permitted engagement window and produces the required roadmap and action plan;
- Donworth can remain vendor-agnostic and separate any later implementation into a new, independently approved contract; and
- the work does not delay the paying Here’s Health engagement or higher-priority accepted delivery gates.

One adviser confirmation and one paid engagement would validate the route, not a wider market.

### Kill gate

Discard or park the wedge if there is no eligible existing account, the buyer wants only implementation, the problem is generic or unmeasured, provider acceptance depends on credentials Donworth does not have, adviser review adds more friction than a direct sale, the buyer will proceed only if reimbursement is guaranteed, the roadmap is unlikely to change a decision, or the work competes with current paid delivery.

## Downside and safeguards

- **Generic-consulting drift:** the vault explicitly rejects abstract AI strategy. Anchor the work to one costly workflow and a falsifiable baseline; do not sell “AI transformation.”
- **Grant-led demand distortion:** public support can make a weak problem look commercial. The buyer must still value and pay for the outcome if the claim is delayed or reduced.
- **Provider rejection:** no published approved list does not mean Donworth is accepted. Enterprise Ireland can reject the provider or project.
- **Eligibility error:** employee count, agency relationship, manufacturing/international-trade status and Built to Innovate scope require adviser confirmation.
- **Scope contamination:** implementation, configuration, product development, routine audits and training are outside Digital Discovery. Keep any later build separate.
- **Conflict risk:** follow-on implementation could compromise vendor neutrality or provider suitability. Disclose the commercial interest and preserve competitive alternatives.
- **Timing risk:** the FAQ says the offer is subject to Digital Transition Fund budget availability up to 2026. Do not imply an open-ended window or create artificial urgency.
- **Cash-flow risk:** the buyer pays the provider and later claims with invoice and proof of payment; reimbursement is not upfront or guaranteed.
- **Evidence leakage:** do not put customer identities, internal systems, employee data, credentials or confidential Here’s Health/OpenHouse/Evolv material in Ground Zero.
- **Priority drift:** this is one unsent proof, not an active sales lane. Here’s Health delivery and existing approval-gated outreach remain ahead of it.

## Approval boundary

This note authorises public-source research and one unsent internal provider pack only. It does not authorise:

- contact with Enterprise Ireland, agency advisers, consultants, prospects, clients or intermediaries;
- representing Donworth as approved, briefed, shortlisted, panelled, grant-eligible or grant-funded;
- stating that any buyer, provider, activity, price or cost is eligible;
- submitting an application, joining a consultant pool, signing a Letter of Offer or provider contract, or starting funded work;
- using client records, confidential systems, credentials or production data;
- setting a customer price, promising reimbursement, discounting an implementation or changing an existing proposal;
- external messaging, outreach, spending, production mutation, software development or delivery work.

Sam must approve the exact account, pack and contact. The buyer must own the application and confirm the project and provider with its agency adviser before any work starts. Any paid engagement, consultant briefing, follow-on implementation or second-account test requires separate approval.

## Open gaps

- A current eligible account already inside Sam's warm or researched network.
- Donworth's provider acceptability for that exact project and buyer.
- Current remaining programme budget and the buyer's exact agency route.
- VAT, non-eligible cost and invoice treatment for the proposed scope.
- Whether a five-day rather than seven-day engagement can produce the required roadmap without becoming superficial.
- Whether the buyer values a discovery-only output enough to pre-fund it.
- Whether follow-on Donworth implementation is compatible with the required vendor neutrality and any provider-conflict rules.
