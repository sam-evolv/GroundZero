# OpenHouse founder-sales operating kit

## Objective

Create one independent paid developer deployment. OpenHouse product work is subordinate to that commercial proof unless release safety or a named buyer requirement demands it.

## One offer

> OpenHouse gives every completed home one living record for its documents, systems, warranties and history. Homeowners can get practical guidance grounded in their own home, while the developer sees repeated questions, missing information and where aftercare needs attention.

The first commercial offer is a bounded paid scheme pilot, not a free trial and not a portfolio-wide software replacement.

### Working pilot hypothesis

- one named scheme or phase;
- one developer operational owner;
- one approved unit list and document source;
- first validated test home before any homeowner launch;
- 25 to 100 provisioned homes as the likely first cohort;
- a clear handover and escalation path;
- weekly evidence on access, question categories, missing information and human escalation;
- written continue, change or stop decision.

### Working commercial hypothesis

Use this as an internal starting position, not a published price list:

- €2,500 scheme setup;
- €99 per provisioned home;
- recurring fee only where an active aftercare or portfolio workflow creates continuing value.

Illustrative pilot values:

- 25 homes: €4,975;
- 50 homes: €7,450;
- 100 homes: €12,400;
- 250 homes: €27,250.

These prices are unvalidated. The important rule is that an independent live pilot is paid. A short workflow conversation can be free; implementation is not.

## Buyer

Primary roles:

- Customer Care Director or Head of Customer Care;
- Customer Experience Director;
- Aftercare or Warranty Director;
- Residential Operations Director;
- Quality Director;
- Managing Director at a regional builder where those functions are combined.

Avoid generic innovation contacts without operating ownership. The person must own handover, homeowner support, warranty, aftercare or the budget attached to those workflows.

## Evidence Sam can use

- Sam is one of three people running Longview Estates and works directly across residential development delivery, sales, handover and aftercare.
- OpenHouse has operating data across four Longview developments.
- The conservative evidence set contains 259 property-specific questions across 79 home contexts.
- 245 questions were typed rather than selected from suggested prompts.
- A controlled exact-home reviewer environment was independently tested for the recorded access and grounding checks.
- The approved show-house proof contained 59 documents and one video.

Do not claim external paying customers, measured workload reduction, measured answer accuracy, homeowner satisfaction improvement or portfolio-wide enterprise readiness.

## Outbound operating rhythm

The system must create enough conversations to learn without requiring Sam to spend each evening doing list research.

### Agent work

- research companies and current schemes;
- identify a real operating owner;
- preserve source URLs;
- draft one personalised opening line;
- prepare the next message and follow-up date;
- keep the pipeline and evidence ledger current;
- summarise replies and prepare the next move.

### Sam work

- approve and send messages;
- use his own network where a relationship exists;
- conduct discovery calls;
- make the commercial ask;
- decide price and scope;
- approve any external proposal or commitment.

### Volume target

- 20 qualified first contacts per week;
- four first contacts per weekday on average;
- follow-ups run separately and do not replace new first contacts;
- Ireland, UK and US operate in parallel;
- keep the buyer and offer specific even as volume rises.

The first 80 qualified contacts form the initial evidence set. Do not change the product after five ignored messages.

### Directional funnel model

Use this to diagnose the system, not as a forecast or guarantee:

- 100 target accounts over eight weeks;
- 60 meaningfully reached;
- nine positive replies at a 15% positive reply rate;
- five qualified conversations at roughly a 60% reply-to-call rate;
- one paid pilot at a 20% qualified-call close rate.

Each account still needs one primary contact, one secondary route where possible, one active or recently completed development, one specific reason to speak now and a plausible path to a pilot decision. Volume does not excuse generic targeting.

## Discovery call

The call has five jobs:

1. map the current workflow from sale agreed through handover and the first year after completion;
2. identify who owns information, homeowner questions, defects, warranties and contractor follow-up;
3. quantify the repeated work, evidence failures or customer consequences;
4. establish the existing systems and why they do or do not solve it;
5. agree a concrete next step, owner and date.

Core questions:

- Walk me through what happens from completion preparation to the homeowner receiving the keys.
- What does the homeowner receive, and who assembles it?
- Where do questions go after completion?
- How do you distinguish missing information, a defect and a warranty matter?
- What evidence is required before an issue is closed?
- Which questions or failures repeat across homes?
- What does management currently see across schemes or communities?
- Which upcoming phase would provide the cleanest test?
- What would a pilot have to prove to continue?
- Who else must approve it?

## Qualification gate

A prospect is qualified when:

- there is an active or upcoming scheme/community;
- the buyer owns a repeated handover, aftercare, quality or warranty workflow;
- the existing process has a measurable gap or cost;
- a controlled pilot can coexist with incumbent systems;
- a named person can approve payment and data access;
- there is a decision window rather than general curiosity.

- proposals sent;

## Decision rules after 80 first contacts

- Low replies: target, contact data or opening line is wrong.
- Replies but few meetings: the ask is weak or the pain is not owned by that person.
- Meetings but no qualified problem: the wedge is wrong or too broad.
- Qualified problems but no proposal: the next step is too large or unclear.
- Proposals but no payment: price, trust, timing, procurement or implementation risk is blocking the sale.
- One paid pilot: pause broad product speculation and deliver the promised outcome exceptionally well.
