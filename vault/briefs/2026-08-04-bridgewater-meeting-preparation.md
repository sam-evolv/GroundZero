# Bridgewater meeting preparation

For the concise in-meeting version, use [[briefs/2026-08-04-bridgewater-meeting-card]].

## Decision

Treat this as a discovery conversation with a relevant demonstration, not a generic product pitch.

The objective is to learn how Bridgewater currently manages homeowner handover and aftercare, show the smallest part of OpenHouse that maps to their real workflow, and secure one concrete next step.

A good outcome is one of:

- a second workflow session with the person who owns handover or customer care;
- permission to map one live or upcoming scheme;
- a bounded one-scheme pilot proposal;
- or a clear reason why the fit is weak.

Do not leave with only "send me more information."

## Meeting structure

Assume a 45-minute conversation unless the invitation says otherwise.

### 0 to 5 minutes: rapport and frame

- Thank David for making the time.
- Acknowledge that Carol shared the interview.
- Give a short founder and product explanation.
- Ask permission to understand Bridgewater's current process before showing the product.

Suggested opening:

> Thanks for making the time. Carol mentioned she shared the interview, so rather than repeat all of that, I will give you the short version. I am one of three people running Longview Estates and I built OpenHouse from dealing directly with sales, handover and homeowner questions across our own developments. It gives each completed home one living record for its documents, systems, warranties and history, with a homeowner interface and developer visibility behind it. I would like to understand how Bridgewater currently handles that journey and then show you the parts that are actually relevant.

### 5 to 20 minutes: discovery

Ask about the current process before showing features.

Do not turn the list below into an interrogation. Start broad, follow the real friction, and use the remaining questions only to fill gaps.

#### Handover

1. Can you walk me through what happens from sale agreed to the homeowner receiving the keys?
2. Who assembles the final handover information and what does the buyer receive?
3. Does that process change between Waterfall, The Orchard, Glenwood and Ocean Drive?
4. How do the selling agent, site team and Bridgewater hand information between each other?
5. What normally arrives late or needs to be chased before handover?

#### Homeowner support and aftercare

6. Where do homeowner questions go after completion?
7. Who decides whether a question is informational, a snag, a warranty matter or something that needs a contractor?
8. How are actions assigned, followed up and closed?
9. What evidence is required before Bridgewater regards an item as resolved?
10. How is the homeowner kept updated while an issue is open?
11. Which questions or problems repeat most often in the first three to twelve months?
12. Is there one place where management can see recurring questions and unresolved gaps across schemes?

#### Systems and homeowner education

13. How do homeowners learn to operate heating, ventilation, hot water, EV provision and other systems in their specific home?
14. Are manuals and warranties currently organised by house type, unit or scheme?
15. How often are customer-care questions really missing-information or system-understanding questions rather than defects?

#### Commercial qualification

16. Which part of the current journey creates the most avoidable work or customer friction?
17. Is improving this workflow a current priority, or mainly something that would be nice to have?
18. Who owns the process operationally?
19. Who would need to approve a pilot and a data-processing arrangement?
20. Which upcoming phase or scheme would provide a clean, low-risk test?
21. What would a pilot have to prove for Bridgewater to continue using it?

2. **Home record and documents**

   - Use the certified show-house example with 59 documents and one video if appropriate.
   - Emphasise finding the right information after handover, not merely storing PDFs.

3. **One grounded homeowner question**
   - Use a question whose answer has already been certified against the exact home record, such as the AquaBox warranty date.
   - Show the answer and source context.
   - Explain that uncertain or unsupported questions should be escalated rather than invented.

4. **Developer learning loop**

## The concise OpenHouse explanation

> OpenHouse gives each completed home a living record for its documents, systems, warranties and history. Homeowners get practical answers and guidance grounded in their own home. Developers get a more consistent handover experience and evidence about recurring questions, missing information and where aftercare needs attention.

If David asks what makes it different from a normal portal:

> A normal portal stores information. OpenHouse connects the information to the individual home, lets the homeowner use it through practical questions and workflows, and turns those interactions into structured feedback for the developer.

Suggested transition:

The sensible next step would be to take one upcoming phase, map the exact handover and aftercare workflow with the person who owns it, and agree what we would need to prove before either of us commits to anything larger.

### Candidate pilot choices

- **The Orchard:** smaller and easier to control, potentially useful for a low-risk first implementation.
- **A Waterfall Avenue phase:** more unit and house-type complexity, with stronger operational evidence if Bridgewater is ready for a larger test.
- **Another scheme chosen by David:** preferable if it has a clearer completion window, cleaner documents or a motivated internal owner.

Do not choose the scheme for Bridgewater in the first meeting. Ask which one provides the cleanest test.

### Pilot scope

- one named Bridgewater operational owner;
- one defined phase or homeowner cohort;
- agreed unit and document inputs;
- one branded exact-home record per participating home;
- homeowner access to approved documents and home-specific guidance;
- a bounded escalation or aftercare path;
- a short weekly developer scorecard;
- and a written continue, change or stop decision.

### Measures to agree before launch

- required handover evidence completeness;
- time from approved completion evidence to homeowner access;
- homeowner access and return usage;
- question volume and categories;
- questions resolved from approved evidence;
- questions requiring human escalation;
- repeated information gaps corrected during the pilot;
- and developer time spent on the agreed workflow.

## Likely objections

### "We already have a CRM, portal or document system"

> I would not ask you to replace a working core system. The pilot should sit around one handover and aftercare workflow and use the approved information you already have. If it cannot create value without a major systems replacement, it is the wrong first pilot.

### "This sounds like another system for the team to maintain"

> That is exactly what the pilot needs to test. We would define the information inputs and measure Bridgewater time prospectively. If it adds administration rather than reducing repeated work or improving visibility, we should stop.

### "Will homeowners actually use it?"

> I cannot claim that yet for Bridgewater. We would define access and return usage before launch and measure it on one cohort. The Longview evidence shows genuine property-specific questions, but Bridgewater adoption has to be proven independently.

### "How can we trust AI answers?"

> The Assistant is designed to answer from the authorised home record and approved source material, not from generic guesses. We have independently tested one controlled exact-home environment. The pilot would still need agreed content ownership, escalation rules and prospective answer-quality review.

### "What about data security?"

> The controlled reviewer environment was tested for exact-home access, cross-home denial, logout, expiry and revocation, including direct data-plane probes. I would not treat that as permission for an unrestricted portfolio rollout. A Bridgewater pilot would have its own data scope, processing terms and release gate.

### "How long would implementation take?"

> I would need to see the unit data, document set, completion timing and required workflow before committing to a date. The first output should be a short implementation map, not an invented timeline.

### "Can it integrate with our current systems?"

> Possibly, but I would not make integration a first-meeting promise. We should first identify the actual source systems, export formats and whether a bounded pilot can work from approved files or structured exports.
