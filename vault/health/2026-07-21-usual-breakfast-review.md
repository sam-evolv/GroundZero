## What is good

- Oats are a useful, filling carbohydrate and fibre base for morning training and work.
- The breakfast is repeatable, which makes it easy to control and track.
- The creatine label confirms one scoop provides 5 g of 100% creatine monohydrate.
- The multivitamin is not a substitute for food, but taking one tablet with breakfast follows the visible label direction.

## Main constraint

The meal appears carbohydrate-heavy and probably protein-light. Exact calorie and protein content cannot be inferred reliably from a photo because the dry oat, granola, milk, and cooking-liquid amounts are unknown. Granola, nuts, dried fruit, and milk can make the calories move substantially while adding comparatively little protein.

This is not a reason to remove porridge. It is a reason to give the breakfast a defined protein anchor and measure the calorie-dense dry ingredients once.

## Default recomposition version

Use this as the standard version after recording the current original portions once:

- 60 g dry oats.
- 25 to 30 g granola, weighed rather than poured freely.
- One scoop of whey mixed in after cooking, or 200 to 250 g skyr/Greek yoghurt alongside it.
- Berries or another fruit for fibre and volume.
- Tea as normal.
- 5 g creatine monohydrate daily. Timing is not important; consistency is.

The objective is for breakfast to contribute roughly 30 to 40 g protein toward the daily 180 g target. Use the actual labels of the chosen oats, granola, dairy, and whey to log the exact total.

## Supplement review

### Creatine

- Label: 5 g serving, 100% creatine monohydrate.
- Practical use: one 5 g scoop daily is sufficient. A loading phase is optional, not required for this plan.
- Keep daily use consistent, including rest days.
- Track bodyweight using seven-day averages because early scale movement can include normal changes in body water.

### Electrolyte

Visible label values are per two tablets in 200 ml water: 16 kcal, 3.57 g carbohydrate, 0.69 g salt, 277 mg sodium, 193 mg potassium, and 3.27 g glucose.

- Useful around long, hot, or very sweaty runs/conditioning sessions.
- Not essential for an ordinary one-hour gym session if hydration and normal meals are adequate.
- Plain water is fine on routine days. Do not add electrolytes unnecessarily if following a medically advised low-sodium plan or if kidney/heart health requires specific advice.

### Multivitamin

Visible label instruction: one tablet daily; product is not a substitute for a varied, balanced diet.

- Fine as an insurance habit if tolerated, taken with food.
- It does not drive fat loss or muscle gain.
- Avoid stacking it with additional high-dose multivitamins or individual high-dose vitamins unless advised by a clinician.
