## What the photos show

### Strong starting assets

- There is a clear base of muscle through the upper arms, shoulders, and chest. The arms in particular are not starting from zero: the triceps and upper-arm shape are already visible from the side and flexed shots.
- The back and rear shoulders have a visible base. There is enough upper-body structure that fat loss should change the overall look quickly.
- This is a good recomposition starting point: the goal is to reveal existing shape while adding a modest amount of focused arm and upper-back stimulus.

### Main fat-loss opportunity

- The majority of visible softness is carried around the lower abdomen, waist, and hips.
- This cannot be spot-reduced. The visual route to a flatter waist is steady overall fat loss, daily movement, and consistent nutrition while preserving strength.
- From these photos alone, a deliberately broad visual estimate would place body fat in the low-to-mid twenties. It is not a diagnostic measurement and should not drive day-to-day decisions; waist and seven-day bodyweight averages will be more useful.

### Programming implications

- Keep the planned moderate deficit, not an aggressive cut. The existing arms, chest, and shoulders should look better quickly as the waist comes down.
- Retain the four-day strength plan with direct biceps and triceps work twice weekly, plus the optional arm pump only when recovered.
- Prioritise chest-supported rows, neutral-grip pulldowns, rear delts, lateral delts, and pain-free pressing. This supports the shoulder history and will improve the upper-body frame without forcing overhead work.
- Continue low-impact cardio as the default. Do not use extra running as the main fat-loss lever while shin splints are a recurring issue.

## What cannot be concluded from these photos

- A mirror photo cannot reliably assess body-fat percentage, posture, shoulder injury, strength, or meaningful left-right asymmetry.
- The back shot is a flexed/twisted position, so it is useful for a visual record but not for diagnosing imbalance.
- Future comparisons must use the same morning timing, distance, lighting, relaxed posture, and clothing to be meaningful.

## Four-week visual target

The realistic target is a visibly tighter waist and lower abdomen, a stronger arm outline, and better shoulder-to-waist contrast. With strong adherence, the scale may fall roughly 2 to 3 kg over the block, but the trend in waist, photos, and gym performance determines whether changes are needed.
