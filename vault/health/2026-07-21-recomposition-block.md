# Four-Week Recomposition Block

**Start date:** 21 July 2026  
**Owner:** Sam Donworth  
**Goal:** reduce waist and hip fat while maintaining or improving strength, visibly improving arm size, fitness, and overall leanness.

## Outcome for this block

This is a four-week recomposition block, not a crash diet. The score is consistent execution:

- Lose an average of **0.4 to 0.7 kg per week** after the first seven-day baseline.
- Keep strength stable or improve it in the main lifts.
- Complete four strength sessions most weeks.
- Build daily movement toward 9,000 to 10,000 steps.
- Keep shoulders calm and avoid flaring shin splints.

A useful outcome after four weeks is approximately 2 to 3 kg lower on the scale, a visibly tighter waist, and better arm/upper-body shape. Photos, waist trend, gym performance, energy, and sleep are more useful than scale weight alone.

## Baseline

- Age: 26
- Sex: male
- Height: 5 ft 10 in / 1.778 m
- Starting bodyweight: approximately 93 kg
- Estimated BMI: 29.4. This is a screening number only, not a body-composition diagnosis.
- Estimated resting energy requirement: approximately 1,916 kcal/day.
- Estimated maintenance range: approximately 2,800 to 3,150 kcal/day. This will be replaced by real intake and weight-trend data.
- Sleep: approximately seven hours per night.
- Steps: variable, normally below 10,000.
- Current cardio: 10 to 15 km running per week, plus incline treadmill walking or rowing.
- Training availability: 60-minute morning sessions three to four weekdays, plus one or two weekend sessions.
- Constraints: previous rugby-related shoulder issues; occasional shin splints.

## Non-negotiable safety rules

- No sharp pain, instability, numbness, or lingering joint pain. Stop that movement and replace it.
- Keep pressing pain-free. Neutral grips, machines, cables, and landmine presses are preferred to forcing barbell or overhead work.
- Do not run through shin pain. Replace a painful run with incline walking, bike, elliptical, or rowing.
- If shoulder symptoms are worsening, recurrent, or affect sleep or daily life, arrange a sports physio assessment rather than self-programming around an unknown injury.
- Do not turn this into an extreme calorie cut. Recovery, work performance, and training quality matter.

## Weekly training structure

Default week:

- Monday: Upper A
- Tuesday: Lower A
- Wednesday: 25 to 35 minutes low-impact zone-2 cardio or rest
- Thursday: Upper B
- Friday: rest, steps, and optional mobility
- Saturday: Lower B
- Sunday: optional 25 to 35 minutes low-impact zone-2 cardio, plus an arm pump only if recovered

If only three gym sessions happen, do the next session in sequence rather than skipping the lower body or arms permanently. The target is four strength sessions, not perfection.

### Warm-up for every lift

Allow 7 to 8 minutes.

- 3 to 4 minutes easy rower, incline walk, or bike.
- Two ramp-up sets before the first two big exercises.
- On upper days: two light rounds of 12 cable external rotations, 12 band pull-aparts, and 10 scapular wall slides only if completely pain-free.
- On lower days: 15 controlled tibialis raises, 12 calf raises, and 10 bodyweight squats.

### Upper A: chest, back, delts, arms

- Neutral-grip machine chest press or low-incline dumbbell press: 3 sets of 6 to 10.
- Chest-supported row: 3 sets of 8 to 12.
- Neutral-grip lat pulldown: 3 sets of 8 to 12.
- Cable or machine lateral raise: 3 sets of 12 to 20.
- Reverse pec deck or cable rear-delt fly: 2 sets of 12 to 20.
- EZ-bar curl or dumbbell curl: 3 sets of 8 to 12.
- Rope press-down: 3 sets of 10 to 15.

### Lower A: legs, posterior chain, trunk

- Leg press, hack squat, or safety-bar squat: 3 sets of 8 to 12.
- Romanian deadlift: 3 sets of 6 to 10.
- Seated or lying leg curl: 3 sets of 10 to 15.
- Split squat or controlled step-up: 2 sets of 8 to 12 each side.
- Standing calf raise: 3 sets of 10 to 15.
- Dead bug or Pallof press: 3 sets of 8 to 12 each side.

### Upper B: back, pain-free pressing, arms priority

- Assisted pull-up or neutral-grip pulldown: 3 sets of 6 to 10.
- One-arm cable row or supported dumbbell row: 3 sets of 8 to 12.
- Half-kneeling landmine press or another pain-free machine press: 3 sets of 8 to 12.
- Cable fly only if shoulder-friendly: 2 sets of 10 to 15.
- Cable lateral raise: 3 sets of 12 to 20.
- Incline dumbbell curl or cable curl: 3 sets of 10 to 15.
- Overhead rope extension only if shoulder-friendly, otherwise press-down: 3 sets of 10 to 15.

### Lower B: legs, glutes, athletic conditioning support

- Trap-bar deadlift, hip thrust, or another comfortable heavy hinge: 3 sets of 5 to 8.
- Hack squat or goblet squat: 3 sets of 8 to 12.
- Leg extension: 2 sets of 12 to 15.
- Leg curl: 2 sets of 10 to 15.
- Tibialis raise: 3 sets of 15 to 25.
- Seated calf raise: 3 sets of 12 to 20.
- Side plank: 3 sets of 30 to 45 seconds each side.

### Optional Sunday arm pump

Only add this when shoulder comfort, sleep, and energy are good.

- Cable curl: 2 sets of 12 to 15.
- Rope press-down: 2 sets of 12 to 15.
- Hammer curl: 2 sets of 12 to 15.
- Lateral raise: 2 sets of 15 to 20.

Stop with at least two good reps in reserve. This is extra stimulus, not a recovery debt.

## Progression and effort rules

- Start week one with approximately **three reps in reserve** on every working set.
- Week two: use the same exercises, add a rep where possible, or add one small load increment after reaching the top of the range.
- Week three: aim for one to two reps in reserve on final sets, without joint pain or ugly form.
- Week four: keep the same quality. If sleep, shins, or shoulders are poor, reduce each exercise by one set rather than forcing a hard week.
- When all prescribed sets reach the top of the rep range with clean technique, increase the load at the next session.
- Log load, reps, pain from 0 to 10, and energy from 1 to 5 for the first three exercises each day.

## Cardio and activity

- Start with two 25 to 35-minute zone-2 sessions weekly. Use incline walking, rowing, bike, or elliptical. Zone 2 means conversational but purposeful.
- Keep running to one or two easy sessions weekly only if shin pain stays at 0 to 2 out of 10 during and the next morning.
- Avoid adding sprints, hills, or sharp increases in weekly running volume during this block.
- Step target: average 7,000 per day in week one, 8,000 in week two, 9,000 in week three, and 9,000 to 10,000 in week four.

## Nutrition: starting targets

Initial targets will be checked against seven days of real intake and morning bodyweight.

- Start around **2,500 to 2,600 kcal/day**.
- Protein: **180 g/day** as the practical target.
- Fibre: 30 to 40 g/day.
- Water: 2.5 to 3.5 litres daily, more around training and running.
- Keep the electrolyte if it helps training, but it does not replace food or hydration.

### Practical meal changes using current foods

**Breakfast**

Porridge is a good base but needs a protein anchor. Add whey, Greek yoghurt/skyr, eggs, or another 35 to 45 g protein source. Add fruit for fibre.

**Lunch**

The chicken is useful. The calorie-dense parts are likely chorizo, pesto, coleslaw, pasta, and potatoes when combined casually. Keep the meal, but choose a primary carb portion and measure the calorie-dense additions for two weeks:

- chicken: aim for 180 to 220 g cooked
- chorizo: use it as flavour, not the protein base
- pesto: measure one portion
- potatoes or pasta: choose a deliberate portion rather than both automatically
- add bulky salad/vegetables before increasing sauces or oils

**Dinner**

Stir fries, bolognese, burgers, and roasts can all fit. Build each around 45 to 60 g protein, two fists of vegetables/salad, and one intentional carbohydrate serving. Make burgers at home with lean meat when possible and keep sauces/cheese deliberate rather than accidental.

**Alcohol**

Keep it sporadic. For this four-week block, cap any occasion at two drinks and avoid turning the following day into an untracked takeaway day.

## Daily minimum scorecard

- Morning bodyweight after toilet, before food or drink.
- Protein: 180 g.
- Calories: within the starting range until adjusted.
- Steps: hit the current weekly target.
- Training or cardio completed as planned.
- Sleep: aim for 7.5 hours, with seven hours as the hard floor.
- Note shoulder pain, shin pain, hunger, and energy if they are unusual.

## Weekly check-in: every Sunday morning

Record:

- Seven-day average bodyweight, not a single weigh-in.
- Waist measurement at the navel, relaxed, same conditions.
- Step average.
- Number of strength sessions, cardio sessions, and runs.
- Average sleep.
- Average calories and protein, once food logging begins.
- Best working sets for the main lifts.
- Shoulder and shin symptoms from 0 to 10.
- Front, side, and back photos every two weeks under the same lighting, distance, relaxed posture, and time of day. Face is optional.

## Adjustment rules

- If the seven-day weight average has not fallen for two consecutive weeks and food logging was accurate, reduce daily intake by 150 to 200 kcal or add 1,500 to 2,000 daily steps.
- If weight is falling faster than 0.9 kg/week, gym performance is falling, or hunger/recovery are poor, add 150 to 200 kcal.
- If waist is shrinking while weight holds steady and strength is improving, do not change anything.
- If shins worsen, replace running immediately with low-impact cardio and assess footwear, surfaces, and training load.
