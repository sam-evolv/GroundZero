# Ireland GPT - Product Strategy Session
Date: 6 August 2026

---

# Core Vision

Ireland GPT is not an AI chatbot.

It is a personal operating system that sits between a person and their digital life.

The goal is to make AI disappear.

The user should never think about:
- Models
- APIs
- Connectors
- Tokens
- Prompt engineering

They should simply ask for something to be done and trust that it happens.

The experience should feel closer to having an incredibly capable personal assistant than using software.

---

# Mission

Bring true AI agents to ordinary people.

Today:

- ChatGPT is largely used as an expensive Google search.
- Claude is still a tool.
- Gemini is still a tool.

Hermes proves what AI agents are capable of, but is far too technical for the average person.

Ireland GPT exists to package agentic AI into something anyone can use.

The goal is to remove every possible piece of friction.

---

# Product Philosophy

The benchmark is not ChatGPT.

The benchmark is:

- Apple
- Superhuman
- Linear
- Notion

Beautiful software.

Minimal friction.

Everything feels obvious.

---

# Biggest Insight

People do not care which model they're using.

Nobody outside the AI world knows or cares whether something is GPT-5.6, Luna, DeepSeek, Claude or Gemini.

They care about one thing.

> Did it get the job done?

Therefore:

The application should intelligently route every request to the cheapest model capable of producing the required result.

Users never see the routing.

They only experience the outcome.

---

# Pricing Philosophy

Most people cannot justify €20/month.

Not because AI isn't valuable.

Because they barely use it.

Instead:

- Low entry subscription (~€5)
- Medium tier (~€15)
- Higher professional tier

Use intelligent routing so marginal costs remain extremely low.

Capture the huge audience currently unwilling to subscribe to ChatGPT.

---

# Product Positioning

Do not market AI.

Potential positioning:

- Your digital assistant
- Your personal operating system
- Your everyday assistant
- Your second brain
- Your digital co-pilot

Possibly avoid "AI" entirely in the branding.

---

# The North Star

People shouldn't say:

> "I used Ireland GPT."

They should say:

> "I asked it."

or

> "My assistant sorted it."

---

# First-Time User Experience

The first few minutes are everything.

There needs to be an immediate "holy shit" moment.

The user must experience genuine delegation immediately.

Potential examples:

- Get an insurance quote
- Send emails
- Chase invoices
- Organise calendar
- Book appointments
- Gather files
- Create presentations
- Build videos
- Complete repetitive admin

The user should think:

> "I never want to do that myself again."

---

# Focus on Jobs People Hate

Research Ireland's most hated administrative jobs.

Examples:

- Car insurance
- Utility providers
- Revenue paperwork
- Phone providers
- Chasing invoices
- Booking appointments
- Emailing accountants
- Expense reports
- Scheduling meetings

Own these.

Do not try to do everything initially.

Become famous for eliminating life's annoying admin.

---

# Delegation > Intelligence

The product promise is not intelligence.

The promise is delegation.

People don't actually want AI.

They want responsibility removed from them.

The emotional outcome is:

"I don't need to think about this anymore."

---

# Frictionless Connectors

Connectors are essential.

However users should never browse integrations.

Instead:

User:

> "Help me organise my week."

Assistant:

> "To do that I'll need access to your calendar."

One tap.

Done.

Every connector should appear exactly when required.

Never before.

Capabilities first.

Technology second.

---

# Interface Direction

Not another ChatGPT clone.

Still conversational.

But differentiated.

Likely includes:

- Chat
- Voice
- Images
- Video
- Documents
- History
- Tasks
- Memory

Need significant design work to make it feel unlike ChatGPT or Claude.

---
# Messaging Interfaces

Huge opportunity.

Many people still don't naturally use AI apps.

Instead:

Allow users to communicate through:

- SMS
- iMessage
- WhatsApp
- Other messaging apps

Examples:

"Email Jim."

"Send me that PDF."

"Book Tuesday."

"Cancel my appointment."

Meet people where they already are.

---

# Personal Knowledge Layer

Potential moat.

Instead of just memory...

Create a living model of the user.

Think:

Digital identity.

Digital passport.

Life graph.

It understands:

- Projects
- Goals
- Family
- Work
- Preferences
- Relationships
- Documents
- Habits

Not merely storing information.

Understanding it.

---

# Dynamic User Understanding

Rather than a static profile.

Display things like:

## What I know

- Important people
- Projects
- Current goals
- Habits

## I'm unsure about

Areas with lower confidence.

Allow correction.

## Knowledge gaps

Suggest connectors that improve usefulness.

Example:

> Connect Calendar to unlock automatic planning.

---

# The Hidden Moat

Long-term value is not the model.

It is permission.

Every connected service increases switching costs.

Eventually:

The assistant knows everything.

Everything connects through it.

It becomes the data layer and the action layer.

---

# Learning User Playbooks

Teach the assistant how you work.

Examples:

"When travelling..."

Always choose:

- Cheapest direct flight
- Hotel under €180
- Walking distance to city centre

Examples:

"When writing LinkedIn posts..."

Always:

- Professional tone
- UK English
- Under 400 words

These become reusable playbooks.

---

# Specialist Agents

Potential future feature.

Instead of one assistant.

Have specialists sharing the same memory.

Examples:

- Finance
- Travel
- Health
- Career
- Business
- Home

Users never think about which one they're talking to.

The system routes internally.

---

# Household Subscription

Strong long-term opportunity.

Not simply "Family Plan."

Instead:

Household Operating System.

Shared knowledge:

- Bills
- Warranties
- Calendars
- School events
- Holidays
- Insurance
- Documents

Private memory remains private.

Shared memory is available where appropriate.

---

# Family Features

Potential features:

- Shared household calendar
- Shared documents
- Passport reminders
- Holiday planning
- Household admin
- School schedules
- Vehicle servicing
- Warranty reminders

The assistant becomes the family's organiser.

---

# Child Safety

Potential future feature.

Rather than complete surveillance.

Provide:

Age-based accounts.

Transparent safety systems.

Notify parents of significant safety concerns rather than exposing every conversation.

Must balance:

- Privacy
- Trust
- Safety

---

# Founder Marketing Strategy

One of the strongest ideas discussed.

Run the company using the product.

The company itself becomes proof.

Examples:

"This marketing video was created entirely by Ireland GPT."

"This presentation was produced autonomously."

"This business is being built with the same assistant we're selling."

Rather than telling people it's powerful.

Show them.

Every day.

---

# Content Strategy

Document everything.

Build in public.

Show:

- Videos
- Automations
- Time saved
- Marketing assets
- Business workflows

Every piece of content demonstrates the product.

Marketing becomes product demonstration.

---

# The Hermes Insight

Hermes demonstrated what is possible.

Example:

Creating a YC walkthrough video:

Previously:

- Screen recording
- Editing
- Music
- Titles
- Export

Estimated:

3-4 hours.

Hermes:

~15 minutes.

Autonomous.

Music generated automatically.

Finished without intervention.

This was the "holy shit" moment.

The goal is to make experiences like this available to everyone.

---

# Core Product Principles

- Hide complexity.
- Hide models.
- Hide routing.
- Hide connectors until needed.
- Reduce clicks.
- Reduce thinking.
- Build trust.
- Deliver outcomes.
- Earn automation gradually.

---

# Final Thought

The company is not really selling AI.

It is selling time.
Every feature should answer one question:

> "Does this remove something people hate doing?"

If the answer is yes,

build it.

If not,

don't.
