# Ireland GPT Product, Pricing, GTM and Delegation Strategy

## Provenance note

Sam supplied this ChatGPT strategy output on 6 August 2026. This is a structured capture of the supplied content rather than a claim that ChatGPT's factual assertions are verified. One optional-personalisation passage was malformed in the pasted source and the final product-doctrine passage repeated earlier text. The intended strategic points remain legible and are preserved below without treating the duplicated tail as new instruction.

## Core thesis

IrelandGPT should not be another chatbot. It should package the capability emerging in agent frameworks such as Hermes into a consumer product ordinary people can understand, trust and use.

> Complex under the hood. Calm on the surface.

Users should not need to understand models, tokens, APIs, connectors, prompt engineering or workflows. They should say what they want done and the product should work out the rest.

The long-term product is a personal execution layer between a person and their digital world.

North star:

> Give people back an hour of their life every day.

The product sells time, relief, delegation, reduced mental load and execution, not AI. A Time Given Back metric could show tasks completed and estimated hours saved this week, this month and since joining.

## Delegation category

Emotional promise:

> Hand it over. It gets handled.

The assistant compounds across:

1. Memory: understands the person, preferences, relationships, projects, documents and routines.
2. Action: acts across external services and applications.
3. Judgment: knows when to act and when to ask.
4. Initiative: eventually identifies useful actions before being explicitly asked.

The key metric is how much responsibility a user feels comfortable handing over.

## Trust ladder

1. Suggest.
2. Draft.
3. Prepare and request one-tap approval.
4. Limited automatic execution for trusted categories.
5. Autonomous execution within agreed boundaries.

Every release should let the user delegate one more thing without increasing complexity.

## Hermes as the engine

Do not rebuild agent infrastructure from scratch. Use Hermes and its ecosystem for underlying capability while the business focuses on:

- UX and UI;
- branding;
- trust and permissions;
- integrations;
- memory;
- model routing;
- reliability;
- distribution;
- marketing.

The company owns the consumer delegation layer and end-user relationship. This is more than a cosmetic wrapper.

## Capability without feature creep

Capability is not feature creep; interface complexity is. The assistant may perform hundreds of jobs while the ordinary experience remains:

> Ask → Approve if necessary → Done

The product should learn the user. The user should not have to learn the product.

## From tasks to orchestration

Administrative work is an initial wedge, but orchestration is larger. A request such as “Give us something good to do Saturday night” can eventually coordinate preferences, calendars, weather, restaurant and activity discovery, travel time, bookings, transport and calendar updates.

The user expresses the outcome. The assistant handles the workflow.

The same intelligence can eventually coordinate:

- personal administration, appointments, insurance, travel, bills, renewals, shopping and household work;
- work email, presentations, documents, research, meetings, marketing, reporting and CRM/admin;
- future home energy, heating, security, devices and routines.

## Voice and messaging

Conversational voice is an early priority. Voice, app chat, messaging and desktop should be interfaces into the same assistant identity and context.

Outbound phone calls are later. A future proposition is:

> Never wait on hold again.

Calls must identify the assistant appropriately, remain within delegated authority and escalate when necessary.

Messaging can support mainstream adoption through policy-compliant WhatsApp and other viable channels.

> Meet users where they already are.

## Capability discovery

Ordinary users face an imagination problem: an agent's capabilities are hidden behind an input box.

Build Explore or Inspiration around real jobs rather than prompts:

- organise my week;
- chase an invoice;
- prepare a presentation;
- compare insurance options;
- organise receipts for my accountant;
- plan a Saturday night;
- research a purchase;
- create a marketing video;
- summarise a document;
- draft an email;
- organise a trip.

A “Try this” action should turn inspiration directly into delegation. Discovery should also occur contextually after successful tasks.

## Onboarding and context

Minimise forms, setup, mandatory questions and early permissions. Support Apple and Google account creation where possible.

> Onboarding should be a 90-day conversation, not a 10-minute form.

Optional personalisation questions include:

- What would you love never to have to do again?
- Where would you like the most help?
- Is this mainly for work, home or both?
- How proactive should I be?
- Should I suggest, draft or act when possible?

Every question must justify its friction.

Build understanding through:

- free context naturally provided in conversation;
- earned context learned through repeated use;
- connected context from authorised calendar, email, documents and services.

A pre-signup sandbox should let the user experience useful work from information they voluntarily provide, then ask whether they want IrelandGPT to complete it. Registration should feel like continuation, not administration.

## Activation

Do not optimise primarily for registration. Optimise for:

> Time to First Delegation

Target the first real successfully handed-over task within five minutes, with the first strong proof moment as early as possible.

## Pricing and cost hypotheses

Proposed entry tier: €9.99 per month, meaningfully below common €20+ AI subscriptions while retaining genuine agentic utility.

Potential entry capabilities:

- everyday assistance;
- research;
- email-related tasks;
- scheduling;
- basic document work;
- bounded agentic administration;
- everyday personal assistance.

Working KPI: average direct AI cost near €2.50 per active €9.99 subscriber per month or less. Intelligent routing, caching, efficient context and limits for expensive work act as cost governors. Users should not see model routing.

Wider costs include Vercel, Supabase, model/API usage, authentication, storage, notifications, observability, payments, messaging, voice, customer acquisition, compliance, operations and human escalation.

AI-native support should handle most routine support, with a human route for billing disputes, security, account recovery and serious edge cases.

## Irish positioning

Irish identity is an initial wedge, expressed through terminology, services, administrative processes, cultural context and restrained humour.

> Competence first. Personality second.

The assistant should feel naturally Irish rather than themed or clichéd. Reliable capability is mandatory; occasional genuine humour may create organic sharing.

## Screenshotability and product-led proof

Design for moments where someone thinks:

> Jesus, look what this thing just did.

Shareable moments come from surprising execution, meaningful time saved, contextual understanding and occasional genuinely funny responses.

The product should eventually create its own marketing loop:

1. identify a relatable problem;
2. plan a solution;
3. execute the task;
4. record the execution;
5. edit the recording;
6. create copy and artwork;
7. produce the social asset;
8. publish after founder approval;
9. analyse performance;
10. improve the next piece.

Demonstrations should show “We asked it to do this annoying job. Here is what happened,” not abstract agentic claims.

## Founder-led distribution and first 100

Early distribution uses Sam's network, LinkedIn, direct outreach, WhatsApp groups, testers and professional contacts.

Core invitation:

> Give me one job you hate doing. Let me see if this can do it for you.

Target the first 100 enthusiastic users rather than mass scale. Use them for product evidence, real use cases, testimonials, referrals, word of mouth and proof of product pull.

Reliability matters more than externally claimed breadth. This does not reverse the internal full-Hermes parity requirement; it narrows launch promises and proof to workflows that actually pass.

## Referrals and partnerships

Time Given Back may support a promise-aligned referral:

> Your assistant gave you 11 hours back this month. Give someone else some time back.

Potential later trusted distribution partners include credit unions, universities, employers, membership organisations, professional associations and selected service providers. The strategic benefit is borrowed trust, not distribution alone.

## Competitive position and timing

Do not compete with ChatGPT, Claude or other foundation-model companies on raw intelligence. Compete on:

- UX;
- localisation;
- trust;
- consumer focus;
- integrations;
- persistent context;
- pricing;
- distribution;
- agentic execution;
- speed;
- opinionated product design.

The opportunity is the transition from asking AI questions to handing AI responsibility for outcomes. The giants will add agentic functionality. The defence is moving faster on a trusted, localised, low-friction personal assistant for ordinary people that reliably completes work.

## Product doctrine

- Ask for outcomes, not workflows.
- Competence first. Personality second.
- Every release should let the user delegate one more thing without increasing complexity.
- Capability is not feature creep; interface complexity is.
- Complex under the hood. Calm on the surface.
