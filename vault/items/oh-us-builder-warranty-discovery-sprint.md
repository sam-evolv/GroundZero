# Validate the US regional-builder warranty and homeowner-record wedge

## Thesis

US regional homebuilders have a recurring post-close workflow across homeowner handoff, warranty intake, evidence collection, trade coordination and quality learning. OpenHouse may fit as the exact-home record and homeowner-guidance layer that coexists with the builder's warranty or ERP system.

The discovery lane must test this before any US localisation or product build.

## Initial offer

A bounded community or phase pilot:

- 20 to 50 recently completed or soon-to-close homes;
- one approved document and home-data source;
- exact-home record for documents, systems, warranties and history;
- homeowner guidance grounded in approved evidence;
- structured capture of repeated questions and missing information;
- escalation into the builder's current warranty or customer-care process;
- named sponsor, metrics and decision date;
- no requirement to replace the incumbent ERP or warranty system.

## Buyer

Prioritise:

- VP or Director of Warranty;
- VP or Director of Customer Experience;
- Quality or Homeowner Experience leader;
- Operations leader at a regional builder where these functions are combined.

Avoid broad innovation outreach that is detached from the operating workflow.

## Validation sequence

1. Research 20 regional builders with active communities, visible homeowner resources and credible operating ownership.
2. Identify one primary and one secondary contact per account.
3. Run targeted direct outreach and channel-led introduction requests in parallel.
4. Complete five qualified workflow conversations before any US-specific build.
5. Ask each qualified buyer for a paid pilot, data-backed design partnership or introduction to the budget owner.
6. Record incumbent systems, right-to-repair or warranty constraints, evidence requirements, procurement friction and willingness to pay.

## Promotion gate

Promote from discovery into implementation only if:

- at least three buyers confirm the same costly handoff, evidence or warranty-intake gap;
- one named builder offers payment, usable data or a signed commitment;
- OpenHouse can coexist with the incumbent workflow;
- the pilot does not require unsupported legal claims, automated coverage decisions or a separate US codebase;
- the contract value justifies support, security and data-processing requirements.

## Risks

- Established warranty, ERP and customer-care vendors may already solve the minimum need.
- State warranty and right-to-repair requirements vary.
- US buyers may expect SOC 2, US data residency, SSO, SMS and integrations before a live pilot.
- A remote Irish founder may struggle to create trust without a channel or reference.
- The first wedge must be a complementary exact-home and homeowner-guidance layer, not a claim to replace the builder's system of record.

## Stop rules

- No US-specific feature build before five conversations or a paid commitment.
- No generic mass email to public-builder executive teams.
- No claims about warranty coverage, legal compliance, cost reduction or dispute outcomes without evidence.
- No free live pilot.
