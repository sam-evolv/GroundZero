# Autonomous business launch loop

## Summary
Build a product that does not just brainstorm a business. It uses Ground Zero as the memory layer, then runs a loop:

1. understand the founder
2. find a wedge with real unfair advantage
3. shape the offer and buyer
4. generate the first site and outreach
5. collect signal
6. improve the business from evidence
7. keep updating the operating context

The point is to create real value fast, with approval gates around customer-facing actions.

## What makes this better than Henry

Henry's pitch is broad: read context, pick a challenge, launch a site, draft marketing, keep improving.

The Ground Zero version is better because it adds:

- a durable shared context layer
- live company/project state
- decision history
- structured capture of feedback and objections
- approval-gated execution
- a stronger feedback loop tied to the vault

## Core principle

Do not build a generic business brainstorm tool.
Build a *founder operating system for launching and improving one real wedge at a time*.

## Workflow

### 1. Founder understanding
Collect:
- website
- social profiles
- past work
- audience
- skills
- constraints
- goals

Then summarize:
- what the founder is unusually well placed to do
- what they should not do
- what kind of customers they can credibly serve

### 2. Wedge selection
Find a specific challenge with one or more of:
- existing domain knowledge
- existing distribution
- urgent pain
- clear budget
- short sales cycle
- document-heavy or repetitive workflow

The wedge should be narrow enough to ship, but valuable enough to sell.

### 3. Offer shaping
Define:
- buyer
- pain
- promise
- proof
- first validation test
- path to revenue
- pricing hypothesis

The product should prefer a painful problem over a clever idea.

### 4. Launch assets
Generate:
- first customer-facing page
- CTA
- lead form
- outreach draft
- follow-up sequence

The goal is not polish. The goal is contact with the market.

### 5. Signal capture
Track:
- leads
- replies
- qualified interest
- objections
- ignored messages
- conversions
- revenue

Store the signal back into Ground Zero.

### 6. Iteration loop
Use the signal to update:
- positioning
- page copy
- pricing
- qualification
- lead targeting
- next campaign
- bottleneck analysis

## Approval gates

Autonomy should be strongest where it is low risk:

- research
- synthesis
- drafts
- recommendations
- page generation
- follow-up suggestions

Approval required for:

- publishing
- sending messages
- spending money
- customer-facing commitments

## The first viable MVP

The smallest version that still creates value:

1. ingest founder context
2. score 3-5 possible wedges
3. pick the best wedge with a reason
4. draft the offer and landing page
5. draft one outreach sequence
6. write the plan into the vault
7. surface the next highest-leverage action

## Best first wedges for Sam

Given the current context, the strongest first bets are likely:

- OpenBook: local SMB booking and no-show reduction
- OpenHouse: developer handover, aftercare, and evidence workflows
- consultancy-style wedge: document-heavy operational pain with short sales cycles

## Metrics that matter

- time from intake to launchable page
- time from launch to first reply
- reply rate
- qualified interest rate
- conversion to call or pilot
- revenue created
- quality of the learned wedge

## Non-goals

- not a generic chatbot
- not a vibes-based idea generator
- not a full company replacement
- not a design tool first
- not an automation that acts without guardrails

## Open questions

- Should the product be optimized for new business creation or for improving an existing business?
- Should it focus first on one industry wedge or stay general?
- Should the output be a brief, a page, or a full launch kit?

## Recommendation

For Sam, the best version is likely a **founder launch loop on top of Ground Zero**:

- Ground Zero provides memory and state
- the system finds a wedge
- it drafts the business assets
- it collects evidence
- it updates the plan
- it keeps moving until there is real market signal

That makes it useful for OpenBook, OpenHouse, and future ventures without losing the user's operating context.
