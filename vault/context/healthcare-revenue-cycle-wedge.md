# Healthcare revenue-cycle wedge

## Sector
Hospital and health-system revenue-cycle operations in the US/UK-style healthcare admin market.

## The bottleneck
Claims adjudication, denial management, prior authorisation, claim-status chasing, and appeals.

This work is expensive because it is:
- repetitive
- document-heavy
- rules-heavy
- full of payer-specific edge cases
- spread across people, portals, faxes, emails, PDFs, and EHRs

## Why this is a strong wedge
The pain is directly tied to cash collection. If the workflow is improved, the buyer can measure the value immediately in:
- fewer denials
- faster reimbursement
- fewer appeals
- less staff time spent on admin
- lower write-offs

## Evidence of scale
- Premier reported that claims adjudication cost healthcare providers **more than $25.7 billion in 2023**.
- Premier also said **70% of denials were ultimately overturned**, but only after multiple rounds of review.
- The AHA reported hospitals and health systems spent **$19.7 billion in 2022** trying to overturn denied claims.
- The AHA also reported **nearly 15%** of claims to private payers are initially denied, and **54.3%** of denied claims are ultimately overturned after appeals.
- CAQH reported that automating prior authorisation workflows could save **$515 million annually** and **14 minutes per authorisation**.
- CAQH also said claim-status automation could save staff **up to 18 minutes per patient visit**.

## Product concept
**Revenue Cycle Copilot**

An AI workflow product that:
- predicts likely denials before submission
- checks missing documentation against payer rules
- assembles claim packets automatically
- drafts appeal letters from chart evidence
- tracks claim status and escalates exceptions
- logs every action for auditability

## Consultancy offer
Start as a high-touch implementation service:
1. map the client’s denial and prior-auth failure points
2. connect to claims / EHR / document systems
3. configure payer-specific rules
4. deploy denial-prevention workflows
5. measure savings in cash collected, denial rate, and staff time

Then productize into software once the workflow repeats.

## Implementation shape
- phase 1: denial classification and appeal drafting
- phase 2: prior auth packet assembly
- phase 3: claim-status automation and follow-up queues
- phase 4: payer rule memory and continuous learning
- phase 5: performance dashboard tied to recovered revenue

## Why AI fits
This is a narrow, repetitive, language-heavy problem with structured outputs.
AI is good at:
- extracting evidence from charts
- matching it to payer requirements
- drafting correspondence
- classifying denial reasons
- automating follow-up

## Plain-English pitch
"We help hospitals get paid faster by stopping avoidable denials and automatically building the paperwork needed to win appeals."
